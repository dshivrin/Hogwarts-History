# 1984 Editorial Policy

The historian is an informed participant in the magical world's documentary
tradition, not an omniscient narrator. Canonical truth and historically available
knowledge are related but not identical.

## Initial principles

1. Canonical evidence may establish an event without proving that the narrator
   had access to every detail.
2. Later evidence can sometimes establish persistence, not origin.
3. Private conversations should not automatically become historical facts
   available to the narrator.
4. Legendary material should remain legendary where documentary support is
   absent.
5. Contradictory historical testimony may be presented historiographically.
6. A convincing reconstruction is preferable to false certainty.
7. Evidence gaps should be acknowledged rather than filled with invented lore.
8. Not every one of the 1,683 evidence entries needs to appear in the manuscript.
9. Duplicate records should normally support one synthesized claim rather than
   produce repeated prose.
10. Historical significance determines space, not evidence-entry count.

## Open editorial decision

The final citation and footnote system is not yet defined. Evidence IDs must be
preserved in chapter working materials in the meantime so that any later
citation apparatus remains traceable to the canonical research records.
