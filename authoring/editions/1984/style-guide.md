# 1984 Edition Style Guide

Status: INITIAL / DRAFT-01 CALIBRATION

This guide is provisional. Chapters 1–3 will be used to calibrate the voice and
may produce revisions before later chapters are drafted.

## Intended voice

- Write serious historical nonfiction from within the magical world.
- Address readers who already understand ordinary magical life; the book is not
  written for Muggles.
- Use moderately formal British prose that remains clear and enjoyable.
- Allow restrained dry humour where it suits the historian's perspective.
- Prefer historical causation, development, conflict, and consequence to lists
  of facts.
- Use anecdotes when they illuminate institutional history.
- Distinguish evidence, tradition, and speculation.

## Avoid

- fan-wiki or lore-encyclopedia presentation;
- the voice and apparatus of a modern academic journal;
- fake medieval or archaic English;
- modern fandom vocabulary;
- excessive dramatic prose;
- explanations of basic magical concepts included merely for an outside reader.

## Confidence language

Useful formulations include:

```text
The surviving records show...
Contemporary accounts suggest...
School tradition maintains...
Later historians have argued...
No surviving evidence establishes...
The precise date is uncertain...
It appears likely that...
```

These are guidance for signalling epistemic limits, not a phrase template to be
repeated mechanically. Vary the prose while preserving the distinction between
documented fact, interpretation, tradition, and uncertainty.
