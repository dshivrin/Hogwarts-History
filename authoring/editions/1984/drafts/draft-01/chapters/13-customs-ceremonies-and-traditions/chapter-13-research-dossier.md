# Chapter 13 Research Dossier

Prepared: 23 September 2026  
Scope: research and proposed outline only; no chapter prose  
Central question: What did Hogwarts repeatedly do, why did those practices
matter, and how did they become part of the school's identity?

## Executive finding

The evidence base is strong enough to outline and, after editorial review of
this dossier and approval of the outline, to draft a focused chapter. It is not
strong enough to write an origins history of most customs. The firmest account
is instead an institutional one: later evidence repeatedly shows Hogwarts
bringing pupils into the school through differentiated arrival and Sorting,
gathering them in the Great Hall at calendrical turning points, making House
membership visible through points and awards, and adapting familiar forms when
danger, bereavement or exceptional inter-school events intervened.

Only two selected threads provide documented historical formation rather than
later observation. The Hogwarts Express was imposed as a standardised route
after secrecy made family-by-family travel untenable, although its precise
acquisition and adoption date remain uncertain. During Armando Dippet's
headship, an attempted Christmas pantomime failed spectacularly and produced a
lasting prohibition. The latter is the chapter's best narrative episode because
it shows a custom being proposed, attempted, destroyed and replaced by a ban.

## D. Candidate historical episodes

### 1. The first-year threshold

The strongest opening scene is the sequence from Hogsmeade station to the
Great Hall: returning pupils separate toward carriages, first-years follow
Hagrid to the lake, see the castle from the water, wait apart, enter in line,
and receive a public House place. The episode should be narrated only to the
extent supported by `ps-ch06-006`, `ps-ch06-007`, `poa-ch05-005`,
`gof-ch11-006`, `ps-ch07-002` and `gof-ch12-003`. The visual effect is secure;
an intentional founder-designed initiation is not.

### 2. Halloween repeated, but not fixed

The three consecutive feasts provide a compact demonstration of recurrence
without ancient origin. Bats and pumpkins recur; entertainment does not have a
fixed programme. The troll, Nick's deathday party and the Fat Lady attack are
events around Halloween, not Halloween customs. This episode is useful mainly
as a methodological contrast between an annual institutional occasion and the
plot events that interrupt it.

### 3. Christmas and the flexible Hall

Christmas supplies the best evidence for repetition plus adaptation: residence
lists recur, twelve trees become “usual,” and staff or pupils are seen doing
seasonal work. When few remain, a single shared table replaces the normal House
arrangement. The chapter should use this as evidence that continuity did not
require identical staging.

### 4. The *Fountain of Fair Fortune* disaster

This is the principal historical episode. Beery attempted to introduce a new
Yuletide entertainment; Dumbledore supplied stage magic; Kettleburn supplied an
enlarged Ashwinder; fire and a student duel forced evacuation; the hospital
wing filled; Dippet banned future pantomimes; the prohibition persisted. The
episode is funny because the evidence is specific, and historically valuable
because cause and institutional consequence are both documented. It must not
be assigned an exact year or treated as a recurring pantomime tradition.

### 5. Exceptional ceremony entering ordinary space

The 1994 tournament is useful only in a compressed sequence: shortened lessons
and formal welcome, public champion selection, then a Yule Ball that replaces
House tables with a procession and inter-school seating. Its value here is to
show Hogwarts using its ordinary ceremonial centre for a nonannual event. The
tournament's history and crisis belong to Chapter 16.

### 6. Mourning changes the ending

Cedric's black-draped Leaving Feast is the strongest direct comparison between
normal custom and crisis adaptation. Dumbledore's later funeral is a separate,
schoolwide memorial that reorganised Houses, grounds and departure timing, but
does not establish a regular funeral tradition. One concise later editorial
section can use both to show that institutional gathering survived even when
celebration did not.

## E. Claims that must not be made

- First-years have crossed the lake since the founders.
- The lake approach was designed as an initiation ceremony.
- Hagrid's role is ancient or attached permanently to the groundskeeper's job.
- The Sorting Ceremony has remained unchanged for a thousand years.

## F. Proposed narrative sequence

The evidence supports a slightly revised version of the requested shape:

1. **From memory to repetition.** Begin exactly at Chapter 12's final sentence.
   Shift from what the Hat preserves to what the assembled school does around
   it.
2. **Crossing the threshold.** Journey, separation at Hogsmeade, lake view and
   waiting chamber. Treat the route as later-attested tradition without an
   origin story.
3. **A child becomes a member.** Procession, Hat song, public name-calling,
   House-table reception, then feast. Do not reopen the Hat's mind or founder
   judgement.
4. **The Hall sets the year in motion.** Feast and usual notices show the whole
   school made present to itself; variable rules demonstrate continuity through
   changing content.
5. **The calendar gathers the school.** Halloween, Christmas and Easter in
   descending order of evidentiary strength. Christmas becomes the main example
   of recurring form adapting to attendance and circumstance.
6. **Competition made visible.** Points accumulate in public; the ending feast
   converts totals into banners, colours and a Cup. Keep Quidditch to a short
   bridge about informal House performance and reserve the sport for Chapter 14.
7. **How a tradition fails to begin.** Give the Dippet pantomime room to unfold;
   end the episode with the ban as the successful institutional residue.
8. **Ceremony under exceptional pressure.** Compress Triwizard welcome,
   selection and Yule Ball; then contrast celebration with black-draped mourning
   and the later lakeside funeral.
9. **What repetition accomplishes.** Return to September: repeated forms place
    each cohort in communities older than itself while still allowing the school
    to alter the performance. The exit should narrow toward Chapter 14 by noting
    that one form of House competition developed its own season, crowds, songs
    and institutional history.

Songs do not receive a freestanding section in the detailed outline. The Hat's
annual performance belongs with Sorting; the newly indexed school song belongs
immediately after the opening notices; and later House chants belong
principally to Chapter 14.

## Recommendation

Proceed to drafting only after human review and approval of the proposed
outline. The evidence base is strong for a chapter about institutional rhythm,
social incorporation and adaptive continuity. It is weak for origins, so the
draft must resist “for a thousand years” language except for the separately
settled annual-song premise. The school-song gap is omittable rather than
blocking: the chapter can proceed without it, but should not claim a stable
Hogwarts musical canon until that passage is indexed.
