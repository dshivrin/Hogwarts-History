# Chapter 13 Evidence Gaps

## Missing chronology

- The corpus does not date the origin of the first-year lake crossing, the
  mature Sorting procedure, Halloween observance, Christmas observance, the
  point system, the House Cup or the end-of-year feast.
- The Hogwarts Express account links the need for coordinated travel to 1692
  but does not give the exact date on which the train became compulsory.
- The *Fountain of Fair Fortune* pantomime occurred while Armando Dippet was
  headmaster and Dumbledore was a young Transfiguration teacher, but its school
  year is not supplied.

## Knowledge-access gaps

- Most procedural evidence is direct Harry-era observation. It supports the
  unified edition and sometimes recurrence in that period, but not unmarked
  reconstructed-Bagshot description.
- The historical Hogwarts Express account is a 2015 official retrospective.
  Its pre-1984 events are later discoveries about older conditions unless an
  earlier accessible carrier is found.
- Dumbledore's pantomime commentary describes a pre-1984 event, but the present
  carrier is later. The outline must attribute the recollection rather than
  place its exact details silently in Bagshot's voice.

## Contradictory or limiting evidence

- No direct contradiction was found among the selected records.
- The 1991 departure used boats for pupils returning to the train, while the
  corpus does not establish this as a universal annual departure custom.
- Christmas evidence shows both four-House dining and a single shared table for
  a small holiday group. These are adaptations to attendance, not competing
  universal rules.

## Unresolved open questions

- When did the mature Sorting ceremony begin, and how much of its current form
  predates the observations of 1991-96?
- Who established the lake route and why was it reserved for first-years?
- Does *Hogwarts: A History* itself cover Sorting custom beyond the explicit
  Great Hall ceiling reference?
- When did Halloween, Christmas, the House Cup and the leaving feast begin?
- Who ordinarily authorises seasonal entertainment and decoration?
- What were ordinary limits on point awards and deductions?
- Did the school song recur, and was its melody or performance method ever
  stable? `ps-ch07-003` establishes the 1991 performance only.
- Which school year contained the Dippet pantomime?
- Was any graduation ceremony regularly practised? No qualifying evidence was
  found.

## Unsupported assumptions

- Founder-era boat crossing, Sorting procedure, feasts or House Cup.
- Uninterrupted thousand-year continuity for any ceremony except the Draft-01
  style lock's settled premise of the Hat's annual songs.
- Hagrid's first-year role as an ancient office rather than a later personnel
  arrangement.
- Halloween ghost entertainment as an annual fixture.
- Easter as a Hogwarts-specific religious or magical ceremony.
- Lockhart's Valentine's event as anything other than a one-off intervention.
- The Yule Ball as an annual Hogwarts custom.
- Dumbledore's funeral as proof of an established headmaster-funeral protocol.

## Source-authority concerns

- `ext-b10-003` is a preservation transcript of retrospective authorial
  speculation and expressly describes an unrealised graduation idea; it cannot
  establish a custom.
- `ootp-ch11-004` is resident-ghost testimony about earlier warning songs but
  supplies no dates or examples.
- `ootp-ch38-002` partly relies on a pupil's interpretation of Flitwick's motive
  for preserving a swamp remnant; it is evidence of a memorialised incident,
  not of a repeated tradition.

## Research-layer issues resolved

- The previously missing *Philosopher's Stone*, Chapter Seven school-song
  passage is now indexed as `ps-ch07-003`. It establishes one observed 1991
  performance after the start-of-term notices: displayed common words,
  individually chosen tunes and collective singing. It does not establish
  recurrence, antiquity or an invariant performance method.
- `ps-ch07-002` covers the pre-Sorting briefing but not all details of the
  procession, alphabetical order, House-table reception or school song. Later
  `gof-ch12-003` safely supports the scroll, stool, name-calling and House-table
  procedure, but the 1991-only details should not be overextended.
