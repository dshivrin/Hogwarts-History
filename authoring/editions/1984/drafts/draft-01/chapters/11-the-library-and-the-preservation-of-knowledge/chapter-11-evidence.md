# Chapter 11 Evidence Retrieval — The Library and the Written Store

## Scope and method

This dossier records Stage 1 retrieval only. It is not an outline and does not
advance Chapter 11 beyond `planned`. Candidates were found through the indexed
query interface, the generated list of explicit *Hogwarts: A History*
references, and narrow searches of canonical source YAML. The research layer
was read only.

The editor's working title, *The Library and the Written Store*, is retained
here. Project control presently calls Chapter 11 *The Library and the
Preservation of Knowledge*.

Temporal labels follow the chapter brief:

- **A — pre-1984 direct:** an event, object or practice securely attested
  before 1984.
- **B — later evidence of older material:** a later source establishes an
  older work, record, event or condition; only the older fact may be carried
  backwards.
- **C — later ordinary Hogwarts:** a 1990s or later observation useful for a
  labelled reconstruction of later library life.
- **D — exceptional later conditions:** censorship, war or emergency control
  that cannot represent ordinary Hogwarts.

“Confidence” below reports confidence in the stated factual proposition, not
permission to backdate it.

## Retrieval assessment

The evidence supports a bounded institutional argument. Hogwarts required
books as part of formal education, maintained a later-attested library used
for study and independent research, preserved older and specialised material,
and placed some works behind permissions or removed them from pupil access.
It also kept administrative records and, in later evidence, an archive outside
the ordinary pupil library. The strongest procedural detail is later than
1984. The evidence therefore supports “preservation with regulated access,”
but not an unbroken ancient library system, a complete Restricted Section
code, or a timeless portrait of Irma Pince's practice.
