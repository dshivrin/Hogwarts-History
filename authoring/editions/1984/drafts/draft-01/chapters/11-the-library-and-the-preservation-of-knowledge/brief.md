## Chapter purpose

Explain how Hogwarts accumulated, organised, protected, restricted and
transmitted written knowledge, and how pupils used that knowledge for required
work and independent inquiry. The library is the physical centre, but the
chapter distinguishes course books, library holdings, private books,
departmental copies, administrative records and the later Hogwarts Archives.

## Central historical question

How did written knowledge extend education beyond direct teaching while
remaining subject to institutional control?

## Working argument

Hogwarts did not rely solely on knowledge passing from professor to pupil. Its
books and records allowed information to survive individual teachers,
curricula and pupil generations. Later evidence shows pupils using that store
for coursework, revision and self-directed research, sometimes finding that
the needed fact was present but difficult to locate. Access was not unlimited:
teachers authorised some restricted consultation, the librarian mediated
access, and headmasters or governors could influence what remained available.
The evidence therefore supports a bounded tension between preservation and
regulated access, not a claim that Hogwarts possessed or freely offered all
magical knowledge.

## Entry and exit

- **Entry:** take up Chapter 10's closing distinction between knowledge passed
  pupil to pupil and knowledge retained in a written store.
- **Exit:** show that written sources were only one form of institutional
  memory, preparing Chapter 12's portraits, ghosts and other resident voices
  without beginning their history.

## Required boundaries

- Keep *Hogwarts: A History* important but controlled; do not infer its author,
  publication history or the provenance of Hermione's private copy.
- Keep library, classroom books, private collections, disciplinary records and
  archives distinct.
- Separate the age of a work, edition, physical copy and Hogwarts custody.
- Treat Pince's later severity as personal evidence unless a rule is separately
  attested.
- Treat the Malfoy–Beedle dispute as post-1984 and Umbridge's information
  controls as exceptional.
