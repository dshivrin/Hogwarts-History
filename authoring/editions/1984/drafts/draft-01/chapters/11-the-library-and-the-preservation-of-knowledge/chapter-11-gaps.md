# Chapter 11 Gap Analysis — The Library and the Written Store

## Status and scope

This is the Stage 2 gap analysis derived from `chapter-11-evidence.md`. It does
not resolve gaps from memory, infer a complete policy from isolated scenes, or
advance the chapter to outline or prose. Questions below distinguish material
that is simply unknown from material that may merit bounded source inspection
in a later evidence-review stage.

## Pre-1984 foundation

- The dataset supplies no direct pre-1984 description of the library's room,
  staff, opening hours, arrangement, borrowing procedure or Restricted Section
  rules.
- Pre-1984 anchors concern particular books, Riddle-era research, old school
  records and the recognised place of at least one textbook. They do not amount
  to a full 1984 operating picture.
- No evidence dates the establishment of the library, the Restricted Section,
  the librarian's office or any catalogue.
- No evidence establishes continuous operation of the same library collection
  from Hogwarts's foundation to 1984.

**Consequence:** the reconstructed Bagshot layer may discuss the institutional
value of written knowledge and securely older examples, but detailed everyday
library operation must remain a labelled later reconstruction.

## Purpose, collection policy and acquisition

- No source states a formal mission for the Hogwarts library.
- The evidence does not explain who selected ordinary acquisitions, approved
  donations, chose editions, withdrew outdated works or paid for books.
- Professor influence over annual course books is demonstrated later, but the
  respective powers of teachers, librarian, headmaster and governors over the
  general collection are not defined.
- The Malfoy–Beedle dispute shows a governor attempting removal, Dumbledore
  refusing and a Board majority supporting him. It does not reveal a standing
  challenge procedure, voting rule or ordinary acquisitions policy.
- No evidence says whether professors could place books on reserve, recommend
  purchases or move works into the Restricted Section.

## Textbooks and course reading

- Later lists establish required books by year, but no surviving pre-1984
  Hogwarts list has been identified in the structured evidence.
- It is unclear whether every listed title was mandatory, merely recommended,
  or sometimes shared or supplied by the school.
- The evidence does not distinguish school approval, teacher prescription and
  commercial availability for most titles.
- *Fantastic Beasts* is described as long approved, but the first adoption
  date, required years and edition history are unknown.
- The second-year Lockhart list shows a teacher appointment shaping purchases,
  but does not establish the formal authority by which the list was adopted.
- The old *Advanced Potion-Making* copy belonged to a classroom stock. Its
  presence cannot be used to age the library collection.

## Ordinary library use

- Later scenes show study, homework, revision, conversation and research, but
  no source supplies ordinary opening or closing hours.
- The evidence does not define seating rules, permissible noise, group-work
  rules or whether food and drink were allowed.
- Hermione-heavy examples reveal what a determined pupil could do, not how
  frequently the average pupil used the library.
- The dataset contains no systematic evidence for use by House, age, subject
  or social background.
- No source establishes whether classes were formally taught in the library,
  whether librarians offered research instruction, or whether professors
  assigned library sessions.
- The Invisibility section is observed once; no complete subject arrangement,
  shelving taxonomy, catalogue, index or finding aid is attested.

## Independent research and research failure

- The Flamel search establishes multiple reference books and difficulty
  finding specialised information, but not how pupils normally searched beyond
  scanning shelves and consulting remembered titles.
- No source identifies a card catalogue, enchanted catalogue, shelf marks,
  accession register or librarian-created index.
- Pince assists in the second-task search, but the passage does not specify what
  assistance she gives or whether reference help was a normal duty.
- The evidence does not explain whether pupils could request books from other
  institutions, consult professors' private collections or use interlibrary
  loans.
- Failed research may show an incomplete collection, poor indexing, a badly
  framed question or genuine rarity. The source does not decide among these.

## Restricted Section policy

- Later sources securely establish the section and a teacher-signed-note
  procedure, but not its adoption date or pre-1984 continuity.
- The full decision rule for placing a work in the Restricted Section is
  unknown.
- Age alone is not shown to determine access. A second-year obtains a book with
  a signature, while an older champion seeks the same administrative route.
- The evidence does not establish which teachers could sign, whether the note
  had to name a book or purpose, how long permission lasted, or whether Pince
  could refuse an apparently valid request.
- The sources do not say whether a restricted book could ordinarily leave the
  library, how long it could be held, or whether use was supervised at a table.
- The rope boundary and screaming book are observed during a clandestine night
  entry. They do not establish daytime security, a magical alarm system or a
  property shared by all restricted books.
- The evidence does not show that every Restricted Section work concerned Dark
  Magic. Advanced Potions, Defence, survival research and one much-later
  literary claim point to a broader but undefined range.
- No comprehensive Restricted Section inventory should be attempted.

## Dangerous knowledge

- *Magick Moste Evile* mentions Horcruxes but refuses instruction; *Secrets of
  the Darkest Art* supplies instructions and was later removed. The dataset
  does not establish whether they were contemporaneously shelved together or
  whether both were ever in the Restricted Section.
- Dumbledore's removal of Horcrux books is undated. It cannot safely be placed
  before or after his 1940s conversation with Riddle, his accession as
  headmaster, or Voldemort's first rise without further evidence.
- The source says Dumbledore removed the books but did not destroy them. It does
  not state his motive, formal authority, consultation with Pince or governors,
  or whether the removal was recorded.
- Hermione suggests that Voldemort may have learned from *Secrets of the
  Darkest Art*, but the source does not prove he read the work at Hogwarts.
- The evidence does not establish whether Hogwarts preserved dangerous works
  for pedagogical, historical, defensive or merely inherited reasons.
- No safe general statement can claim that teachers refused to teach everything
  present in the library; only particular examples support that distinction.

## Librarianship, borrowing and book care

- Irma Pince is directly attested only in later Hogwarts evidence. Her tenure
  before the 1990s and any predecessor are not established.
- Pince's personal severity must remain distinct from institutional policy.
- The *Quidditch Through the Ages* plate proves borrower and due-date recording,
  but no ordinary loan period, renewal practice, overdue treatment, fine scale
  or lost-book procedure is attested.
- Snape's “outside the school” rule has only medium confidence because Harry
  suspects it was invented. It requires corroboration before use as settled
  policy.
- Dumbledore's humorous warning about jinxes and fines does not define which
  protections were usual or who imposed them.
- The evidence does not establish staff size. It cannot support an assumption
  that Pince worked alone simply because no assistant is named.
- No source establishes conservation, repair, rebinding, environmental
  protection or response to magically hazardous books.

## Age and provenance of the collection

- The age of a work, the age of an edition, the age of a physical copy and the
  duration of Hogwarts custody remain separate questions in nearly every case.
- Medieval legal cases in later volumes do not make the volumes medieval.
- The “old” Flamel book is undated and untitled.
- The fifteenth-century origin of Beedle's work does not date the Hogwarts copy
  challenged by Malfoy.
- The nearly fifty-year-old Potions book is from a classroom cupboard, not the
  library.
- Old newspapers and discipline cards prove retention across decades, not an
  uninterrupted thousand-year archive.
- No evidence supports calling the Hogwarts library itself a thousand-year-old
  continuous collection.
- No chain of acquisition or ownership has been established for any ancient or
  medieval work in the library.

## *Hogwarts: A History*

- No source names the author in the fictional canon evidence collected here;
  the reconstruction must not treat its own Bathilda premise as proof of
  in-world authorship.
- The work's publication date, edition history, publisher and original purpose
  remain unidentified in the current dataset.
- `cos-ch09-001` confirms multiple Hogwarts library copies and Hermione's
  private copy at home, but not where she bought or obtained it.
- No source says Hermione consulted a library copy before first term; her first
  cited knowledge may derive from private reading.
- Repeated citations show that Hermione regarded the book as authoritative.
  They do not establish that all pupils read it or accepted it.
- The book contains a mixture of architectural, protective, historical and
  rule-related claims, but no table of contents or full scope is available.
- Its silence on one institutional subject demonstrates a limit but not the
  cause of the omission.
- A later physical event may confirm a claim Hermione attributes to the book;
  that does not independently date the book's wording or edition.
- Rowling's interview comment (`ext-b07-004`) concerns narrative craft and is
  unnecessary as historical evidence for the fictional institution.

## Records, archives and institutional memory

- Filch's files, prefect lists, trophy-room shields, award records, newspapers,
  the later Hogwarts Archives and the headmaster's Pensieve are distinct
  repositories or record forms. The evidence does not justify merging them.
- Pupil access varies sharply: Riddle's research is reported, Hermione uses
  newspaper material, Harry handles discipline cards only under detention, and
  the later archive appears administratively controlled.
- No central school archive, archivist, retention schedule or finding aid is
  attested before 1984.
- The later Hogwarts Archives are named only after Dumbledore's death; their
  founding date, holdings and relation to the library are unknown.
- The Pensieve is non-written and office-restricted. It may be better reserved
  for Chapter 12 unless used briefly to sharpen the distinction among forms of
  institutional memory.
- Admission records, the Book of Admittance and Quill of Acceptance require
  separate treatment if included. Their inaccessible-tower context does not
  make them library holdings or ordinary pupil sources.
- No evidence has yet been selected for examination papers, routine attendance
  registers or a comprehensive pupil roll accessible to researchers.

## Cultural reading and knowledge before Hogwarts

- *Quidditch Through the Ages* and *Beedle* establish reading beyond direct
  coursework, but the corpus does not provide a broad survey of fiction,
  biography or recreational reading in the Hogwarts library.
- Beedle's tales are familiar to many Hogwarts pupils, but no proportion,
  House distribution or curricular use is established.
- Ron, Harry and Hermione illustrate unequal inherited knowledge. They do not
  justify a universal binary between wizard-raised and Muggle-raised pupils.
- Hermione's extensive pre-term reading shows that books could help a
  Muggle-raised pupil acquire magical knowledge. The claim that books
  institutionally “bridged” background differences remains a bounded inference,
  not a measured outcome.
- Harry's ignorance stems from exceptional suppression as well as Muggle
  upbringing and should not stand for every Muggle-raised pupil.
- The evidence does not show that Hogwarts deliberately designed the library
  to equalise cultural knowledge.

## Censorship and control

- The Malfoy–Beedle dispute is post-1984. Its institutional value is clear, but
  it cannot establish ordinary governance in 1984 or earlier.
- No formal Hogwarts collection-challenge rule or governor power over
  individual books is stated.
- Dumbledore's successful refusal shows one balance of headmaster and Board
  support, not a universal veto or precedent.
- Removal of Horcrux books differs from placing books in the Restricted Section
  and from destroying them; the reasons and procedures must not be collapsed.
- Umbridge's *Quibbler* ban controlled possession of a periodical throughout
  the school and belongs to exceptional Ministry intervention, not ordinary
  librarianship.
- Beatrix Bloxam's sanitised tales concern publishing and family reading, not a
  Hogwarts collection decision.
- The later claim about *Sonnets of a Sorcerer* is too remote to establish a
  longstanding category of banned literary works.

## Questions the current extraction cannot answer

1. When was a Hogwarts library first established, and where was it before the
   later room attested in the novels?
2. Did the Restricted Section exist before 1984, and if so under what name and
   rules?
3. Who chose, purchased, catalogued, repaired and withdrew books?
4. What formal authority did the librarian possess relative to teachers and
   the headmaster?
5. How were ordinary books arranged and located beyond occasional named
   sections?
6. What were the ordinary borrowing period, renewal rules, overdue penalties
   and rules for taking books beyond the castle?
7. Which restricted works could be removed from the library, and under what
   supervision?
8. When did Dumbledore remove the Horcrux books, and by what authority?
9. How many old works survived as old physical copies rather than recent
   editions of old texts?
10. Was there a pre-1984 archive distinct from the library, and could pupils or
    historians consult it?
11. Who wrote *Hogwarts: A History*, when was it first published, and how did
    Hogwarts acquire multiple copies?
12. How representative were Hermione's research habits of the wider pupil
    body?

  as an explicit verification point before drafting.

## Stage 2 conclusion

The current extraction can support a chapter built around written knowledge as
a durable but regulated institutional resource. It cannot support a continuous
history of the library from the founders, a detailed 1984 operating manual, a
complete Restricted Section policy or an archival map of Hogwarts. The
chapter's strongest honest structure will therefore distinguish:

1. securely older books, records and pre-1984 research;
2. later ordinary observations of library work and permission;
3. distinct administrative and archival memory; and
4. exceptional later efforts to remove or suppress written material.
