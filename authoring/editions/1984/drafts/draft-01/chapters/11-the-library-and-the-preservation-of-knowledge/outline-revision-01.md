## Central argument

Hogwarts did not depend entirely on knowledge passing directly from professor
to pupil. Required books, a working library, specialised works and retained
records allowed information to survive individual teachers and generations.
Pupils could use that written store to complete assigned work or to pursue
questions of their own, although an answer might be difficult to locate or
absent from the available shelves. Access was not unlimited: later evidence
shows teacher permission, librarian mediation, removal and political dispute
governing what pupils could read. Hogwarts therefore preserved written
knowledge while also regulating access to it, without evidence for a timeless,
complete or unrestricted collection.
