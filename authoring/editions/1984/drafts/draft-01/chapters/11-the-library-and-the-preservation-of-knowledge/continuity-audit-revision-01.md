## Internal continuity

- The opening's later-evidence limitation governs ordinary library operation
  and is renewed only where evidence class changes.
- Course books lead naturally to library use, then to research, discoverability
  and the special boundary around restricted works.
- The discussion of dangerous knowledge moves through four non-equivalent
  conditions: permissioned use, textual refusal, difficulty of discovery and
  removal from pupil access.
- Historical depth follows removal because the argument broadens from dangerous
  survivals to older ordinary material.
- *Hogwarts: A History* serves as one controlled case of written institutional
  memory rather than a substitute for the chapter's full argument.
- Disciplinary files, Riddle's mixed sources and the later Archives remain
  distinct repositories and access situations.
- The Malfoy dispute and Umbridge ban increase the scale of control from a
  collection challenge to exceptional schoolwide possession censorship.
- The conclusion preserves both sides of the argument: written survival and
  selective access.
