## Consolidated claims

### C5-01 — Slytherin sought exclusion, not merely preference

- **Historical conclusion:** After several years of cooperation, Slytherin
  sought to restrict magical education at Hogwarts to children from
  all-magical families and objected to admitting pupils of Muggle parentage.
  This was an exclusionary admission demand, not merely preferential treatment
  within his own House.
- **Classification:** established
- **Event chronology:** founder era, after several years of joint operation
- **Knowledge access:** reconstructed Bagshot narrative candidate
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** The record does not preserve Slytherin's proposed rule in
  formal language or show that Hogwarts ever adopted it.
- **Primary section:** A Disagreement the Houses Could Not Contain
- **Cross-chapter status:** inherited distinction from Chapters 2 and 4;
  developed here into the cause of rupture

### C5-04 — Slytherin departed; compulsion remains a later complication

  no procedure and cannot silently sharpen Bagshot's account.
- **Classification:** established departure; disputed or layered mechanism

- **Limitations:** No date, formal act, farewell, destination, or precise degree

### C5-05 — Hogwarts and Slytherin House survived the founder's exit

- **Historical conclusion:** The founder departed, but the school did not end
  and his House was not erased. The Hat tradition explicitly moves from four
  founders to three while continuing to speak from the later four-House school.
  The statement that institutional life continued is a bounded inference from
  this continuity, not a claim that there was no disruption.
- **Classification:** strongly supported bounded inference
- **Event chronology:** immediately after the departure and continuing thereafter
- **Knowledge access:** reconstructed Bagshot narrative candidate
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** No source describes the first term after departure, the
  governance response, staffing, enrolment, or whether Slytherin House changed
  internally.
- **Primary section:** Hogwarts Without Slytherin
- **Cross-chapter status:** new consequence; do not reopen general House history

### C5-06 — Before later proof, the Chamber was a detailed but unverified tradition

- **Historical conclusion:** The pre-1984 school tradition claimed that
  Slytherin secretly built a chamber unknown to the other founders, sealed a
  monster within it, and reserved its opening to a true heir who would purge
  pupils deemed unworthy. Slytherin's remembered ability to speak with snakes
  later became associated with the tradition, but the initial historical
  account did not identify the monster or access language as proven facts.
- **Classification:** attributed tradition
- **Event chronology:** alleged founder-era construction and future intention
- **Knowledge access:** reconstructed Bagshot narrative may report the legend,
  not its later proof
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** No contemporary founder record establishes construction,
  timing, monster species, or whether Slytherin publicly announced the story.
- **Primary section:** The Chamber Beneath the School
- **Cross-chapter status:** new tradition owned by Chapter 5

- **Historical conclusion:** Learned witches and wizards and successive school
  leaders searched for the Chamber repeatedly without finding accepted
  evidence. In the knowledge available before later discovery, treating the
  story as unproved or legendary was therefore a defensible historical
  judgement, even though categorical denial proved wrong.
- **Classification:** established searches plus editorial interpretation
- **Event chronology:** repeated searches over an undated span before 1984
- **Knowledge access:** reconstructed Bagshot narrative candidate
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** No complete search record, dates, methods, or names survive in

  and that a Basilisk was concealed within it. The later official account
  identifies these as Slytherin's arrangements and ties control to him and his
  descendants. This confirms the legend's core while leaving some construction
  details and the later alteration history separate.
- **Classification:** later discovery about an earlier institution
- **Event chronology:** founder-era origin claimed; mechanisms observed in 1993
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition only
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** Direct observation does not establish that Slytherin
  personally made every surviving feature; Riddle's heir claims are
  self-interested; no later fact may be voiced as Bagshot knowledge.
- **Primary section:** The Chamber Beneath the School, as a concise later

### C5-09 — The surviving entrance was historically altered

- **Historical conclusion:** The entrance found in the twentieth century was
  not an untouched founder-era installation. A later official account says an
  original trapdoor and tunnels were threatened by eighteenth-century plumbing
  and secretly adapted by Corvinus Gaunt; later observation confirms the
  bathroom and pipe mechanism. The alteration helps explain why architectural
  inspection could fail without making every failed search identical.
- **Classification:** later retrospective account corroborated by later observation
- **Event chronology:** founder era, eighteenth-century alteration, 1993 discovery
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition only
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** No exact date, plan, or magical method survives, and the
  identity of the bathroom is later observational knowledge.
- **Primary section:** A Legend the School Could Not Prove
- **Cross-chapter status:** use only to explain concealment; Chapter 6 owns full
  architectural development

- **Historical conclusion:** Later history shows blood-status prejudice
  surviving in school speech, elite adult society, and eventually state
  persecution. These later forms demonstrate ideological persistence, not a
  continuous organisation founded by Slytherin and not the collective guilt of
  Slytherin House.
- **Classification:** later historical consequence and bounded editorial interpretation
- **Event chronology:** 1992, 1994, and 1997
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition only
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** The examples do not trace a complete chain of transmission

  House. Detailed later political history is deferred.
