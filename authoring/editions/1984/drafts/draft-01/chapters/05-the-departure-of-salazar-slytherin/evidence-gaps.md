# Chapter 5 Source and Evidence Gaps

## The dispute

- The strongest historical account says Slytherin wanted magical learning kept
  within all-magical families and disliked admitting pupils of Muggle
  parentage. It supports exclusion, not merely preferential treatment.
- No formal proposal, rule text, vote, admissions register, or founder letter
  survives in the selected evidence.
- The rift is described as being between Slytherin and “the others,” but only
  Gryffindor is named in a serious direct argument. Hufflepuff's broader welcome
  supplies ideological contrast; Ravenclaw's admission position is not recorded.
- “A few years” and “several happy years” provide only a broad duration. No year,
  term, season, or exact sequence of intermediate disputes can be supplied.
- The historical minimum is disagreement, a growing rift, and a serious
  argument. Duelling and fighting come from the Sorting Hat's poetic tradition.
  No source identifies combatants, spells, injuries, a victor, or a single duel
  that decided the outcome.
- The Hat says Houses turned upon one another, but it does not establish how
  many pupils participated or whether every founder fought personally.

## The departure

- `cos-ch09-004` says Slytherin left after the serious argument.
  `ootp-ch11-003` remembers that he departed and fighting subsided.
  `ext-a01-001`, published much later, says the other three founders forced him
  out. The later source gives no mechanism.
- The chapter therefore cannot claim a voluntary resignation, formal expulsion,
  vote, banishment, defeated duel, or negotiated withdrawal.
- No precise date, farewell, destination, or immediate administrative response
  is recorded.
- Continuation of Hogwarts and of four Houses is strongly supported, but no
  source describes the first days or term after departure. “The school
  survived” is secure; “operations continued without interruption” would be too
  strong.
- Nothing selected explains who taught Slytherin pupils immediately after he
  left, whether House leadership changed, or how the remaining founders
  represented his place in the school.

## The Chamber tradition before confirmation

- The pre-confirmation tradition names a hidden Chamber, an unspecified horror
  or monster, a true heir, and a future purge. It does not identify the monster
  as a Basilisk or state Parselmouth-only access as established history.
- The legend says the other founders knew nothing of the Chamber. No
  contemporary founder document independently verifies their ignorance.
- No selected pre-1984 source dates construction relative to the dispute or
  departure. The later official account says the Chamber existed before
  Slytherin was forced out but does not give a year.
- The later official account speculates that the Chamber may initially have
  served as a place for disapproved instruction. That suggestion is explicitly
  speculative and does not advance to the outline or draft.
- The means by which the Chamber story first entered school tradition is
  unknown. A later official account infers that Slytherin spoke of it or allowed
  others to enter, but no named witness or record survives.
- The phrase “true heir” does not establish a legal inheritance rule, a unique
  genealogical claimant in every generation, or a complete line of descent.

## Searches, concealment, and architecture

- Repeated searches are well attested, but their dates, leaders, methods,
  duration, and coverage are not.
- Pre-1984 investigators' failure justified scepticism but did not prove
  non-existence. Their categorical denial was historically understandable and
  factually wrong.
- The later official explanation—none of the searchers was a Parselmouth—was not
  available to the reconstructed narrator and cannot appear as her explanation.
- The entrance discovered in 1993 belonged to an eighteenth-century adaptation
  made after plumbing threatened an older trapdoor. Founder-era construction
  must not be described with modern bathroom or pipe details.
- The exact year of the plumbing work, the original trapdoor's position, and the
  magic used by Corvinus Gaunt are not recorded.
- The observed route includes plumbing, deep tunnels, serpent gates, and a
  central Chamber. The claim that part of the route lies beneath the lake is a
  character inference, not a surveyed fact.
- Direct observation cannot prove Slytherin personally made every surviving
  carving, statue, passage, or control; later heirs may have altered the complex.

## Later confirmation and source access

- `cos-ch16-006`, `cos-ch16-007`, `cos-ch17-001`, and `cos-ch17-006` record
  discoveries after the 1984 narrative boundary. They may confirm older facts
  only in an identifiable later editorial addition.
- `ext-a01-001` through `ext-a01-003` are high-authority official retrospective
  sources published in 2015. Their authority does not make them available to
  Bagshot.
- The later evidence confirms a real Chamber, Parseltongue-responsive access,
  and a Basilisk. It does not make every pre-existing rumour, heir claimant, or
  interpretation correct.
- Riddle's descent and rarity claims in `cos-ch17-004` are self-interested
  testimony. They corroborate how the heir tradition was claimed, not a complete
  neutral genealogy.
- Later confirmation should be concise. Retelling the attacks, victims, diary,
  rescue, or battle would displace the chapter's founder-era subject.

## Ideological afterlife

- Later evidence establishes blood-status prejudice among some pupils and
  adults and its eventual conversion into state persecution. It does not prove
  an uninterrupted institutional line from Slytherin to every later adherent.
- Hogwarts's admission of Muggle-born pupils demonstrates that Slytherin's
  restriction did not become the school's standing policy.
- No later Slytherin pupil's misconduct proves Salazar's personal intentions.
  Conversely, Salazar's doctrine does not make every member of his House an
  adherent.
- The examples selected for ideological survival are illustrative, not a full
  social or political history. Detailed late-twentieth-century treatment is
  reserved for later chapters.

## Tempting but unsupported conclusions

- Gryffindor defeated Slytherin in a decisive duel.
- The four founders held a formal vote or trial.
- Slytherin was formally expelled under a recorded school law.
- Hufflepuff and Ravenclaw took identical positions or fought Slytherin directly.
- The other founders discovered the Chamber before Slytherin left.
- Slytherin built the Chamber only after the final argument.
- Bagshot knew the monster was a Basilisk or the entrance required Parseltongue.
- The bathroom and plumbing formed part of Slytherin's original design.
- Every failed search was equally thorough or used the same methods.
- Every later pure-blood ideologue inherited doctrine directly from Slytherin.
- Every Slytherin pupil shared the founder's blood-status views.

## Research-layer observations

- The extraction-era classifications of several Chamber-discovery entries call
  them pre-1984 historical candidates because the structures are old. For this
  chapter, their **knowledge access** is later discovery; event chronology does
  not override that distinction.
- The source layer contains ample evidence for later Chamber crises, but the
  selected set deliberately excludes episode detail not required for founder-era
  confirmation.
- No research-layer file was modified while recording these gaps.
