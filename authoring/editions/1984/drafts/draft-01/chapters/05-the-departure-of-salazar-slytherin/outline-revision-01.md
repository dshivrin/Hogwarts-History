## Chapter argument

The House arrangement could preserve different educational judgements only
while the founders accepted one school community. Slytherin's attempt to make
ancestry a condition of admission challenged that community itself. The dispute
grew from disagreement to a serious rupture associated in school tradition with
duelling and fighting, ending in Slytherin's departure. Hogwarts survived and
retained his House, but the unresolved conflict entered institutional memory in
the Chamber legend. Before 1984, repeated failed searches made scepticism
reasonable; later evidence proved the Chamber, Basilisk, and language lock real
without making that proof part of Bagshot's knowledge. The resulting legacy is
both ideological and material: an exclusionary doctrine outlived its author,
and the castle concealed a mechanism made to enact it.

## Opening movement

Begin at Chapter 4's exact endpoint. Do not re-explain four House ideals.
Contrast differences about qualities within admitted pupils with Slytherin's
question of who could enter at all. Remind the reader in one sentence that the
founders had cooperated for years so the rupture registers as the failure of a
real alliance, not the inevitable behaviour of lifelong enemies.

## Historical progression

House compromise reaches its limit → admission dispute widens → serious
Gryffindor–Slytherin argument → fighting preserved in Hat tradition → Slytherin
departs → Hogwarts and his House survive → Chamber tradition attaches a hidden
threat to the rupture → repeated failed searches produce scepticism → later
discoveries confirm the threat and explain concealment → the doctrine and the
institutional memory outlive the founder.

## Conclusion

Close the founders' era without a modern plot summary. Hogwarts survived the
loss of one maker, but did not remove his name, House, doctrine from memory, or
the secret later shown to exist beneath the school. The concluding image should
be institutional rather than sensational: a castle able to outlast its makers
and to conceal the consequences of their disagreements, preparing the turn to
Chapter 6, *A Castle Unlike Any Other*.
