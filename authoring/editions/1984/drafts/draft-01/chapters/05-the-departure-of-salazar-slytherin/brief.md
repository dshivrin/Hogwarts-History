## Chapter purpose

Reconstruct the first major internal crisis in Hogwarts history: the passage
from a containable difference among four educators to an irreconcilable dispute
over who was entitled to enter the school, Slytherin's departure, and the
institutional memory preserved in the Chamber tradition.

## Chapter argument

The founders' cooperation was real and lasted for years. It failed when
Slytherin sought to exclude pupils of Muggle parentage from magical education.
The firm historical account gives a growing rift, a serious argument with
Gryffindor, and Slytherin's departure; the Sorting Hat's continuing tradition
adds wider House conflict, duelling, and fighting. Hogwarts survived and kept
Slytherin's House, but it also preserved a darker story: that he had left a
hidden chamber, a monster, and a future heir charged with completing his
exclusion by violence. Repeated failed searches made the story seem legendary
before 1984. Later discoveries proved the Chamber and Basilisk real, but that
confirmation belongs to a later editorial voice and must never be presented as
knowledge available to the reconstructed narrator.

## Entry and exit

- **Entry point:** Chapter 4's final distinction between a House preference and
  an ancestry barrier, at the moment the House compromise ceases to contain the
  disagreement.
- **Exit point:** the founders' era closes with a school that has survived its
  first rupture while carrying both Slytherin's House and an unresolved legend;
  Chapter 6 can then turn to the changing castle that inherited those secrets.

## Included subjects

- Slytherin's demand to restrict Hogwarts to all-magical families and exclude
  pupils of Muggle parentage;
- the opposition of the other founders, with particular weight given to
  Gryffindor where the historical account is explicit;
- the escalation from disagreement to rupture, including the Hat's attributed
  tradition of duelling and fighting;
- Slytherin's departure and the uncertainty over whether he left or was forced
  out;
- the continuation of Hogwarts and the survival of Slytherin House;
- the pre-1984 Chamber tradition: concealed room, unknown monster, true heir,
  and intended purge;
- Slytherin's remembered Parseltongue and the later-established access mechanism;
- repeated searches and the historical reasons for scepticism;
- later confirmation of the Chamber, Basilisk, and Parselmouth-only mechanisms,
  marked as later evidence;
- the persistence of blood-status ideology without treating all later
  Slytherins as adherents.

## Excluded or deferred subjects

- founder biographies, the foundation, and the general history of Houses;
- invented debates, votes, expulsion proceedings, duels, farewells, or dates;
- a detailed retelling of the 1940s or 1990s Chamber crises;
- a full history of the Gaunt family, Tom Riddle, Voldemort, or Horcruxes;
- a general history of blood prejudice or later Slytherin pupils;
- a castle-room catalogue, detailed plumbing history, or architectural tour;
- any claim that Slytherin's admission doctrine became Hogwarts policy.

## Presentation model

Use the unified narrative model. Reconstructed Bagshot passages may state the
pre-1984 historical account and the Chamber tradition with appropriate
attribution. Later discoveries enter only through explicit temporal language
such as “later evidence established” or “subsequent investigation confirmed.”
No sentence may allow later proof to become invisible knowledge of the
pre-1984 narrator.
