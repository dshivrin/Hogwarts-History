# Chapter 18 Evidence Gaps

## Establishment, appointment and succession

- The selected evidence describes a long succession of headmasters and
  headmistresses but does not date the creation of the office or prove that its
  form remained unchanged from the founding onward.
- Dilys Derwent is the earliest securely dated named holder in the selected
  record, serving from 1741 to 1768. This is not evidence that she was the first
  Headmistress or that no earlier named head can exist outside the selected
  corpus.
- No selected source explains a normal appointment process for heads.
  Governor authority over appointment or suspension is stated in 1993, but the
  mechanism by which Dippet, Dumbledore or other earlier heads entered office
  is not preserved.
- McGonagall's immediate occupation of the office after Dumbledore's death
  suggests practical succession from deputy to acting headship, but no
  appointment instrument or governor decision is shown.
- Snape's later appointment is announced publicly, but the appointing body and
  lawful procedure are not identified in the preserved newspaper passage.

## Ordinary powers

- No written charter, employment code, disciplinary code or comprehensive
  statement of headmasterial powers was located.
- Dippet could place Kettleburn on probation and ban future pantomimes, but the
  evidence does not define probation, identify its duration, or show whether
  Dippet possessed unrestricted dismissal power.
- Dippet and Dumbledore rejected teaching applications, and Dumbledore made or
  announced later appointments. These cases support strong practical staffing
  discretion but not exclusive appointment power in every period.
- Dumbledore refused Hagrid's resignation. The evidence does not establish
  whether every teacher's resignation required a head's acceptance.
- The headmaster could control or contest access to the castle in several later
  crises. The legal basis, ordinary procedure and historical continuity of
  those acts remain unstated.

## The 1943 Chamber crisis

- Dippet's refusal of Riddle's summer residence and his reference to Ministry
  discussion of closure are preserved through a memory controlled by Riddle's
  diary. No independent contemporary administrative record was located.
- Riddle's first account falsely identifies Hagrid as the culprit. His later
  confession establishes the frame-up and says Dumbledore persuaded Dippet to
  retain Hagrid as gamekeeper, but it remains testimony from the perpetrator.
- The surviving material does not identify who formally ordered Hagrid's
  expulsion or wand-breaking, what process was followed, or what evidence was
  heard.
- The source shows that Dippet acted while the Ministry discussed closure; it
  does not establish who possessed final legal closure authority in 1943.

## Dumbledore: office and personal influence

- Dumbledore's magical reputation, political offices and personal alliances
  are independently attested, but the selected cases rarely isolate exactly
  how much compliance followed from the headship and how much from the man.
- Fudge's hostile description of Dumbledore's “free rein” is unusually useful
  practical evidence but is not a neutral constitutional statement.
- The Lupin accommodation establishes a headmaster-approved admission and
  significant physical provision. It does not identify every approving body,
  builder, cost, staff participant or legal rule.
- Dumbledore's continuing influence after suspension is expressed through
  loyalty and rhetoric. It is evidence of personal authority, not a power of
  every removed head.

## Governors and Ministry

- The 1993 Board comprised twelve governors and used a signed suspension
  order. The source does not show ordinary meeting rules, voting thresholds,
  terms of membership or the routine relationship between the Board and head.
- Coercion reportedly affected the suspension signatures. The incident proves
  that a suspension mechanism was invoked, not that this was a normal or valid
  use of it.
- Dumbledore's recall and Lucius Malfoy's removal are recorded without complete
  formal procedures.
- Later closure deliberation assigns the final reopening decision to governors,
  but does not establish that the same allocation existed in 1943.
- Ministry interventions vary from negotiation to new legislation and
  coercive occupation. The selected evidence does not reduce them to one stable
  jurisdictional rule.

## Exceptional headships

- Umbridge's powers changed as she moved from teacher to High Inquisitor to
  Ministry-appointed head. The evidence does not show that the ordinary office
  of head carried all powers she had received by decree.
- The headmaster's office reportedly resisted Umbridge, but this is second-hand
  testimony and cannot define a legal succession rule.
- Snape's appointment occurred under a Voldemort-controlled Ministry. His
  nominal powers, the Carrows' powers and Voldemort's external commands cannot
  be cleanly separated in every case.
- Snape's private promise to protect pupils explains motive and constraint; it
  does not turn all public acts of his headship into Dumbledore-directed policy.
- Staff resistance ended Snape's practical control, but no on-page legal
  revocation of his title is preserved.

## Delegation

- McGonagall's title, admissions correspondence, disciplinary responsibility,
  crisis work during Dumbledore's absence and post-death leadership support a
  delegated hierarchy in practice.
- No selected source supplies a complete job description for the Deputy
  Headmistress, defines automatic acting authority, or proves that every head
  used the same pattern of delegation.

## Unresolved historical questions

- When and by whom was the office of Headmaster or Headmistress established?
- Who normally appointed a head before the documented governor and Ministry
  confrontations?
- Did governors possess the same powers in Dilys Derwent's, Eupraxia Mole's,
  Dippet's and Dumbledore's periods?
- Which staffing decisions required consultation, and which could a head make
  alone?
- Could a head close Hogwarts, or only recommend closure to governors or the
  Ministry?
- Which decisions by Dippet, Dumbledore and later heads became durable school
  practice rather than ending with their tenure?
- What precise authority did a deputy exercise when the head was present,
  absent, suspended or dead?

## Claims deliberately excluded

- The Headmaster or Headmistress owned Hogwarts or possessed absolute power.
- The Board of Governors elected every head.
- The Ministry normally appointed the head before its exceptional later
  interventions.
- Every head possessed identical powers or followed identical procedures.
- Dumbledore's influence, magical ability or external political standing
  belonged to the office itself.
- Umbridge's decree-created powers or Snape's wartime position represented
  ordinary Hogwarts government.
- The portrait office, Pensieve or castle itself conclusively chose lawful
  successors.
- Every individual decision became permanent institutional policy.
