## Purpose

Examine the office of Headmaster or Headmistress as an institution. Individual
heads enter only where their decisions reveal the office's powers, limits,
delegations, dependence upon personal judgement, or vulnerability to external
authority. The chapter begins where Chapter 17 ends: Hogwarts's protections do
not recognise failure or alter themselves; a person or group must judge and
act.

## Central historical question

What did it mean to govern Hogwarts, and how much could one Headmaster or
Headmistress change an institution much older than themselves?

The approved argumentative sequence is:

> office → individual exercise of authority → Dippet and Dumbledore →
> governors → Ministry → exceptional or fragmented authority → person versus
> office

## Working hypothesis to test

The evidence may support an office that directed Hogwarts without owning it,
possessed wide practical discretion over school life, and could alter rules,
staffing, access, accommodation and crisis response. It may also show that the
office was constrained by governors, Ministry action, law, other staff,
political conditions and the authority of the particular person occupying it.

This is a research hypothesis, not an authorised conclusion. Each power must
be demonstrated separately. Repeated action by Dumbledore cannot be converted
into a timeless constitutional rule.

## Chronological and source-access layers

The reconstruction boundary remains 1984.

1. **Reconstructed Bagshot layer:** claims reasonably available to a history
   completed by 1984.
2. **Later-carried history:** pre-1984 events disclosed by later sources. These
   may enlarge the history but may not be voiced as knowledge demonstrated to
   the reconstructed narrator.
3. **Later observation of an older institution:** post-1984 evidence that an
   older-looking office, practice or relationship persisted, without proving
   its origin or unchanged continuity.
4. **Later editorial history:** post-1984 events used to test or demonstrate
   the office. They must not be projected backwards.
5. **Excluded or deferred:** material that is altered-timeline, biographical
   without institutional function, unsupported, or owned by another chapter.

No separate addendum is proposed. Later material is structurally necessary to
test the institutional argument, but the manuscript must keep chronology and
source access legible in evidence comments and natural attribution.

## Required analytical distinctions

- the continuity of the office versus the conduct of an individual head;
- demonstrated authority versus personal influence;
- ordinary school government versus emergency action;
- headmasterial authority versus delegated staff authority;
- practical action versus an unknown legal or constitutional basis;
- Hogwarts autonomy versus governor or Ministry intervention;
- holding the title versus possessing practical independence.

## Required subjects

- Earliest reliable evidence for the office, securely identified heads, and
  the gaps in appointment and succession history.
- Ordinary authority over rules, pupils, staff, access, accommodation,
  ceremonies, facilities and security, only where separately demonstrated.
- Armando Dippet as the principal earlier case: the 1943 Chamber crisis,
  Riddle's summer request, Hagrid's expulsion and retention, Kettleburn's
  probation, the pantomime prohibition, and Riddle's teaching request.
- Albus Dumbledore as the strongest case for transformative discretion:
  Lupin's admission and accommodation, Hagrid's continuing place and later
  appointment, staff selection and retention, access and security decisions,
  and relations with governors and the Ministry.
- The Board of Governors' demonstrated suspension and recall powers, including
  coercion in the second Chamber crisis and the absence of a complete normal
  procedure.
- The school–Ministry boundary, especially where external action affected
  closure, discipline, staffing, inspection and curriculum.
- Dolores Umbridge's separate roles as teacher, Ministry representative, High
  Inquisitor and Ministry-appointed head of Hogwarts.
- Severus Snape's wartime headship as evidence that title and independence can
  diverge.
- Minerva McGonagall's deputy and acting functions as evidence that Hogwarts
  government was delegated rather than performed by one person alone.
- A final distinction between the ancient institution and its temporary
  custodians, leading to Chapter 19, *Hogwarts in the Modern Age*.

## Editorial notes

- Prefer decisions that reveal authority to a catalogue of office-holders.
- For every decision identify the actor, the action, the demonstrated reach,
  the source-access layer, and what the evidence does not reveal.
- State the difference between an office-holder's formal or demonstrated power
  and deference earned through reputation, ability, political office or
  personal loyalty.
- Use one meaningful limitation beside each claim rather than accumulating
  drafting-room caveats.
- The argument should move from ordinary exercise of authority towards
  contested and fragmented authority, then return to institutional continuity.
