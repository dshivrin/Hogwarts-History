## Chapter argument

Hogwarts required a person empowered to turn observation into decision: to
admit or exclude, appoint or refuse, tolerate or prohibit, preserve a practice
or alter it. The Headmaster or Headmistress supplied that centre of judgement
and enjoyed substantial practical discretion within the school. Yet the head
did not own Hogwarts, did not perform all government alone and did not exercise
the same independence in every period. Governors could separate an incumbent
from the office; the Ministry could negotiate, legislate or impose; other staff
could administer, succeed or resist; and the authority commanded by one
remarkable person could exceed what the office alone explains.

The office therefore joined continuity to contingency. Its holders inherited
an institution older than themselves, but their decisions changed who could
belong, whom Hogwarts employed, which dangers it tolerated, which activities
it prohibited and how far outsiders entered its government. Hogwarts survived
them, though never wholly unchanged by them.

### Paragraph plan

18. Begin with Lupin because it most clearly joins office and individual
   judgement. Once Dumbledore became headmaster he admitted a pupil whom common
   expectation would have excluded, subject to precautions.
19. Describe only the scale of institutional alteration: tree, route, remote
   building, secrecy and staff participation. The case shows that admission
   could cause Hogwarts to alter its physical practice around one pupil. Do not
   repeat Chapter 17's containment analysis.
20. State the limit. A later near-fatal breach led to a confidentiality order,
   not a recorded redesign or expulsion. The evidence demonstrates a decision,
   not its legal basis, every approval behind it or a universal admissions
   principle.
21. Return briefly to Hagrid. Dumbledore's earlier influence preserved a place
   under Dippet; during his own headship he later added teaching duties. This is
   continuity of personal judgement across two institutional positions, not
   proof that the teacher and head possessed equal authority.
22. Compare the appointment of Lupin with Dumbledore's later refusal of
   Voldemort. In each case the head judged access to pupils through a teaching
   post, but sympathy in one case and distrust in another do not themselves
   define a written eligibility rule.
23. Use the public appointment of Hagrid and Lupin, the later Slughorn/Snape
   announcements and the hostile press account to establish a visible pattern
   of practical staffing discretion. Avoid an appointment catalogue.
24. Give the resignation case its distinct function. Dumbledore reads parent
   response, rejects universal popularity as a standard and refuses Hagrid's
   resignation. This establishes retention and judgement under pressure, not a
   general rule that a head can compel all staff to remain.
25. Draw the section's bounded conclusion: Dumbledore could make Hogwarts
   hospitable to people another head might have excluded, but the sources show
   choices by Dumbledore, not an impersonal programme that automatically
   survived him.

### Paragraph plan

31. Introduce the twelve-signature Order of Suspension as the clearest surviving
   statement of Board power. The source explicitly assigns appointment or
   suspension of the head to governors.
32. Place the qualification in the same movement. Hagrid alleges threats and
   later Dumbledore reports coercion. The incident demonstrates an invoked
   mechanism without establishing that this was a normal or legitimate use of
   it.
33. Separate office from personal authority. Dumbledore steps aside, but
   speaks of loyalty and assistance continuing. Treat this as a claim about his
   influence and the school's loyalties, not a constitutional power of a
   suspended head.
34. Record the reversal: the other governors recall him and Lucius is later
   removed. The evidence reveals recall and Board-removal powers without their
   complete procedures.
35. Use Dumbledore's later commentary only to extend the political timescale:

36. Reconnect the 1943 closure discussion with later jurisdictional evidence.

37. Use the revised expulsion notice to show negotiation rather than either

38. Present Fudge's “free rein” complaint as a hostile admission of ordinary
   practical autonomy over staff and teaching. Its political context both
   strengthens its evidentiary value and prevents treating it as a charter.
39. Introduce Umbridge by office sequence. She arrives as a teacher placed by
   Ministry intervention, then becomes the first High Inquisitor through
   unprecedented legislation. Do not call those inquisitorial powers powers of
   an ordinary teacher or head.

   Inquisitor can override other staff. This shows the Ministry rewriting the
   internal hierarchy rather than merely supervising from outside.
41. Use the Trelawney confrontation to divide authority precisely. Umbridge can
   dismiss; Dumbledore retains authority over residence. Then use Firenze to
   show that Ministry appointment arises only if the head cannot find a

   The reported refusal of office access should be presented cautiously. Its

   reproduce Dumbledore's practical acceptance or authority.
43. Conclude that the Umbridge year is constitutionally revealing because it is
   abnormal. It makes competing powers visible, but it cannot be projected
   backwards as the normal school–Ministry relationship.

### Paragraph plan

44. Introduce Snape through the public announcement of his headship alongside
   the Carrows. The title is clear; the appointing authority and independent
   scope are not. The regime surrounding him was externally controlled.
45. Add the private counterfact to the public office: Dumbledore had secured
   Snape's promise to protect pupils if Voldemort seized Hogwarts. This explains
   divided allegiance without making every later act benevolent or independent.
46. End the tenure at the point of practical control. McGonagall, Flitwick and
   Sprout resist; Snape flees. No formal dismissal is shown. A headship can end
   in fact before the record supplies a lawful procedure.
47. Return to Dumbledore as the opposite problem. Public achievement, magical
   reputation, external office and personal loyalty enlarged the authority he
   could exercise. Where sources cannot separate deference to Dumbledore from
   deference to the Headmaster, say so once and do not pretend precision.
48. Compare without ranking biographies. Dippet, Dumbledore, Umbridge and Snape
   held recognisably related titles under profoundly different conditions.
   Equal title did not produce equal judgement, independence, legitimacy or
   effect.

## Conclusion: Temporary Custodians

- **The purpose of this movement is to show:** Hogwarts outlasted individual
  heads while absorbing consequences from their decisions, and that tension
  leads into the school's modern age.
- **Primary claims:** C18-12.
- **Relevant evidence IDs:** bounded synthesis from `ext-a16-005`,
  `beedle-ch02-005`, `poa-ch18-003`, `cos-ch14-006`, `ootp-ch26-006`,
  `dh-ch30-005`.
- **Authorship layer:** bounded editorial synthesis.

### Paragraph plan

49. State the supported balance. The head directed Hogwarts but did not own it;
   could change practice but not guarantee permanence; could command staff but
   also depended upon them; could resist outsiders but could be suspended,
   overruled, installed or displaced.
50. Return to concrete consequences rather than abstraction. Different heads
   changed whom Hogwarts admitted, retained or trusted, which activities it
   prohibited, what exceptions it accepted and how far external authority
   entered the castle.
51. Close on institution and custodian. Hogwarts survived each holder because
   the office, staff and school continued beyond one life or tenure. It did not
   emerge untouched: accumulated decisions and contests helped make the school
   inherited by the modern age.
52. Build the Chapter 19 transition from the verified title *Hogwarts in the
   Modern Age*. The transition should turn from temporary custodians to the
   longer modern pressures—administrative, political and social—under which an
   ancient school had to continue. Do not preview unverified Chapter 19 details.
