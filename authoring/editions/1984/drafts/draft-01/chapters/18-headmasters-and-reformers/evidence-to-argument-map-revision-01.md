## Claims omitted from the argument

- A complete chronology of heads: evidence contains gaps.
- A normal appointment formula: no selected source supplies one.
- A universal staffing power: examples establish practice but not exclusivity.
- A head's unilateral closure power: the evidence divides or obscures closure
  authority.
- A timeless equivalence between Dumbledore's reach and the office.
- Any inference that Umbridge or Snape represents ordinary school government.
- Full Dippet and Dumbledore biography or generational narrative, reserved for
  the later verified Chapter 20 title.

## Narrative result

The chapter should not read as a second governance survey. Its narrative
question is what happens when a durable office is occupied by different
people. Dippet supplies the earlier control case; Dumbledore demonstrates the
greatest visible discretion; McGonagall reveals delegation; the governors and
Ministry expose limits; Umbridge and Snape demonstrate fragmentation. The
conclusion can then move from individual custodians to the pressures of
*Hogwarts in the Modern Age* without claiming that every change was reform or
every reform endured.
