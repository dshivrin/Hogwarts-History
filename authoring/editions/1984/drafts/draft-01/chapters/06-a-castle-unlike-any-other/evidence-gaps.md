# Chapter 6 Source and Evidence Gaps

## The original castle

- The sources do not preserve a founder-era floor plan, building account,
  construction sequence, list of builders, or division of architectural labour.
- Chapter 3 has already established the foundation and construction at the
  supported level. Chapter 6 cannot enlarge that history by inventing which
  founder designed a room or enchantment.
- No selected evidence dates the Great Hall ceiling, the changing staircases,
  unusual doors, concealed shortcuts, trick steps, or the Room of Requirement
  to the founders.
- Founder-era language attached to the Gryffindor dormitory rule establishes an
  old institutional assumption, not necessarily the date of the currently
  observed slide enchantment.
- Towers, dungeons, corridors, and named rooms establish broad internal
  divisions, but the selected evidence cannot turn them into a complete plan.

## The Great Hall

- `ps-ch07-001` explicitly attributes the enchanted ceiling fact to
  *Hogwarts: A History*. It is therefore core material rather than merely a
  later observation.
- The source says the ceiling resembles the outside sky. It does not explain
  whether it is transparent, illusory, meteorological, predictive, or linked by
  a particular spell.
- The maker, date, maintenance, limits, and history of alteration are unknown.
- Later feast decorations and ceremonial uses belong chiefly to chapters on
  Houses, traditions, education, or crises and are not evidence about the
  ceiling's origin.

## Stairs, doors, and circulation

- `ps-ch08-001` describes 142 staircases in 1991. The number cannot be projected
  backwards as an unchanging founder-era total.
- The same passage says some staircases lead somewhere different at particular
  times; it does not establish that every staircase physically moves or that
  all routes change on one system.
- The vanishing or trick step is repeatedly observed, but no source says whether
  it was designed as a trap, developed accidentally, or is repaired and returns.
- Doors that respond to politeness or tickling and apparent doors that are walls
  establish conditional thresholds, but their makers, dates, and purposes are
  unknown.
- Returning pupils' difficulty finding North Tower supports unreliable
  navigation. It does not prove that their route changed on that day.
- Portrait-guided wayfinding is incidental architectural evidence here; the
  nature and society of portraits remain reserved for Chapter 12.

## Mapping Hogwarts

- The Marauder's Map proves that a detailed magical representation of Hogwarts
  can exist. It does not prove that the map is complete or static.
- `poa-ch10-003` does not by itself identify the mapmakers, creation date, or
  method. Those details are unnecessary for Chapter 6's mapping argument.
- The Room of Requirement's absence from the map in `hbp-ch21-004` is observed;
  the claim that it becomes Unplottable is Hermione's reasoned explanation, not
  a technical statement from a creator.
- The two interview transcripts support an editorial conclusion that a fixed
  blueprint is unsuitable. They are authority-D preservation carriers and must
  not be converted into Bagshot's in-universe testimony.
- “Difficult to map as a normal static building” is supported. “Impossible to
  map in any form” is contradicted by the Marauder's Map and must not be claimed.

## The Room of Requirement

- The room is not named in Dumbledore's earlier chamber-pot anecdote. Later
  evidence identifies the same behaviour, but the relationship should be stated
  as a strong later identification rather than a fact available within the
  anecdote itself.
- Dobby supplies the first explicit operating account: real need, conditional
  appearance, and provision suited to the seeker. No creator or founding date is
  established.
- One observed configuration supplies defensive equipment; another stores
  objects hidden by generations. Neither inventory defines the room's permanent
  contents.
- The active room can resist an imprecise request and appears absent from the
  Marauder's Map. The source does not establish whether every configuration is
  equally unmappable.
- The Mirror of Erised's approximate century in the room proves pre-Harry-era
  use but comes from a later official retrospective. It cannot enter Bagshot's
  knowledge without explicit editorial attribution.
- Later wartime evidence adds selective access, expansion, facilities, and
  moving exits, but those details are not necessary to the present revision and
  remain deferred.

## Alteration and architectural layers

- The Chamber entrance is the clearest dated alteration: an older trapdoor and
  tunnels were threatened by eighteenth-century plumbing, and Corvinus Gaunt
  preserved access beneath the new bathroom.
- No exact year, plan, construction team, or magical method survives.
- Chapter 5 already explains why the concealed entrance mattered to the Chamber
  tradition. Chapter 6 may use the same fact only for its changed historical
  function: old structure surviving beneath later work.
- Classroom eleven's forest-like refitting demonstrates later adaptation of an
  existing room, but the source does not prove that the room transformed itself
  or that the change was permanent.
- These examples support accumulation and adaptation. They do not justify a
  claim that every room has changed, that the castle itself consciously chooses
  renovations, or that a continuous renovation programme existed.

## Chronology and source access

- Harry-era observations may confirm long-lived features but rarely date their
  origin. Existence in the 1990s is not proof of Bagshot's knowledge in 1984.
- Pre-1984 event chronology in `ext-a14-003` and `ext-a01-002` remains later
  official retrospective knowledge.
- `gof-ch23-004`, the Room of Requirement cluster, and `ootp-ch27-001` are
  post-1984 events or observations. They require later editorial attribution.
- The interview sources are out-of-universe commentary and cannot be blended
  invisibly into reconstructed historical narration.

## Chapter-boundary risks

- A route leaving the castle may illustrate an internal threshold but must not
  develop the grounds, Hogsmeade, lake, Forest, gates, or estate boundaries.
- Common-room architecture cannot become another House chapter.
- Classroom use cannot become a curriculum or teaching chapter.
- The headmaster's office cannot become a history of headmasters.
- Portraits and ghosts cannot become a treatment of castle memory through
  residents; Chapter 12 owns that subject.
- Room of Requirement crisis uses, cabinet breach, evacuation, and battle
  functions belong to later institutional history.
- Protective enchantments are deferred except where a threshold's operation is
  itself the architectural point.

## Tempting but unsupported conclusions

- The founders designed all 142 staircases or the castle's present plan.
- Every staircase moves, or all movement follows one timetable.
- The castle is literally sentient.
- Doors, steps, and rooms behave unusually for one common magical reason.
- The Room of Requirement was created by a named founder.
- Dumbledore or any headmaster possessed a complete map of Hogwarts.
- The Marauder's Map represents every room and every configuration.
- The Great Hall ceiling predicts weather or opens onto the sky.
- The eighteenth-century plumbing project was a schoolwide renovation programme
  of known extent.
- Classroom eleven's forest environment was permanent or self-generated.
- Every present-day feature is either original or a documented later addition.

## Research-layer observations

- Extraction-era classifications often label old structures documented later
  as pre-1984 historical candidates. This revision separates the feature's age
  from Bagshot's access to the confirming source.
- The evidence corpus is rich in room and route observations but sparse in
  construction dates. The chapter must argue from behaviour and documented
  alteration rather than pretend to supply an architectural chronology.
- Two preserved 2000 interviews independently state the floor-plan problem, but
  both remain lower-authority carriers and a conceptual duplicate cluster.
- No research-layer file was modified while recording these gaps.
