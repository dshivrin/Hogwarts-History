## Consolidated claims

### C6-01 — The Great Hall establishes architecture as magical environment

- **Historical conclusion:** The Great Hall's ceiling is enchanted to resemble
  the outside sky. This is not merely a later observation: the fact is
  explicitly attributed within the novel to *Hogwarts: A History*. It gives the
  chapter its clearest opening example of a built interior refusing the visual
  limits of an ordinary roof.
- **Classification:** established original-book-core fact
- **Event chronology:** mature Hogwarts; origin and date unknown
- **Knowledge access:** directly available to the reconstructed historical
  tradition through the explicit in-universe book citation
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** No maker, date, spell, maintenance history, or technical
  explanation is supported. Resemblance to the sky is not proof of literal
  openness, weather production, or prediction.
- **Primary section:** A Castle That Refuses to Stay Still
- **Cross-chapter status:** new architectural subject; ceremonial Great Hall use
  remains with Houses, traditions, education, and crisis chapters

### C6-02 — Ordinary circulation is conditional rather than mechanically fixed

- **Historical conclusion:** Hogwarts navigation depends on more than corridors
  joining fixed destinations. The castle includes staircases with time-dependent
  destinations, a vanishing or trick step, doors that respond to manners or
  touch, apparent doors that are walls, concealed shortcuts, and a trapdoor that
  supplies a ladder. These are representative systems, not an exhaustive list.
- **Classification:** established later observation of long-lived castle behaviour
- **Event chronology:** observed from 1991 to 1995; origins undated
- **Knowledge access:** Harry-era confirmation; only the architectural principle,
  not every later scene, may be integrated through later editorial narration
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** The evidence does not say every staircase moves, every feature
  shares one cause, or any selected feature dates from the founders.
- **Primary section:** Stairs, Corridors and Doors
- **Cross-chapter status:** new; portraits remain incidental and their history is deferred

### C6-03 — The castle makes knowledge of routes a practical inheritance

- **Historical conclusion:** Hogwarts can be navigated, but not simply by
  reading surfaces once. Returning pupils still become lost; shortcuts can be
  hidden behind tapestries; dangerous steps must be remembered; and guidance
  may come from inhabitants who move through the castle differently. Familiarity
  is therefore accumulated practice rather than mastery of a stable diagram.
- **Classification:** bounded editorial inference from direct observations
- **Event chronology:** mature school observed in the 1990s; age of routes unknown
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial synthesis
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** Difficulty does not prove that a particular route changed at
  that moment, and portrait behaviour is not developed beyond wayfinding.
- **Primary section:** Stairs, Corridors and Doors
- **Cross-chapter status:** school-life anecdotes serve architecture only; general
  pupil life and portraits are deferred

### C6-04 — Some architecture enforces rules rather than merely enclosing them

- **Historical conclusion:** At least one Hogwarts staircase responds to the
  user's eligibility under an inherited dormitory rule, transforming into a
  slide when a boy attempts to enter the girls' rooms. This shows institutional
  judgement embedded in architecture, while the evidence remains too narrow to
  describe a castle-wide access system.
- **Classification:** established rule plus later-observed enforcement
- **Event chronology:** rationale attributed to the founders; operation observed in 1995
- **Knowledge access:** rule explicitly associated with *Hogwarts: A History*;
  physical demonstration is later confirmation
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** Applies directly only to the observed Gryffindor staircase;
  the current mechanism's construction date and other Houses' arrangements are unknown.
- **Primary section:** Stairs, Corridors and Doors

### C6-05 — The Room of Requirement answers need, not a permanent room programme

- **Historical conclusion:** A room inside Hogwarts can appear only under
  conditions of genuine need, provide a configuration suited to that need, and
  later be absent or differently configured. Dumbledore's unknown chamber-pot
  room is best treated as an earlier encounter with this behaviour; later
  evidence names the Room of Requirement and directly observes its access and
  transformation.
- **Classification:** later discovery and identification of an older castle feature
- **Event chronology:** room origin unknown; uses observed before or during 1994 and in 1995
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition only
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** Dumbledore does not name the room in his anecdote; Dobby is the
  main source for the operating rule; one defensive configuration is not permanent.
- **Primary section:** Rooms That Do Not Behave Like Rooms
- **Cross-chapter status:** D.A. teaching and politics are omitted; the room is
  included only as architecture

### C6-06 — Responsive space defeats both imprecise searching and complete mapping

- **Historical conclusion:** Hogwarts is not unmappable in every sense: the
  Marauder's Map represents the castle, occupants, and secret passages in
  remarkable detail. Its limits are equally important. A seeker who cannot
  specify the needed Room of Requirement configuration may fail to enter it,
  and an active configuration can disappear from the map. The contrast supports
  a bounded conclusion that no ordinary fixed plan exhausts the castle.
- **Classification:** established observations plus bounded editorial interpretation
- **Event chronology:** map capacity observed in 1993 and room limits observed in
  1997; the selected map entry does not establish its creation date
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition; precise map history is not required
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** The room's Unplottable status is a reasoned character
  explanation; the evidence does not prove that every room or configuration is absent from every map.
- **Primary section:** Rooms That Do Not Behave Like Rooms
- **Cross-chapter status:** mapmakers, surveillance, rule-breaking, and external
  passages are deferred

  contained objects hidden by generations, while a later official retrospective
  places the Mirror of Erised there for approximately a century. The room

  catalogue, chronology, or account of each user's intention.
- **Classification:** later observation corroborated by official retrospective
- **Event chronology:** multi-generational accumulation; Mirror storage begins

- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition only
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`

  dates are unknown; individual objects must not be overinterpreted.
- **Primary section:** What the Castle Preserves

### C6-08 — Hogwarts was built in layers rather than completed once

- **Historical conclusion:** The clearest dated architectural sequence joins an
  older concealed Chamber approach to eighteenth-century plumbing: new work
  threatened the original entrance, and a later inhabitant secretly adapted it
  beneath the new bathroom. A separate modern example shows an existing
  little-used classroom refitted into a forest-like environment for a new user.
  Together they establish accumulation and adaptation without implying a known
  renovation programme.
- **Classification:** later retrospective history plus later observed adaptation
- **Event chronology:** founder-era route; eighteenth-century overlay; 1996 room refitting
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial addition only
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** Chapter 5 owns the Chamber narrative; no exact plumbing year,
  plan, workforce, or magical method is known. Classroom eleven's change may be
  temporary and is not proof of autonomous transformation.
- **Primary section:** A Castle Built in Layers
- **Cross-chapter status:** included for architectural change only; Chamber plot,
  education, and headmaster policy are deferred

### C6-09 — The castle is a historical source whose evidence is spatial and incomplete

- **Historical conclusion:** Hogwarts preserves history materially: public
  enchantments still perform; routes retain conditional habits; rules operate
  through thresholds; hidden rooms answer later needs; forgotten objects gather;
  and newer work covers or adapts older structures. This is a bounded editorial
  synthesis, not a claim that the castle is conscious or that every layer can be dated.
- **Classification:** bounded editorial inference
- **Event chronology:** accumulated across undated and dated phases
- **Knowledge access:** unified-edition interpretation grounded in named evidence
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`
- **Limitations:** The synthesis cannot reconstruct a complete architectural
  chronology, original plan, or universal mechanism.
- **Primary section:** What the Castle Preserves
- **Cross-chapter status:** concludes the interior argument and opens naturally
  towards the wider physical setting reserved for Chapter 7

## Verification items

No unresolved verification item blocks Revision 01. Reviewed source
relationships and limits are resolved as follows:

- `ext-b01-003` and `ext-b03-001` are conceptual duplicates from separate
  authority-D preservation transcripts. The synthesis uses the first as later
  editorial commentary and the second only as independent corroboration; neither
  becomes in-universe evidence.
- `gof-ch23-004` does not name the Room of Requirement. Its relationship to the
  later named room is established by `ootp-ch18-005`, which connects Dumbledore's
  anecdote to the Come and Go Room. The earlier passage is described as an
  encounter later identified, not as contemporary knowledge of the name.
- `poa-ch10-003` establishes extensive mapping but not completeness.
  `hbp-ch21-004` supplies the observed blind spot and preserves the uncertainty
  around the proposed Unplottable explanation.
- `ext-a01-002` repeats a Chapter 5 fact for a different historical function.
  The outline restricts it to architectural layering and forbids retelling the
  Chamber narrative.
