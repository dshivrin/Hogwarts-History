## Chapter argument

Hogwarts Castle became an environment whose magic operates through space
itself. Its public architecture can reproduce the world beyond its walls; its
routes and thresholds depend on time, memory, touch, manners, identity, or
need; and some spaces resist both ordinary search and sophisticated mapping.
These features did not necessarily arrive together or originate with the
founders. Dated later alteration and deliberate refitting show a castle built
in layers, while hidden accumulation shows that use itself leaves an archive.
Hogwarts is therefore not a monument frozen at foundation but a historical
source whose evidence survives in movement, concealment, adaptation, and habit.

## Opening movement

Begin exactly where Chapter 5 ends: the founders are gone, but the castle has
kept both their visible institution and work later inhabitants could not fully
see. Move at once from the Chamber as one example to the general problem of
describing Hogwarts architecture. Introduce the Great Hall ceiling early as the
strongest public example and the rare feature explicitly tied to *Hogwarts: A
History*. Do not reopen Slytherin's conflict or narrate the Chamber.

### IV. A Castle Built in Layers

- **The purpose of this section is to show:** that Hogwarts accumulated changes

- **Strongest evidence IDs:** `ext-a01-002`, `ootp-ch27-001`.
- **Chronological notes:** founder-era concealed access precedes eighteenth-century

- **Later-evidence handling:** both are later editorial evidence. The Chamber

- **Narrative movement:** old route threatened by new construction → secret
  adaptation preserves function under a changed surface → much later existing

- **Explicitly deferred:** Corvinus Gaunt genealogy, Chamber crisis, plumbing
  survey, Firenze's teaching, Dumbledore's governance, accessibility policy, and
  invented builders or dates.
- **Overlap risk:** the section must add the new concept of architectural layers,
  not repeat Chapter 5's concealment argument or begin Chapter 9's education history.
- **Authorship layer:** later editorial addition.

### V. What the Castle Preserves

- **The purpose of this section is to show:** how hidden and responsive
  architecture turns Hogwarts itself into a peculiar historical record.
- **Main historical claims:** C6-07 and C6-09.
- **Strongest evidence IDs:** `hbp-ch24-005`, `ext-a14-003`, supported by brief
  recalls of `ps-ch07-001`, `ps-ch08-001`, `ootp-ch17-002`, `ootp-ch18-005`,
  and `ext-a01-002` without re-proving them.
- **Chronological notes:** the Mirror's approximate century of concealment gives
  one bounded pre-Harry chronology; “objects hidden by generations” establishes
  accumulation without dating the first deposit.
- **Later-evidence handling:** both storage sources are later evidence. Preserve
  approximate dating and do not assign individual objects a history not supplied
  by the source.
- **Narrative movement:** hidden room as unintended archive → durable public
  enchantment, learned routes, active rules, forgotten deposits, and layered
  access as different kinds of material memory → complete the argument that the
  castle accumulated rather than remained frozen.
- **Explicitly deferred:** histories of the Mirror, diadem, Horcruxes, banned
  books, contraband, cabinet, ghosts, portraits, and library preservation.
- **Overlap risk:** Chapter 12 owns conscious memory through portraits and ghosts;
  Chapter 11 owns preserved written knowledge. This section concerns spatial and
  material traces only.
- **Closing movement:** allow the interior argument to open outward. The castle's
  walls do not exhaust the place whose history they contain; its towers, doors,
  and passages meet a wider magical landscape. Do not announce the next chapter.
- **Authorship layer:** later editorial addition for the storage evidence,
  followed by unified-edition historical synthesis.

## Historical progression

Founder-era conflict leaves a surviving castle → the Great Hall makes magical
architecture publicly visible → everyday circulation shows conditional routes
and thresholds → responsive rooms expose the limits of search and mapping →
dated alteration proves layered construction → concealed accumulation reveals
the castle as a material archive → attention widens naturally beyond the walls.
