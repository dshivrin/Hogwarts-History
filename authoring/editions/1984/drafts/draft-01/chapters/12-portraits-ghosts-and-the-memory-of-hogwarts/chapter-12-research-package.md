# Chapter 12 Research Package — The Living Memory of Hogwarts

## Status and scope

This is the controlling research package, not the Chapter 12 manuscript.
Revision 01 has now been drafted and audited from the approved formal outline;
explicit final editorial approval remains pending. The working title *The
Living Memory of Hogwarts* is retained for the manuscript, while project
control calls the chapter *Portraits, Ghosts and the Memory of Hogwarts*.

The package tests the question: **How can the past remain present inside
Hogwarts, and what kind of knowledge can those surviving forms preserve or
communicate?** It keeps portraits, headmaster portraits, ghosts, the Sorting
Hat, and Pensieve memories separate. It does not treat responsiveness as proof
of consciousness, identity, a soul, or literal resurrection.

Retrieval used the permanent indexed query tool, tag and text searches of the
full canonical corpus, inspection of the canonical source YAML, and narrow
source checks where the brief identified a specific passage not represented by
a dedicated evidence entry. The two resulting source gaps were closed by
`ext-a17-005` and `beedle-ch04-007`; no broader research-layer extraction was
performed. The principal
tags included `ghosts`, `house-ghost`, `ghost-lore`, `ghost-society`,
`ghost-teacher`, `portraits`, `magical-portraits`, `portrait-limits`,
`portrait-mobility`, `portrait-network`, `headmaster-portraits`,
`institutional-memory`, `sorting-hat`, `pensieve`, `pensieve-memory`,
`memory-magic`, and `memory-modification`. Named-person searches covered the
Fat Lady, Sir Cadogan, Phineas Nigellus Black, Nearly Headless Nick, the Fat
Friar, the Bloody Baron, the Grey Lady/Helena Ravenclaw, Moaning Myrtle, and
Professor Binns.

### Chronology notation

- **P84-direct** — the event or condition occurred by 1984, although the
  surviving carrier may be later.
- **Later-to-earlier** — a post-1984 source explicitly reports an earlier
  event, object, or condition; the earlier proposition may enter only as an
  attributable later discovery unless independent Bagshot access is shown.
- **Later-only** — evidence of a post-1984 event or practice that cannot be
  projected backwards.
- **Uncertain** — the source does not securely date the relevant condition.

“1984 use” below means possible use in reconstructed Bagshot narration. A
qualified “yes” still requires attribution matching the stated source access.
“Unified use” means use in an identifiable later editorial layer.

## B. Evidence gaps

The following questions remain unanswered by the current corpus. None should be
filled by inference.

### Portraits and headmaster portraits

1. When were magical portraits first installed at Hogwarts?
2. When did the former-head portrait practice begin?
3. Did every head receive an office portrait, or could a portrait be omitted?
   `ext-a05-003` says “traditionally,” not universally.
4. Who commissions, paints, installs, or activates a head's portrait?
5. How does the pre-death training process relate to Dumbledore's immediate
   appearance after death in `hbp-ch29-005`?
6. Can a portrait learn and permanently retain new information after the
   subject's death, or does it merely observe, report, and use preloaded
   memories? The corpus demonstrates later observation but does not define
   durable learning.
7. Can ordinary portraits communicate through all paintings, only connected
   Hogwarts paintings, or a narrower social network?
8. How are House portrait guardians normally selected, dismissed, or replaced?
9. Are the former heads seen socialising near Gryffindor Tower in
   `poa-ch11-006` the same trained office portraits?
10. Who besides the current head may formally consult former-head portraits?
11. Do portraits possess private memories not deliberately imparted by their
    subjects?
12. Is any portrait's advice binding, recorded, or subject to institutional
    procedure? No evidence supports a formal council.

### Ghosts

13. When did each named ghost begin residing at Hogwarts?
14. When did the four-House ghost arrangement begin, and how were the ghosts
    chosen or accepted?
15. How long has Nick served as a guide or Gryffindor-associated ghost?
16. When did Binns die, and how long had he taught before and after death?
17. Is Binns still formally employed, paid, appointed, or merely permitted to
    continue? The sources show function, not administrative status.
18. How accurately do ghosts remember over centuries, and can their memories
    change?
19. What can ghosts perceive when passing through walls, pipes, or occupied
    rooms, and what limits their observation?
20. Can ghosts deliberately manipulate physical objects? The corpus shows
    interaction with space and exceptional petrification, not a general rule.
21. Does Helena's long silence reflect incapacity, choice, shame, or House role?

### Sorting Hat

22. What independent evidence, if any, corroborates the legend that the Hat
    belonged to Gryffindor and was jointly enchanted?
23. What does “contains the intelligence of the four founders” technically
    mean? The source does not define identity, consciousness, or a complete
    transfer of memory.
24. How does the Hat create a new song each year?
25. How does it learn about current danger or compare present pupils with prior
    generations?
26. Does it retain individual memories of every Sorting, or only judgemental
    capacity and general knowledge?
27. How old is the physical hat, as distinct from the age of the tradition
    about it?
28. The indexed `ps-ch07-002` entry does not capture the Hat's private dialogue
    with Harry. A controlled extraction is needed before that scene supports a
    detailed claim about mind-to-mind response.

### Pensieve and memories

29. When did Hogwarts acquire or take ownership of its Pensieve?
30. When did successive heads begin leaving memories in it?
31. Which heads deposited memories, under what selection rule, and whether any
    deposits were mandatory remain unknown.
32. What memories were present by 1984?
33. Could Bagshot, pupils, staff, governors, or historians ever consult the
    school Pensieve, or was access limited to the incumbent head?
34. How are memories catalogued, identified, authenticated, or protected?
35. `ext-a17-005` says the Pensieve faithfully recreates details stored in the
    subconscious, but does not explain what was never retained, how interested
    perspective affects the result, or how deliberate alteration interacts
    with that reconstruction.
36. Can an altered memory be restored by the Pensieve itself, or only replaced
    by obtaining a less altered source from the witness? The Slughorn sequence
    supports the latter case only.
37. The date of the runes, their engraver, their function, and their relation to
    the object's manufacture are unknown. `ext-a17-002` dates the artefact as a
    whole, not the engraving separately.
38. `beedle-ch04-007` distinguishes photographs, portraits, and ghosts, but it
    does not explain how any of the three is created or define the metaphysical
    status of a speaking likeness or ghost.

## C. Contradictions and ambiguities

### 1. “Dumbledore's Pensieve” versus the school's Pensieve

- `gof-ch30-002`, `ootp-ch24-004`, and `ootp-ch28-006` use Dumbledore-centred
  language and show his personal control of the object.
- `ext-a17-001` explicitly says the Hogwarts Pensieve belongs to the school,
  not an individual.
- These are not necessarily contradictory: narrative possessive language may
  indicate custody or use. The earlier novel scenes alone do not settle title,
  while the institutional ownership claim is a 2015 retrospective source.

### 2. Ordinary portraits versus trained head portraits

- `ext-a05-002` describes ordinary portraits as artist-limited,
  two-dimensional representations.
- `ext-a05-003` describes pre-death training and deliberate transfer of useful
  memories to a head's portrait.
- The sources support different portrait classes or degrees of preparation,
  not a contradiction and not one rule for all images.

### 3. Portrait mobility inside and outside Hogwarts

- `ext-a05-001`, `gof-ch17-007`, and `dh-ch33-002` support movement among
  pictures within Hogwarts.
- `ootp-ch22-002`, `ootp-ch23-002`, and `dh-ch15-007` limit travel outside the
  castle to another portrait of the same subject.
- “Portrait network” must therefore be used carefully: communication among
  castle paintings is broader than a subject's external travel.

### 4. Portrait observation versus portrait knowledge

- `ext-a05-004` and `dh-ch33-002` show that portraits can listen or go to watch.
- `ext-a05-002` limits ordinary portraits' retained depth; `dh-ch16-002` shows
  a reporter's bias and selective disclosure.
- Seeing or hearing a new event does not by itself prove permanent learning,
  complete comprehension, neutrality, or access to the living subject's mind.

### 5. Immediate portrait appearance versus pre-death training

- `ext-a05-003` says a head's portrait is painted and trained before death.
- `hbp-ch29-005` shows Dumbledore appearing among the dead heads immediately
  after death.
- The accounts are compatible if the prepared portrait becomes publicly active
  at death, but the activation mechanism is not stated and must not be invented.

### 6. Ghost as surviving witness versus ghost as incomplete life

- `dh-ch31-002` presents Helena as the same first-person witness to founder-era
  events.
- `ootp-ch38-006` calls ghosthood an imprint or imitation rather than full life
  and denies Nick complete knowledge of death.
- The safest formulation is behavioural and evidentiary: ghosts retain personal
  identity claims and memories, but do not constitute restored embodied life or
  perfect testimony.

### 7. The Hat's own account versus the legend of its origin

- `gof-ch12-002` and `ootp-ch11-003` record claims in the Hat's songs.
- `ext-a02-001` explicitly frames Gryffindor's ownership and joint enchantment
  as legend.
- The chapter may say that school tradition presents an intentional transfer of
  founder judgement. It should not promote the precise genesis into an
  independently documented fact.

### 8. Ancient artefact, ancient runes, and Hogwarts ownership

- `ext-a17-002` calls the Pensieve older than Hogwarts and notes modified Saxon
  runes.
- `ext-a17-001` says it belongs to the school; `ext-a17-003` gives an
  unsubstantiated founder-discovery legend.
- The evidence does not date the runes separately, identify a maker, date
  acquisition, or prove ownership from the founding.

### 9. Pensieve detail versus memory fallibility

- `ext-a17-005` claims faithful reconstruction from subconscious detail and
  free movement inside a memory.
- `hbp-ch13-002`, `hbp-ch20-001`, and `hbp-ch20-006` show interested testimony,
  suppression, alteration, and the need for inference.
- The sources support extraordinary access to preserved memory, not an
  infallible objective recording. The exact relationship between subconscious
  detail and deliberate alteration remains unexplained.

### 10. Primary-timeline and altered-timeline portrait testimony

- `cc-p1-a2-s08-002` and `cc-p1-a2-s10-002` give useful formulations of
  portrait limitation but occur in an altered branch.
- A05 supplies primary, official explanatory support for the same distinction.
- The altered-timeline claims should not carry the argument and can be omitted
  without loss.

## E. Editorial recommendation

### 1. Is the proposed scope supported?

Yes, with one adjustment of emphasis. The evidence strongly supports a chapter
about **plural memory systems and their limits**, not a general survey of
supernatural residents. The proposed central argument survives review:
Hogwarts preserves its past in overlapping forms, some responsive, none
complete. A17 now permits a stronger later-edition statement than the brief
initially assumed: the Hogwarts Pensieve is explicitly described as a
school-owned, headmasterial memory library. That statement must remain a later
editorial addition.

### 2. Strongest sections

1. **Portrait mechanism and limits** — A05 gives a rare explicit technical
   distinction between ordinary and trained head portraits, while later scenes
   demonstrate movement, observation, message carrying, and bias.
2. **Headmaster portraits as succession support** — the evidence supports a
   precise institutional function without requiring the overclaim of a council.
3. **Pensieve as historical method** — Dumbledore's inquiry supplies unusually
   clear evidence for collection, comparison, missing sources, inference, and
   altered testimony.
4. **Helena Ravenclaw** — the sharpest example of a first-hand witness surviving
   from the founders' world, paired with equally sharp reliability limits.
5. **Comparative limits** — the corpus supports a useful reader-facing
   distinction among witness, likeness, judgement, and memory.

### 3. Sections requiring later evidence

1. Pre-1984 institutional use of ordinary portraits is weak. Most useful
   observed behaviour is from the 1990s.
2. Ghost residence and House association lack start dates. Individual antiquity
   cannot be substituted for institutional continuity.
3. Binns is compelling but chronologically underdetermined; his death and tenure
   cannot anchor a 1984 narrative without new evidence.
4. The Hat's warning function is supported only by later example and Nick's
   undated recollection; the learning mechanism remains unknown.

### 4. Tempting claims to exclude

- Portraits are the people they depict.
- Headmaster portraits possess souls, consciousness identical to their
  subjects, or binding authority.
- Every former head has a portrait or every portrait was trained.
- The portraits form a governing council.
- Portraits can travel to any picture anywhere.
- Portraits automatically and permanently learn everything that happens after
  death.
- House ghosts have served their Houses since the founding.
- Helena gives a complete or independently verified history of Ravenclaw.
- Binns proves that all staff offices can continue unchanged after death.
- The Sorting Hat contains the founders themselves or infallibly records every
  prior Sorting.
- The Hat's later warning songs were known to Bagshot in their exact form.
- A Pensieve memory is an objective recording independent of a rememberer.
- The Pensieve automatically repairs altered memory.
- Dumbledore's private lessons prove general pupil access.
- The Pensieve's runes date its maker, their engraving, or their function.
- The founders' discovery legend proves Hogwarts possessed the Pensieve from
  the first day.
- Responsive remnants amount to resurrection.

### 5. Recommended conceptual movement

**Opening:** Begin exactly where Chapter 11 ends. Writing allows knowledge to
wait without its possessor. Chapter 12 should then introduce the stranger case
in which a question meets something that reacts. The first two paragraphs
should immediately separate the mechanisms so that the rest of the chapter can
show consequences rather than list marvels.

**Middle:** Move from the closest thing to a surviving person (ghosts), through
constructed likenesses (ordinary portraits), to deliberate institutional
succession (head portraits), then to inherited judgement (the Hat), and finally
to preserved experience examined as evidence (Pensieve memories). This is a
conceptual progression, not a claim of increasing magical sophistication.

**Closing:** Conclude that responsiveness gives Hogwarts continuity without
giving it perfect knowledge. Then pivot to Chapter 13: some surviving forms do
not merely remember the institution but take part in its repeated ceremonies.
The Hat's annual song is the narrow bridge; the full Sorting ceremony, feasts,
awards, and recurring customs remain for the next chapter.
