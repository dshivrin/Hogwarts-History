## Chapter argument

Hogwarts did not preserve its past in a single archive. Its history survived
in overlapping forms: dead witnesses, enchanted likenesses, representations
trained for succession, a founder-associated object that still judged new
pupils, and memories that could be entered and examined. Some of these forms
could answer questions or react to present events, giving the school unusual
continuity. None supplied complete, neutral, or infallible knowledge of the
past.

## Opening

Begin from Chapter 11's final movement: writing allows knowledge to outlive its
possessor, but Hogwarts also contains forms of the past that can answer,
appear, or surround an investigator. Establish immediately that responsiveness
does not make the mechanisms equivalent. This opening is a bounded later
editorial synthesis, not reconstructed 1984 testimony about a unified magical
theory.
