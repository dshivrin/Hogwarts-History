## Chapter argument

Hogwarts extended beyond its walls into a landscape that the institution used,
protected and repeatedly altered. The first-year route joined lake, cliff,
tunnel and castle; cultivated ground supported teaching, work, recreation and
sport; and gates and enchantments controlled access. Yet the lake and Forest
also held communities and populations whose relationship to Hogwarts ranged
from maintenance to tolerance, negotiation, accidental introduction and open
autonomy. The school occupied a landscape without exercising the same authority
over every part of it.

## Candidate opening logic

Do not begin with a catalogue or panoramic tour. Continue Chapter 6’s final
sentence by placing the reader in the first-year boats. The best opening fact is
the route itself: castle above water, boats entering cliff, tunnel beneath the
castle, underground harbour, ascent through rock, emergence onto grass. It
turns Chapter 6’s boundary between interior and exterior into a physical
continuum and establishes the chapter’s method—relationships first, map claims
only where supported.

## Candidate closing logic

The closing paragraph should not merely restate that the grounds are varied.
It should distinguish three demonstrated relationships: space Hogwarts directly
uses and maintains; space it protects or regulates; and inhabited space where
other communities assert their own authority. The Whomping Willow, anti-
Apparition perimeter and centaur claims together show that institutional reach
is real but uneven. That conclusion naturally hands off to Chapter 8’s subject:
who may exercise authority at Hogwarts, through what office, and with what
limits—without explicitly announcing the next chapter.
