## Chapter purpose

Trace how Hogwarts extends beyond the castle into a landscape of water, open
ground, work sites, roads, gates, habitats, and dangerous margins. The chapter
must explain how the school uses and shapes that landscape while preserving the
evidentiary distinction between school-managed space and communities or
populations that are not shown to be school property.

## Central historical question

What kind of place surrounded Hogwarts Castle, and how did that landscape
become part of the institution itself?

## Provisional argument to test

Hogwarts did not end at its walls. The lake, lawns, paths, outbuildings and
Forest formed another institutional layer: partly cultivated, partly regulated,
and partly outside complete school control. The evidence supports this argument
if “grounds” is not allowed to imply undivided ownership or mastery.

## Entry and exit

- **Entry:** continue Chapter 6’s final outward movement. Open with the
  first-year boats seeing the castle above the lake, entering the cliff, passing
  beneath the castle, and emerging onto grass. Do not restart the foundation or
  repeat the interior-architecture argument.
- **Exit:** end with a landscape Hogwarts occupies, protects, modifies and uses
  but does not wholly command. The natural hand-off is to Chapter 8’s question
  of governance and the reach of institutional authority.

## Required subjects

- directly supported topography and approaches;
- the lake as route, habitat, recreation space and exceptional public venue;
- lawns, paths, greenhouses, vegetable patch, Hagrid’s hut and Quidditch pitch
  as working school space;
- the Forbidden Forest as a restricted, inhabited and only partly managed edge;
- centaur and merpeople autonomy;
- thestrals as a school-maintained herd, contrasted with independent or
  introduced populations;
- gates, concealment, anti-Apparition limits and later wartime hardening;
- dated or bounded changes including the Whomping Willow and tournament maze.

## Explicit exclusions

- castle interiors except the lake-to-door transition;
- general histories of Quidditch, subjects, Hogsmeade, the Triwizard
  Tournament, school discipline, Hagrid, centaurs, or the wars;
- creature lists unsupported by a Hogwarts connection;
- fan-map bearings, distances, dimensions or inferred ownership;
- founder-era origins for undated landscape customs or structures.

## Presentation model

Use the unified narrative model. Explicit *Hogwarts: A History* facts and
well-bounded pre-1984 history may enter reconstructed Bagshot narration. Harry-
era observations, official retrospectives, *Cursed Child*, wartime changes and
post-1984 events remain identifiable editorial evidence. No separate addendum is
presently needed; later material can be integrated through explicit temporal
pivots.
