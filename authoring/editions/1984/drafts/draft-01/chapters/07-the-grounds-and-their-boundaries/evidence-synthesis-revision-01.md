## Executive finding

The evidence supports a coherent history of Hogwarts as a landscape, but not a
complete map or an estate survey. The strongest opening evidence is the
first-year approach: pupils see the castle high above the lake, cross by boat,
pass through an ivy-hidden opening in the cliff and a tunnel beneath the castle,
land at an underground harbour, climb through rock and emerge onto grass before
reaching the front door. Water, terrain, engineered passage and ceremony are
therefore joined in a single institutional route. Its age, however, is unknown.

Beyond that approach, the grounds divide into spaces with different functions
and different degrees of school authority. Lawns, paths, greenhouses, a
vegetable patch, the gamekeeper’s dwelling and the Quidditch pitch are observed
as working school space: pupils cross them, classes use them, staff supervise
them, and the pitch can be temporarily rebuilt for another purpose. The lake is
both school setting and habitat. It supports ordinary recreation, dangerous
creatures and an organised merpeople community whose cooperation with Hogwarts
is demonstrated in 1995 but whose subordination is not. A later official
description calls the wider grounds partly a nature reserve; this is a useful
editorial interpretation, not a dated legal status.

The Forbidden Forest supplies the chapter’s essential limit. Hogwarts forbids
ordinary pupil entry, uses its edge for teaching and can send a gamekeeper or
staff into it. Paths, clearings and supervised search procedures show managed
access. They do not prove comprehensive possession. The centaurs state their own
law, reject human service and demonstrate that a headmistress’s title has no
practical force in their territory. Other inhabitants stand in different
relations to the school: Hogwarts maintains thestrals for transport; unicorns
are encountered as independent wildlife; and Hagrid’s introduction of Aragog
produces a colony that eventually exceeds even his safe control.

The provisional thesis therefore survives with one refinement: Hogwarts used,
regulated, protected and repeatedly altered its surroundings, but “the grounds”
must not be written as a single uniformly owned or mastered domain. The chapter
can move historically from integrated approach, through cultivated working
space, to independent habitats and contested authority, then close on the
boundary between institutional reach and neighbouring autonomy.

## Spatial findings

### Certain

- The castle stands high above the lake; the first-year boats move through a
  cliff opening and tunnel beneath it to an underground harbour.
- The harbour route rises through rock, emerges onto grass and continues to
  steps and the oak front door.
- Returning pupils can travel from Hogsmeade station by carriage through
  winged-boar gates to the castle.
- Greenhouses, vegetable patch and lawns lie outside the main castle; students
  cross the vegetable patch to reach the greenhouses.
- Hagrid’s dwelling is at the Forest edge; Forest paths lead from that edge into
  deeper, denser ground.
- A lawn at the Forest edge can be used for Care of Magical Creatures work.
- The Quidditch pitch has stands and a locker room and can be reached from the
  castle grounds.
- The anti-Apparition protection covers the grounds; beyond the gates is an
  observed point where Disapparition becomes possible.

### Probable

- “Underground harbour” is beneath or within the castle’s rocky base, but its
  full engineering and precise footprint are unknown.
- The greenhouses, lawns and vegetable patch form a cultivated zone between or
  around castle and Forest, but their orientation is not recoverable.
- The gates mark a practical boundary on the Hogsmeade approach, though they may
  not define the entire perimeter.

### Uncertain

- Compass directions, exact distances, acreages and full perimeter.
- Whether all or only part of the Forest lies within land legally owned by the
  school.
- Whether the lake’s entire shoreline lies within one administrative boundary.
- Exact positions of pitch, hut and greenhouses relative to each other.
- Whether later references to mountain caves describe terrain inside Hogwarts
  grounds.

## Historical changes

1. During Hagrid’s school years, Aragog was removed from the castle and placed
   in the Forest; with Mosag he founded a colony (`cos-ch15-006`).
2. In Remus Lupin’s arrival year, the Whomping Willow was planted; after a pupil
   injury, approaching it was forbidden (`poa-ch10-002`).
3. By the Harry era, differentiated greenhouses, a maintained thestral herd and
   developed pitch infrastructure were present, but their dates are unknown.
4. In 1995, the Quidditch field was converted into a tall, enchanted maze for
   the third Triwizard task (`gof-ch28-004`, `gof-ch31-002`, `gof-ch31-005`).
5. By 1996, Aragog’s death ended Hagrid’s protected access to the Acromantula
   colony (`hbp-ch22-005`).
6. In 1996, the gates were chained and bewitched and wall protections were
   tightened during wartime (`hbp-ch08-002`).
7. Later testimony claims post-Battle recognition of centaur land and reports
   negotiation with merpeople over a lake search; both remain deferred
   (`cc-p1-a2-s05-001`, `cc-p2-a3-s11-001`).

  evidence is genuinely core: concealment, established protective principles
  and centaur presence—not later mechanisms or encounters.
- Class B facts belong to later editorial attribution unless independent access
  for Bagshot is shown.
- Class C observations prove existence in the Harry era, not origin or timeless
  continuity.
- Class D material must be presented through explicit temporal pivots.
- A09 and A04 are authority-A official retrospectives, but their publication
  dates still bar silent conversion into Bagshot knowledge.
- *Fantastic Beasts* supplies species context only after Hogwarts residence is
  independently established.
- *Cursed Child* entries are primary-timeline later testimony, not pre-1984
  evidence.

## Contradictions and ambiguities

- “Great Lake,” “black lake” and “the lake” must not be silently harmonised into
  a demonstrably ancient proper name.
- Human speakers’ possessive language about centaurs conflicts with centaur
  assertions of separate law and territory; retain the conflict.
- School restriction, school use and school ownership are separate propositions.
- A physical gate boundary and a magical anti-Apparition boundary coincide in
  one later chase but are not proven identical everywhere.
- The Forest contains school-maintained thestrals, an introduced Acromantula
  colony and autonomous centaurs; one management category cannot cover all.
