# Chapter 4 Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

## Founder-era chronology

- No reviewed source dates the creation of the Houses more precisely than the
  broad founder era.
- The evidence does not establish whether the four Houses existed from the
  school's first day, emerged after teaching began, or became formal in stages.
- No source identifies which founder proposed dividing pupils, whether the
  arrangement was negotiated as a formal settlement, or whether one founder
  “created” the House system.
- Hat tradition says living founders divided or took preferred pupils, but it
  does not prove that each founder personally selected every early pupil.
- The precise relation between founder selection and the Book and Quill is not
  recorded. Chapter 3's distinction between admission to Hogwarts and House
  assignment remains the governing boundary.

## Sorting Hat origin and operation

- Gryffindor's ownership is explicitly presented as legend in `ext-a02-001`.
- All-four-founder participation in the Hat's enchantment is multiply preserved
  within the same broad tradition but lacks an independent founder-era record.
- No reviewed evidence dates the enchantment, identifies the first Hat-sorted
  cohort, or explains whether the Hat began work before or only after all four
  founders had ceased selecting pupils.
- The phrase “the founders put some brains in me” and the later description of
  embedded founder intelligence do not explain the magic's technical process.
- The evidence establishes mental assessment, difficult decisions, and
  responsiveness to thoughts, but not a complete rule among current traits,
  admired traits, values, potential, and choice.
- Pupil preference is consequential but not controlling: later testimony says
  Harry's feelings mattered, while Neville's request did not determine his
  House. The boundary between influence and decision remains unknown.
- The primary Harry Sorting exchange was directly checked in the novel PDF but
  lacks an atomic canonical evidence entry. This is a research-layer extraction
  gap; it has not been repaired during authoring.
- No evidence establishes a historical appeals procedure, re-Sorting process,
  or formal review of alleged mistakes.

## Founder ideals and later stereotypes

- The Hat's songs use different vocabulary in different years. The variations
  can broaden an ideal but should not be harmonised into an invented formal
  rubric.
- Hufflepuff's esteem for hard work and broad willingness to teach can coexist,
  but the sources do not record how she applied both principles to actual cases.
- Ravenclaw's intellectual preference does not establish teaching methods,
  curriculum, or a disdain for other abilities.
- Gryffindor's bravery tradition does not establish that every Gryffindor was
  brave at eleven or remained brave thereafter.
- Slytherin's ambition and cunning must remain distinct from his ancestry
  restriction. The sources do not establish that every later Slytherin adopted
  blood-status politics.
- Apparent later mismatch between conduct and House stereotype does not by
  itself prove a Sorting error.
- A preserved post-series interview claims that Slytherin later became less of
  a pure-blood bastion, but the claim has no selected independent corroboration,
  dates, or enrolment data and remains deferred.

## Mature House institution

- The modern school securely documents common rooms, dormitories, House tables,
  House-shaped class groupings, Heads of House, points, the House Cup, House
  ghosts, prefects, and inter-House Quidditch.
- None of the selected evidence securely dates those features to the founder
  era. They must be described as mature or later documented practice.
- No selected source establishes when House-specific common rooms or
  dormitories were created, whether early pupils were residentially separated,
  or whether their present locations are original.
- No selected source dates the offices of Head of House or prefect or proves
  their original duties. One pre-1984 memory places Lucius Malfoy as a prefect,
  but that does not supply an institutional history.
- No selected source dates the point system, visible hourglasses, House Cup,
  House ghosts, House tables, or House Quidditch teams.
- Cross-House classes prove that Houses do not constitute total academic
  separation in the mature school, but the full scheduling logic and its age
  remain unknown.
- House ghosts demonstrate symbolic reach but not original purpose or selection.

## Identity, family, and rivalry

- Family House patterns and expectations are supported by anecdotes, not a
  complete demographic record.
- No evidence supports biological or magical inheritance of House assignment.
- No evidence dates the beginning of family expectations or identifies the
  earliest multi-generation House line.
- Mature competition demonstrably strengthens collective attention and can
  produce hostility, but no selected evidence proves that formal rivalry began
  immediately with the founders.
- The Hat song's claim of founder-era inter-House fighting is poetic testimony
  and belongs primarily to Chapter 5's rupture analysis.
- Later sports songs, badges, and harassment show one developed form of rivalry;
  they must not be universalised across all centuries.

## Knowledge access and source-class limits

- The style lock permits later-recorded Hat songs to represent the long-running
  founder tradition without repeating a continuity argument, but their exact
  post-1984 performances and wording cannot be attributed to the 1984 narrator.
- Official Rowling retrospectives in A02 and A20 are high-authority later
  sources. Their older claims may be integrated only as identifiable later
  editorial additions unless independent 1984-access evidence exists.
- Harry-era observation confirms mature practice but does not by itself prove
  that Bagshot knew the same detail by 1984.
- *Cursed Child* testimony is post-1984 and must remain an attributable later
  addition. Altered-timeline evidence is excluded from the primary historical
  argument.
- Authority-D interview evidence must not carry a major change claim without
  corroboration.

## Tempting but unsupported conclusions

- The Houses were designed in a single meeting as Hogwarts's constitution.
- One founder alone created the House system.
- Every early pupil was personally selected by one founder.
- The Sorting Hat existed and performed the present ceremony from the first day.
- The Hat detects a single fixed essence or inevitable destiny.
- A pupil may freely choose any House.
- House assignment is hereditary.
- Present stereotypes exactly reproduce founder intentions.
- Hufflepuff accepted only pupils rejected as inferior by the other founders.
- Every Slytherin is a pure-blood supremacist or every ambitious pupil belongs in
  Slytherin.
- Common rooms, dormitories, points, Cup, prefects, ghosts, tables, or Quidditch
  teams are founder-created institutions.
- House rivalry began immediately and inevitably with the founders.
