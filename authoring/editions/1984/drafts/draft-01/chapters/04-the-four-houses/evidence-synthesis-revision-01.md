## Chapter Purpose

Explain how founder preferences became durable House identities without
pretending that the mature House institution is fully documented in the
founding era. The chapter begins with one school containing four judgements
about promise, develops the tradition of founder selection and the Hat's
succession role, then shows what House membership demonstrably organized in
the mature school. It ends where Slytherin's ancestry condition ceases to be
merely one preference among four and begins to threaten the common institution.

## Consolidated Claims

### C4-01 — Four preferences became four institutional traditions

- **Claim:** Hogwarts's four Houses bear the founders' names, and continuing
  school tradition explains them as the institutional preservation of four
  different preferences in pupils. The further proposition that Houses were a
  deliberate settlement allowing four educators to coexist is a reasonable
  interpretation of the Hat's account, not an independently documented charter.
- **Classification:** reasonable_inference
- **Evidence class:** directly established naming plus later Hogwarts tradition
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Do not say that one founder invented the system, that a formal
  constitutional bargain survives, or that the mature House institution existed
  complete on Hogwarts's first day.
- **Primary section:** One School, Four Judgements

### C4-02 — The four selection ideals were preferences, not four human species

- **Claim:** Gryffindor tradition prizes bravery, daring, nerve, and chivalry;
  Hufflepuff tradition joins hard work with justice, loyalty, patience, and broad
  willingness to teach; Ravenclaw tradition prizes intelligence, wit, learning,
  and a ready mind; Slytherin tradition prizes ambition, cunning, and shrewdness.
  The sources describe valued qualities, not mutually exclusive biological or
  magical categories.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported

- **Limitations:** Exact formulations vary between songs. Do not convert poetic
