## Chapter purpose

Explain how four founders' differing judgements about promise became four
enduring school communities. The chapter should move from founder selection
preferences, through the attributed tradition of personal selection and the
Sorting Hat's succession role, to the securely documented later institution in
which House membership structures residence, belonging, discipline, reward,
competition, family expectation, and reputation.

## Historical period

The principal historical movement begins in the founder era and follows the
House system into its mature pre-1984 form. Harry-era observations may confirm
later institutional practice but may not be projected unchanged into the
founding. Post-1984 evidence may appear only as an identifiable later editorial
addition or as a recorded gap or deferral.

## Central question

How did four teachers' different preferences become durable institutional
identities capable of surviving the founders themselves?

## Working historical argument

The Sorting Hat's tradition presents the Houses as an arrangement that allowed
four different educational ideals to coexist within one school. Independent
evidence securely establishes the founders, House names, early pupil-seeking,
and the later House institution, but it does not independently prove a formal
founder-era constitutional settlement. The chapter may therefore use the
“one school, four traditions” interpretation as an attributed and bounded
explanation, not as documentary fact.

## Relationship to neighbouring chapters

- **Chapter 2:** inherit the founders' qualities, educational preferences, and
  the distinction between preferred qualities and Slytherin's ancestry
  restriction. Do not repeat biographies, relic histories, or the full
  argument over access.
- **Chapter 3:** begin from the established school and its succession problem.
  Do not repeat construction, concealment, admissions instruments, early
  operation, or the full Book-and-Quill distinction. Develop only the House
  consequence and detailed Hat function that Chapter 3 expressly deferred.
- **Chapter 5:** prepare the pressure created by Slytherin's ancestry condition,
  but stop before fighting, departure, the Chamber tradition, and the rupture's
  consequences.

## Included subjects

- why four Houses exist, at the level the evidence permits;
- the four founder selection philosophies and their limits;
- the tradition that founders selected or divided early pupils personally;
- the Sorting Hat's ownership tradition, joint enchantment, mental assessment,
  difficult decisions, and qualified responsiveness to pupil preference;
- the distinction between valued qualities and mutually exclusive human types;
- House membership as residence, social belonging, staff responsibility,
  collective reward and punishment, and later competition;
- later House reputation, family expectation, and exceptions;
- the chronology problem separating founder intent, early identity, mature
  institution, and later stereotype.

## Explicitly deferred or excluded

- founder biographies and artefact histories already owned by Chapter 2;
- the construction and general foundation history owned by Chapter 3;
- Slytherin's departure, founder fighting, the Chamber, and consequences owned
  by Chapter 5;
- detailed castle architecture, common-room tours, and entrance mechanisms;
- full governance history of Heads of House and prefects;
- general pupil life, curriculum history, and complete disciplinary policy;
- detailed Sorting-ceremony history, annual song performance, and feast ritual
  beyond what is necessary to explain House assignment;
- detailed Quidditch history and match chronology;
- any claim that mature House institutions existed unchanged in the founder era.
