## Strict Chapter 18 reuse check

- 1943: Chapter 18 asks what Dippet could decide. Chapter 19 instead uses the
  Ministry's discussion of closure, the formal exclusion built on a false
  accusation and the demand for a public explanation. The function is
  materially different.
- First Wizarding War: Chapter 18 uses Dumbledore's reputation as evidence of
  authority attached to a head. Chapter 19 uses the same bounded testimony only
  to establish Hogwarts's social meaning as a comparatively safe institution.
- No material concerning Lupin, the Whomping Willow, the Shrieking Shack or an
  accommodation programme appears in the draft or its metadata.

## Book-wide checks

- Names, offices and terms: consistent (`Headmaster`, `Head Girl`, `Head Boy`,
  O.W.L., N.E.W.T., Wizarding Examinations Authority, Ministry).
- Dates and sequence: Statute of Secrecy pressure precedes the nineteenth-
  century railway settlement; Orpington's 1849–1855 ministry precedes 1900;
  Riddle's visit is 1938; the Chamber crisis is 1943; Evans-era contact precedes
  the 1971 intake; Marauders-era examinations and the First Wizarding War occur
  in the 1970s; the television proposal belongs to the early 1980s.
- Knowledge access: later official histories, memories, testimony and 1996
  observation are distinguished from event chronology. No later fact is voiced
  as unmediated reconstructed-1984 knowledge.
- Technology: environmental incompatibility at Hogwarts remains distinct from
  secrecy-based rejection of television elsewhere.
- Thesis: continuity is not turned into a modernisation programme, progressive
  inclusion, bureaucratic growth or increasing Ministry control.
