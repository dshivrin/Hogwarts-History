## Proposed chapter argument

Hogwarts did not cross a single threshold into modernity. Before the twentieth
century began, the ancient school had already absorbed material alteration and
national systems devised for secrecy, transport and access. The modern school
inherited those arrangements rather than inventing them anew.

What the twentieth-century evidence most clearly adds is not a comprehensive
reform programme but the operation of a national institution. Hogwarts could
find and explain itself to children outside established wizarding households,
connect admission to public law and practical support, preserve recognisable
offices and examinations across generations, and face state-level consequence
when an internal emergency threatened the school's continued operation. During
Voldemort's first rise it acquired a reputation as a comparatively safe place,
although the measures behind that reputation are not recoverable.

The castle also remained materially unlike a Muggle modern school. Electrical
and similar technologies failed in its concentrated magical environment even
while wider wizarding Britain selectively adapted Muggle devices. Its old
appearance was therefore neither proof of changelessness nor proof of an
ideological refusal of innovation.

The supported conclusion is qualified continuity with episodic adaptation.
Modern Hogwarts was ancient in form, nationally connected, administratively
recognisable, publicly consequential and materially magical. The evidence
shows changes that mattered, but not steady progress, increasing Ministry
control or a single architect of “modern Hogwarts.”

## Opening: The School Inherited by the Modern Age

  decisions are not themselves a modernisation chronology; Chapter 19 must
  determine what the twentieth-century institution had inherited and what new
  pressures are actually visible.
- **Primary claim:** C19-08 as framing only.
- **Evidence IDs:** no new example; compact synthesis of advancing claims.
- **Authorship layer:** bounded editorial interpretation.
- **Overlap control:** do not recap Chapter 18's headship argument or name its
  sequence of office-holders.

### Paragraph plan

1. Begin from Chapter 18's final distinction: an old institution entered the
   modern period carrying earlier decisions. Turn at once from the people who
   made those decisions to the school that had to operate through them.
2. Reject a false threshold without making the opening a catalogue of caveats.
   “Modern” will mean the conditions under which Hogwarts operated through
   1984, not a claim that every familiar system began in the twentieth century.

### Paragraph plan

3. Establish the chronological correction in one concrete movement. The school

   coordination of pupil travel and after the railway had become compulsory.
4. Add only the dateable outer point: the concealed King's Cross platform was

5. Draw the new consequence rather than repeating Chapter 15: twentieth-century
   Hogwarts began with old ceremonies and architecture already joined to
   national infrastructure. The evidence supports inheritance, not a modern
   founding moment.

## 2. Reaching the Child

- **The purpose of this section is to show:** twentieth-century Hogwarts could
  extend admission, explanation, law, material assistance and correspondence
  into lives not already organised around wizarding institutions.
- **Primary claim:** C19-02.
- **Evidence IDs:** `hbp-ch13-004`, `hbp-ch13-005`, `hbp-ch13-006`,
  `ps-ch04-004`, `dh-ch33-003`, `dh-ch33-004`.
- **Authorship layer:** later-carried pre-1984 memories and recollection.
- **Overlap control:** Chapter 9 owns admissions and Chapter 15 owns the fund's
  economic significance. Do not explain letter wording, book lists, curriculum,
  shopping geography or the Express route.
- **Biographical control:** Tom Riddle and Lily Evans are cases through which
  the school's reach is visible, not subjects of mini-biographies.

### Paragraph plan

6. Move to 1938 through the location rather than the future identity of the
   child: Hogwarts sent a professor into a Muggle orphanage to explain an
   institution of which Tom Riddle had no family knowledge.
7. Separate recognition from initiation. The visit explained that Riddle was a
   wizard, demonstrated the truth of the claim and translated Hogwarts into
   terms a Muggle institution and an unsupported child could act upon. Do not
   narrate Riddle's childhood conduct beyond what the school's response required.
8. Show that admission joined school rule to public law. Theft and harmful
   magic were addressed as conditions of belonging, while expulsion could also
   bring Ministry consequences. Present only the boundary between school and
   state, not a complete discipline code.
9. Add the material route: a Hogwarts fund could pay for necessary books and
   robes, second-hand purchase was acceptable, and instructions plus a ticket
   carried the child from the orphanage towards school. Keep the case narrow;
   no general finance system follows.
10. Compare, without claiming identical procedure, the later Evans-era evidence.
    Lily expected a letter; a child understood that a representative would
    explain Hogwarts to Muggle-born parents; Petunia's exceptional appeal
    received a personal reply. The institution could address the family beyond
    the admitted child, but neither the practice's origin nor a trend towards
    inclusion is known.

### Paragraph plan

11. Introduce the evidentiary problem positively: modern Hogwarts possessed
    recognisable machinery, but the sources usually show a person passing
    through it rather than the machinery being created.
12. Use McGonagall and Riddle as separate pre-1984 points showing prefect and

    comparison; the institutional point is that offices and named achievement
    survived in school memory.
13. Use the Marauders-era O.W.L. and Marchbanks's recollection of examining
    Dumbledore to extend examination continuity across generations. The later
    Wizarding Examinations Authority establishes mature external administration,
    not the body's unchanged existence throughout the period.
14. State the bounded result once: administrative regularity is established;
    administrative expansion is not. This is why Chapter 19 cannot present a
    bureaucratic growth narrative.

## 4. When a School Crisis Became Public

- **The purpose of this section is to show:** in 1943 an internal emergency
  placed Hogwarts's continued operation, discipline and public standing under
  pressure from outside the school.
- **Primary claim:** C19-04.
- **Evidence IDs:** `cos-ch13-005`, `cos-ch13-007`, `cos-ch17-003`.
- **Authorship layer:** later-carried memory and confession about a pre-1984
  event.
- **Overlap control:** Chapter 17 owns danger and closure as response; Chapter
  18 owns Dippet's authority and the Hagrid retention decision. Do not narrate
  the Chamber mystery, compare heads, or resolve missing procedure.

### Paragraph plan

15. Pivot to 1943 through institutional consequence: attacks and a pupil's
    death created a possibility that Hogwarts might cease operating.
16. State the Ministry fact exactly. Dippet said the Ministry was discussing
    closure. Treat the discussion as outside pressure with existential stakes,
    not as proof of a settled legal power or a general inspection system.
17. Compress the sanction and correction into one paragraph. Hagrid was blamed
    and expelled; Riddle's later confession exposed the accusation as false.
    The point is that a modern school crisis could produce formal exclusion and
    an official-looking conclusion that was gravely wrong.
18. Draw only the Chapter 19 consequence. Hogwarts remained capable of internal
    action, but death, closure, sanction and public explanation meant that a
    crisis could no longer be treated as a private disorder within old walls.

- **Overlap control:** Chapter 17 owns defences and Chapter 20 owns Dumbledore's

### Paragraph plan

19. Establish the outside condition conservatively: by the end of the 1970s,
    the society from which Hogwarts drew pupils and staff was living under
    organised political violence.
20. Give Hagrid's testimony its proper status. He later called Hogwarts one of

21. Stop at reputation and deterrence. The testimony helps explain what the
    school meant in wartime, but supplies no fortifications, emergency rules or
    proof that ordinary schooling was untouched.

- **Overlap control:** Chapter 6 owns the castle's magical environment. This

### Paragraph plan

22. Start with the school condition attributed to *Hogwarts: A History*:
    electricity, computers, radar and similar Muggle technologies fail around
    Hogwarts because of the concentration of magic.
23. Separate environment from ideology. The evidence explains malfunction; it
    does not record a school ban, a Headmaster's rejection or a doctrine of
    medieval purity.
24. Use the failed early-1980s wizarding television proposal as a narrow
    contrast. New Muggle-derived devices were actively debated elsewhere in
    wizarding Britain and could be stopped for secrecy reasons. None of this
    puts television at Hogwarts; it shows why absence must be explained
    specifically rather than treated as proof of general hostility to change.

## 7. Change Without a Programme

- **The purpose of this section is to show:** the provisional layering thesis
  survives only as a qualified account of inheritance, continuity and
  particular adaptation.
- **Primary claim:** C19-08.
- **Supporting claims:** C19-01 through C19-04, C19-06 and C19-07.
- **Evidence IDs:** synthesis only; introduce no new evidence.
- **Authorship layer:** bounded editorial interpretation.
- **Chapter 20 control:** prepare the Dippet-to-Dumbledore transition without
  beginning it.

### Paragraph plan

25. Answer what changed. Hogwarts operated through national infrastructure,
    could extend administration beyond wizarding households, faced public
    institutional consequences in crisis and occupied a consequential place in
    a society at war.
26. Answer what remained. Ancient architecture, inherited offices and magical
    material conditions continued to shape the school; later systems were
    attached to them rather than replacing the institution's identity.
27. Qualify the thesis and hand forward. The evidence shows no master plan,
    steady liberalisation or simple victory of modernity over tradition. It
    shows an institution meeting new conditions through inherited machinery
    and particular decisions. The next historical question is how the two
    modern headmasterships most closely associated with these pressures met and
    transmitted that inheritance; do not name their full chronology here.
