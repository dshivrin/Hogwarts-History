# Chapter 16 — The Triwizard Tournament

## Outline — Revision 01

**Status:** proposed for editorial review; not yet approved for drafting

**Synthesis dependency:** `evidence-synthesis-revision-01.md` — `PASS`

**Permitted advancing claims:** C16-01 through C16-08 only

**Prepared on:** 2026-09-23

This outline implements the approved seven-part architecture. It does not map
the eight synthesis claims mechanically onto eight sections: C16-02 and C16-03
are combined because school parity and champion representation are two parts of
the same institutional arrangement. C16-06 receives a separate section because
its 1792 account is explicitly attributed to *Hogwarts: A History*.

## Authorship and source-access approach

The chapter should read as a unified institutional history while preserving a
clear division between what may enter reconstructed Bagshot narration and what
must remain later editorial history.

- The approximate foundation, three-school structure, five-year cycle,
  rotating hosts, mounting death toll, discontinuation and failed revival
  attempts concern pre-1984 history. They survive principally through
  Dumbledore's 1994 account and therefore require natural attribution as later-
  carried history rather than presentation as a surviving charter.
- The 1792 incident may enter the reconstructed historical layer with unusual
  confidence because Hermione expressly attributes it to *Hogwarts: A
  History*. The source's omission of house-elves limits completeness, not the
  accuracy of this particular Tournament report.
- The Hogwarts-hosted Tournament of 1994-95 is later editorial history. It may
  illuminate inherited structure and deliberate reform, but its Goblet
  procedure, Age Line, five-person panel, scoring method, task designs and
  safety arrangements must never be backdated.
- The later Beedle introduction supports the classification of Dumbledore's
  commentary as later-carried historical testimony; it supplies no Tournament
  fact and should not appear in reader-facing prose unless source provenance
  itself requires a brief editorial note.
- No separate addendum is proposed. The final section should make the temporal
  pivot to 1994 explicit and keep later events identifiable through natural
  dating, attribution and evidence metadata.

## 1. From relationship to institution

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to receive Chapter 15's established
three-school relationship and show that the Tournament converted contact into a
recurring institution with a stated purpose.

**Advancing claim:** C16-01

### Paragraph plan

1. Begin without resetting the wider-world argument. Move directly from the
   relationship among Hogwarts, Beauxbatons and Durmstrang to the fact that the
   schools created or sustained a recurrent competition through which they met
   as institutions. Keep the bridge short.
2. Place the Tournament approximately: later testimony dated its establishment
   to roughly seven centuries before 1994. Preserve the approximation and do
   not convert it into a founding year, charter or named act of creation.
3. Establish the stated double purpose. It was friendly competition, but the
   competition was also intended to create ties among young witches and
   wizards of different nationalities. Later descriptions of international
   cooperation and magical understanding may confirm how the revived
   institution understood that purpose; they must not be voiced as words from
   an unknown medieval foundation.
4. Close on the practical implication: purpose alone did not create an
   institution. The three schools had to accept recurrence, hosting,
   representation and a common result.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch12-007`, with later interpretive confirmation from
`gof-ch23-005` and `gof-ch37-005`.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** Approximate medieval origin and
pre-1984 institutional history carried in 1994 testimony. Reconstructed
Bagshot-compatible history requires attribution; the 1994 interpretations are
integrated later editorial confirmation.

**Source-access limitation:** No exact date, founders, charter or creating
authority survives. Naming the three schools in the foundational description
does not independently prove the precise moment at which each joined.

**Movement:** The section defines why the Tournament existed. Section 2 shows
what the three schools had to share in order to make it operate.

## 2. Three schools, one champion each

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to show how rotating hospitality,
equal representation and school honour made three independent schools into
participants in one institution without erasing their separate identities.

**Advancing claims:** C16-02 and C16-03

### Paragraph plan

1. Set out only the historically reported minimum: Hogwarts, Beauxbatons and
   Durmstrang took turns hosting a Tournament held once every five years; each
   school supplied one champion; and the three champions faced three tasks.
   Avoid supplementing this compact structure with 1994 procedure.
2. Explain the institutional consequence of rotating hosts. No school
   permanently possessed the event. Each was periodically guest and host, and
   all had to recognise rules and a result operating beyond one school's
   ordinary authority. Present this as a bounded inference from the established
   structure, not as language from a lost constitution.
3. Join parity to representation. A champion competed as the visible bearer of
   a school's honour, not merely as a private prize-seeker. The 1994 protest
   against Hogwarts receiving a second champion exposes the intended equality
   especially clearly, but remains later evidence of how the structure was
   understood.
4. Use the Hogwarts hosting of 1994 sparingly to make hospitality concrete:
   lessons ended early for a formal welcome; visiting pupils arrived with their
   heads; their conveyances occupied the grounds; and they entered the Great
   Hall's social space. State once that delegation size, transport,
   accommodation and seating are not established for older Tournaments.
5. Characterise the relationship as cordial competition. Preserve the
   qualitative evidence of Hogwarts-Beauxbatons rivalry, but exclude the
   irreconcilable claim of sixty-three Hogwarts victories and sixty-two
   Beauxbatons victories from chronology, totals and argument.
6. If the Yule Ball duty is retained, use it only as a compact later example of
   champions publicly representing their schools. Do not let the ball become a
   second account of Chapter 13's ceremonial material.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch12-007`, `gof-ch15-005`, `gof-ch15-007`,
`gof-ch16-001`, `gof-ch17-002`, `gof-ch22-002`, `ext-a29-004`.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** The host cycle, three-school parity,
one-champion representation and three-task structure are later-carried
pre-1984 history. The welcome, delegations, fairness dispute and ceremonial
duty are later editorial illustrations from 1994-95.

**Source-access limitation:** The evidence does not establish historic
delegation size, residence at the host school, the full duties of a host, or a
permanent governing body. The numerical victory totals in `ext-a29-004` remain
deferred and must not appear in the manuscript.

**Movement:** Representation raises the next institutional question: how a
champion was chosen, tried and judged. Section 3 distinguishes the historical
minimum from the unusually detailed machinery visible in the revival.

## 3. Trial and judgement

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to reconstruct what can be said
about champions, tasks and judging while preventing the procedures of 1994
from becoming timeless Tournament rules.

**Advancing claim:** C16-04, with a limited forward reference to C16-06 for the
fact that the three heads judged in 1792.

### Paragraph plan

1. Begin with the narrow historical structure: one champion per school and
   three magical tasks. Note that the three school heads were acting as judges
   in 1792, while reserving the incident itself for Section 5. Do not infer the
   rest of an old judging panel, scoring scale or dispute procedure.
2. Make an explicit temporal pivot to the revival. In 1994 the Goblet received
   names and schools, selected the intended three champions and was described
   as impartial. Pupils ordinarily submitted themselves voluntarily; once a
   name emerged, officials treated selection as a binding magical contract.
3. Separate old structure from new protection. The seventeen-plus restriction
   and Age Line were agreed safety measures for the revival, not ancient
   eligibility rules. The anomalous fourth name may demonstrate the intended
   system's assumptions and rigidity, but should receive no plot summary.
4. Describe the three revival tasks by function rather than spectacle. They
   were spaced through the year and intended to test magical prowess, daring,
   deduction and the ability to cope with danger. Dragons, the lake and the
   maze must not be presented as recurring historical forms.
5. Explain how the revival sought a common result: the three heads were joined
   by two Ministry judges, numerical marks accumulated, and information hidden
   from spectators could be supplied to the panel by the merpeople. These
   details show a shared procedure, not the older Tournament's complete rules.
6. Preserve the tension between formal independence and institutional loyalty.
   The no-teacher-help rule and a judge's private offer of assistance reveal
   that common rules did not dissolve school allegiance. Do not generalise one
   compromised revival into an eternal custom of cheating.
7. End by distinguishing impartial mechanism from invulnerable mechanism. The
   Goblet could judge within the categories presented to it and still be
   deceived about those categories. Reserve the consequences of that weakness
   for Section 7.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch16-002`, `gof-ch16-003`, `gof-ch17-001`,
`gof-ch17-003`, `gof-ch17-006`, `gof-ch20-007`, `gof-ch24-006`,
`gof-ch26-007`; the historical judging point derives from `gof-ch15-003` under
C16-06.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** One-champion and three-task structure
is later-carried history. All detailed selection, scoring, assistance and
five-judge procedure is a later editorial account of 1994-95.

**Source-access limitation:** No source dates the Goblet or proves its use in
older Tournaments. Historical selection, age rules, task spacing, secrecy,
spectators, assistance, scoring, prizes and withdrawal remain unknown.

**Movement:** The revival's stated tests already make danger part of successful
performance. Section 4 turns from procedure to the risk the institution
deliberately preserved.

## 4. Dangerous prestige

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to establish danger as a structural
feature of the Tournament—something tested, rewarded and only partly
controlled—before examining its clearest historical manifestation.

**Advancing claim:** C16-05

### Paragraph plan

1. Establish the historical result before interpreting it: the death toll
   mounted until the old Tournament was discontinued. Use the source's own
   qualitative formulation; supply no count, rate or invented sequence of
   accidents.
2. Explain why isolated-misfortune language is insufficient. The revival still
   assessed daring and the ability to cope with danger. Institutional prestige
   therefore depended in part upon champions entering hazards that ordinary
   school life did not require. Present the link between danger and prestige as
   a bounded synthesis of the stated tests and representative honour.
3. Use the final revival task only as a controlled example. Staff patrols and a
   distress signal were placed around a maze that deliberately contained
   dangerous creatures and enchantments. The precautions operated inside the
   danger rather than removing it.
4. Define the organisers' attempted distinction fairly: they promised to
   prevent mortal danger, not all danger. The historical problem was the
   difficulty of maintaining that boundary while preserving what made the
   Tournament prestigious.
5. Avoid Cedric Diggory's death here. It belongs to the later institutional
   failure in Section 7 because it followed sabotage and murder, not an
   intended task casualty.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch12-007`, `gof-ch16-002`, `gof-ch31-003`,
`gof-ch31-006`.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** The mounting death toll and old
discontinuation are pre-1984 history carried in 1994 testimony. The task aims,
hazards and rescue arrangements are later editorial evidence used only to
illuminate the institution's retained logic.

**Source-access limitation:** No casualty statistics, formal risk policy or
contemporary justification for continuing the old Tournament survives. The
1994 maze cannot establish an older task type.

**Movement:** Having established the structural problem, Section 5 turns back
to 1792—the one securely dated old Tournament in which that danger can be seen
acting upon the institution's governors.

## 5. The Tournament of 1792

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to give the 1792 cockatrice
disaster its proper evidentiary and institutional weight without converting it
into either comic spectacle or a single-cause explanation of abandonment.

**Advancing claim:** C16-06

### Paragraph plan

1. Mark the temporal pivot explicitly. Introduce 1792 as the clearest surviving
   dated incident from the older Tournament and identify its unusual source
   access: Hermione says the account appears in *Hogwarts: A History*.
2. Reconstruct only the secure sequence. The champions were required to catch a
   cockatrice; the creature went on the rampage; and all three participating
   school heads, serving as judges, were injured.
3. Draw the institutional consequence rather than embellishing the scene. A
   competition meant to connect three schools endangered the very heads
   responsible for representing and governing them. The incident joins shared
   judgement, spectacle and uncontrolled danger in one unusually compact case.
4. State the meaningful limits once. The host school, champions, winner, loss-
   of-control mechanism, injuries and immediate administrative response are
   unknown. Do not invent them and do not call 1792 the final Tournament.
5. Separate source completeness from factual contradiction. Hermione's
   criticism that *Hogwarts: A History* omitted house-elves warns that an
   institutional history may be selective; it does not impeach the cockatrice
   report. Do not reopen the project's separate treatment of servitude.
6. End by refusing single-event causation. The evidence connects eventual
   abandonment to a mounting death toll, not specifically to a decree produced
   by the 1792 disaster.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch15-003`. `beedle-fm-004` governs source-method
classification only and does not corroborate the incident.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** Directly dated pre-1984 event with
explicit *Hogwarts: A History* attribution; eligible for reconstructed
historical narration with hidden evidence metadata. Any discussion of the
later carrier's wording remains editorial provenance.

**Source-access limitation:** Exceptional access improves confidence in the
facts actually reported; it does not fill any of the report's missing details
or establish comprehensive coverage of the old Tournament.

**Movement:** The incident demonstrates the problem but does not explain the
administrative end. Section 6 returns to the longer accumulation of deaths,
discontinuation and failed recovery.

## 6. Abandonment without oblivion

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to describe discontinuation as the
result of accumulated danger while showing that institutional memory outlasted
operation.

**Advancing claim:** C16-07

### Paragraph plan

1. Return from the dated case to the longer history. The death toll mounted and
   the Tournament was discontinued; by 1994 it had not been held for more than
   a century. Do not infer a final date from the phrase or force 1792 into that
   role.
2. Preserve the administrative silence. The evidence identifies no decisive
   accident, decree, vote, government, international body or individual with
   final authority to end the Tournament. Describe abandonment as the
   historical outcome of accumulated danger, not a reconstructed meeting.
3. Establish that disappearance did not equal forgetting. Several attempts
   were made over the centuries to reinstate the Tournament, though none was
   very successful before 1994.
4. Resist inventing a history for those attempts. Their dates, proponents,
   proposed safeguards, venues and precise reasons for failure are unknown;
   Hogwarts's direct participation in every attempt is not documented.
5. Draw the bounded institutional inference. Repeated restoration attempts
   show that the three-school form retained prestige and recognisability even
   when it no longer functioned. This persistence prepares the return to
   Hogwarts in 1994 without pretending that the relationship was continuously
   enacted through Tournaments.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch12-007`.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** Undated pre-1994 discontinuation and
failed revivals, all preserved through later testimony. Suitable for attributed
historical narration; administrative detail must remain absent.

**Source-access limitation:** No final old Tournament, cancellation instrument
or discrete failed-revival chronology survives.

**Movement:** The long interruption changes the meaning of 1994: it was not
simply another Tournament, but a negotiated reconstruction of an institution
that had already failed. Section 7 examines what organisers preserved, what
they changed and what the result reveals about Hogwarts.

## 7. Reconstructing the Tournament

**Purpose:** The purpose of this section is to use the 1994 revival as a
controlled comparison, show the limits of its reforms, complete the argument
about Hogwarts within shared authority, and open naturally into Chapter 17.

**Advancing claim:** C16-08, drawing together the consequences of C16-01 through
C16-07 without re-proving them.

### Paragraph plan

1. Make the later temporal pivot explicit. The 1994 revival resulted from
   summer planning by the British Ministry's Departments of International
   Magical Cooperation and Magical Games and Sports, agreement among the three
   heads, and meetings, negotiations and compromises among the participants.
   Do not infer a permanent international governing body.
2. Distinguish preservation from reform in one compact comparison. The revival
   preserved the three schools, one intended champion each, three tasks,
   hosting, school honour and a common result. It added or newly documents the
   seventeen-plus rule, Age Line, Goblet submission procedure, five-person
   panel, exact scoring arrangements, Ministry administration and specific
   rescue precautions.
3. Examine the reform's first weakness without retelling the selection plot.
   The Goblet's categories could be manipulated, the Age Line guarded entry
   rather than the object itself, and the binding contract forced officials to
   accept a result produced outside the intended procedure. The point is the
   limit of procedural impartiality.
4. Reconnect procedure to danger. Rescue signals and staff patrols mitigated
   hazards, but the tasks still rewarded coping with danger. The revival tried
   to preserve the old institution's prestige while separating danger from
   death; its design never made those aims comfortably independent.
5. Treat the final catastrophe with strict economy. An infiltrator manipulated
   selection and the Cup; Cedric Diggory was murdered after removal from the
   intended task. Distinguish hostile intervention from an ordinary task
   casualty while recognising that the Tournament's machinery supplied the
   route through which the attack succeeded. Do not narrate Voldemort's return.
6. Give the later fate at the level supported. A much later student account
   calls 1994-95 the last Tournament and says the competition was cancelled
   afterward. State no decree, date, cancelling authority or permanent legal
   ban. Dumbledore's continued welcome to Beauxbatons and Durmstrang shows that
   the failure of the event did not dissolve the relationship among the
   schools.
7. Complete the Hogwarts argument. The Tournament required Hogwarts to accept
   a host cycle it did not own, a champion who represented it, judges beyond
   its sole control and rules negotiated across institutional boundaries. Its
   history therefore reveals more than foreign contact: Hogwarts could enter a
   shared institution in which no school possessed all authority.
8. Join that achievement to its limit. Prestige and danger reinforced one
   another strongly enough to defeat the old Tournament and to survive inside
   the reconstructed one despite explicit reform. The institution's long
   disappearance is as revealing as its creation because tradition proved
   easier to remember than to make safe.
9. End with a restrained transition to Chapter 17. Widen only from the failed
   boundary between danger and mortal danger to the larger institutional
   problem confronting a school devoted to magic: risk may be regulated,
   restricted or used as grounds for abandoning a tradition, but it cannot be
   made to disappear. Do not introduce general school disasters, wars, attacks
   or defensive history.

**Evidence basis:** `gof-ch12-007`, `gof-ch16-002`, `gof-ch16-003`,
`gof-ch17-001`, `gof-ch17-003`, `gof-ch17-004`, `gof-ch31-003`,
`gof-ch32-002`, `gof-ch35-002`, `gof-ch37-005`,
`cc-p1-a1-s10-002`; institutional conclusion synthesises C16-01 through C16-08.

**Chronological status / passage mode:** Entirely later editorial history and
bounded editorial synthesis. It must be clearly dated after the reconstructed
1984 boundary and cannot be voiced as Bagshot's completed knowledge.

**Source-access limitation:** The later cessation report is compressed and
supplies no administrative record. The sabotage demonstrates vulnerability in
the reconstructed Tournament; it does not establish that every ordinary
safeguard was inherently useless or that Cedric died from an intended task.

**Movement:** This section is the chapter's conclusion. It must not be followed
by an eighth summary section. Its final movement opens Chapter 17's wider
history of danger without beginning that history.

  Voldemort's return.
