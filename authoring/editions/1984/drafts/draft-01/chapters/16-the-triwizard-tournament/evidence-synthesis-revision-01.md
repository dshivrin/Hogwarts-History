## Chapter Purpose

Reconstruct the Tournament as a shared institution through which Hogwarts,
Beauxbatons and Durmstrang accepted recurring competition, representation by
individual champions, rotating hospitality and common judgement. The chapter
must show why danger belonged to the institution's prestige rather than merely
interrupting it, why accumulated casualties eventually defeated the tradition,
and what the deliberately altered 1994 revival reveals without turning that
revival into the historical norm.

## Source-Access Decision

`gof-ch15-003` receives exceptional evidentiary weight. Hermione expressly
attributes the 1792 account to *Hogwarts: A History*, so this is not merely a
later character remembering an old story: it is testimony about what the
fictional work being reconstructed contained. Her immediate criticism that the
book omits Hogwarts house-elves limits its completeness and institutional
perspective; it does not contradict the cockatrice account.

`beedle-fm-004` independently supports the project's method for other later
historical testimony: Rowling's introduction describes Dumbledore's commentary
as containing observations on wizarding history and personal reminiscence and
gives that commentary a later archival and editorial provenance. It therefore
supports classifying such material as later-carried testimony. It supplies no
fact about the Tournament and cannot corroborate the events of 1792.

### C16-01 — The Tournament turned a relationship into a recurring institution

- **Claim:** Later testimony places the Tournament's establishment roughly
  seven centuries before 1994 as a friendly competition among Hogwarts,
  Beauxbatons and Durmstrang. It joined competition to the establishment of ties
  among young witches and wizards of different nationalities; later speakers
  restated that purpose as international cooperation or magical understanding.
- **Event chronology:** approximate medieval origin, followed by an undated
  historical duration.
- **Knowledge access:** pre-1984 history carried by Dumbledore in 1994; later
  statements confirm how revival participants understood the purpose.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported, with approximate dating and no
  surviving founding instrument.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** No exact founding year, founders, charter, or creating
  authority is known. The account names all three schools but does not preserve
  a separate proof that each joined on the first day rather than during the
  institution's earliest development.
- **Intended primary section:** Purpose and Origin
- **Cross-chapter status:** direct handoff from Chapter 15; do not reopen its
  broader survey of international relations.

  space for visitors and recognition of school parity—while the older details
  of delegation size and accommodation remain unknown.
- **Event chronology:** historical recurring structure, illuminated by the
  exceptional Hogwarts-hosted revival of 1994-95.
- **Knowledge access:** the core structure is later-carried pre-1984 history;

  central owner of the Tournament or proves ordinary governmental supervision.
- **Intended primary section:** Three-School Structure and Hosting

### C16-05 — Danger was a constitutive feature, not a sequence of accidents

- **Claim:** The old Tournament ended after its death toll mounted, while the
  revival still made dangerous situations and the ability to cope with them
  part of what champions were judged upon. Safety arrangements operated within
  that hazardous design rather than removing it. The historically safe
  conclusion is therefore that danger and prestige reinforced one another;
  no casualty rate or institutional theory of acceptable risk survives.
- **Event chronology:** undated historical operation and discontinuation,
  illuminated by 1994-95 design.
- **Knowledge access:** later testimony preserves the old death toll; direct
  revival evidence demonstrates the later institution's retained logic.
- **Classification:** historical fact plus bounded editorial inference.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Do not invent statistics, motives, regulations or an exact
  administrative decision. Creature obstacles and the maze are 1994 task
  designs, not traditional task types.
- **Intended primary section:** Danger and Discontinuation
- **Cross-chapter status:** Chapter 16 owns Tournament danger; Chapter 17 owns
  danger at Hogwarts more generally.

### C16-08 — The 1994 reconstruction repaired procedure without resolving danger

- **Claim:** The successful revival required British Ministry organisation,
  agreement among the three heads and inter-school negotiation. It preserved
  the three-school competition, champions, tasks and shared result while adding
  explicit safety and administrative measures. Those measures remained
  vulnerable: the selector could be deceived, the binding rule could compel an
  anomalously chosen pupil, and safeguards inside the tasks could not defeat
  hostile infiltration. Cedric Diggory's death resulted from sabotage and
  murder outside the intended task, not from an ordinary task casualty, yet
  later testimony identifies the 1994-95 event as the last Tournament and says
  it was cancelled afterward.
- **Event chronology:** 1994-95 revival and later retrospective account of its
  cessation.
- **Knowledge access:** later editorial history; none of the outcome belongs to
  a reconstructed 1984 narrator.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported revival history with a late, compressed
  statement about subsequent cancellation.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Keep the plot mechanics subordinate to institutional
  analysis. No source supplies a cancellation decree, date, authority or legal
  permanence. The later welcome extended to Beauxbatons and Durmstrang shows
  that the failed Tournament did not destroy the schools' relationship.
- **Intended primary section:** Revival and Reform
- **Cross-chapter status:** conclude with the limits of shared rules and safety,
  allowing Chapter 17 to widen from Tournament risk to school danger.

## Source Tensions and Required Containment

### The victory-total problem

`ext-a29-004` reports sixty-three Hogwarts victories and sixty-two Beauxbatons
victories. Those 125 wins exclude every Durmstrang victory, yet `gof-ch12-007`
describes a five-year cycle, an origin only roughly seven centuries before 1994
and an interruption exceeding a century. The surviving statements cannot be
confidently reconciled.

The later official entry may support the qualitative claim that cordiality and
rivalry coexisted. Its totals must not be used to calculate Tournament count,
continuity, chronology or a Durmstrang record. No explanation—additional
Tournaments, joint winners, a different counting system, a much earlier origin
or simple inconsistency—may be invented.

### Impartial procedure and vulnerable procedure

The Goblet's description as impartial is compatible with later deception only
if impartiality refers to its evaluation of the categories presented to it,
not immunity from external manipulation. The synthesis supports a limit of
procedural neutrality, not a claim that the object knowingly favoured anyone.

### Promised safety and designed danger

The revival's promise to prevent mortal danger existed beside tasks intended to
test a champion's response to danger. This is the central tension of the reform,
not a factual contradiction: organisers tried to preserve hazardous prestige
while preventing death.
