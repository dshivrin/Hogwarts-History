# Chapter 16 Research Brief — The Triwizard Tournament

Status: research brief only; no evidence selection, synthesis, outline, or
manuscript has been approved or created.

## Research frame

- **Chapter purpose:** reconstruct the Tournament's history, purposes,
  interruptions, and significance to Hogwarts.
- **Entry point:** Chapter 15 has already established the durable relationship
  among Hogwarts, Beauxbatons, and Durmstrang. Chapter 16 should move directly
  from relationship, to institution, to competition.
- **Exit point:** the Tournament shows both Hogwarts's capacity to share rules
  and authority with peer schools and the failure of a prestigious tradition
  to control the dangers it rewarded. This permits Chapter 17 to widen from
  Tournament danger to danger at Hogwarts generally.
- **Presentation model:** the pre-1984 history may enter reconstructed Bagshot
  narration where knowledge access is adequate. The 1994–95 revival and its
  later fate require identifiable later editorial treatment under the active
  style lock.
- **Protected boundary:** general international relations remain in Chapter
  15; general school danger remains in Chapter 17; the Tournament's own
  history, governance, risks, and revival belong here.

## A. Evidence inventory

### 1. Foundation, purpose, cycle, and discontinuation

- `gof-ch12-007` — the central later-carried institutional account. Dumbledore
  says the Tournament was first established roughly seven centuries before
  1994 as a friendly competition among Hogwarts, Beauxbatons, and Durmstrang;
  one champion represented each school in three magical tasks; the schools
  took turns hosting once every five years; the institution was valued for
  establishing ties among young witches and wizards of different
  nationalities; and it was discontinued when the death toll mounted. The same
  entry records several unsuccessful attempts at reinstatement and the
  Ministry-led 1994 revival. **Source role:** establishes the historical core
  and the announced revival. **Limit:** 1994 testimony, approximate dating,
  and no surviving charter, exact dates, or administrative record.
- `gof-ch37-005` — Dumbledore's closing 1995 statement that the Tournament's
  aim was to promote magical understanding. **Source role:** later
  retrospective confirmation of diplomatic purpose. **Limit:** aspirational
  closing speech after catastrophe, not a founding document.
- `gof-ch23-005` — Hermione and Percy describe the revival as a means of
  meeting foreign wizards and international magical cooperation while student
  rivalry pulls in the opposite direction. **Source role:** adds the social
  meaning of the revived institution. **Limit:** informal 1994–95
  interpretation, not an ancient rule.
- `ext-a29-004` — later official history describes Hogwarts and Beauxbatons as
  generally cordial but competitively opposed in the Tournament, and gives
  sixty-three Hogwarts victories to sixty-two Beauxbatons victories. **Source
  role:** adds historical rivalry and a claimed cumulative record. **Limit:**
  undated totals that produce a chronology problem discussed under
  Contradictions.

## F. Contradictions and tensions

### 1. Win totals versus the surviving chronology

`ext-a29-004` gives Hogwarts sixty-three wins and Beauxbatons sixty-two, already
totaling 125 victories before any Durmstrang wins are counted. `gof-ch12-007`
places the foundation only roughly seven centuries before 1994, says the event
occurred once every five years, and says it had not been held for over a
century. Read literally and as an uninterrupted schedule, those statements do
not comfortably allow 125 victories for only two schools plus an unknown
number for Durmstrang.

Possible explanations—an older origin hidden by the approximation, irregular
extra Tournaments, multiple winners, a later counting convention, or a
continuity error—are not established by any selected source. The totals should
therefore not be used to calculate a number of Tournaments or a Durmstrang
record. If retained at all, they require explicit attribution as a later
official claim whose chronology is unresolved.

### 2. “No mortal danger” versus admitted dangerous design

In `gof-ch12-007`, organisers claim to have worked to ensure that no champion
would face mortal danger, yet immediately acknowledge that the tasks would
remain difficult and dangerous. `gof-ch16-002` makes ability to cope with
danger an explicit object of assessment. This is not a direct factual
contradiction: the intended distinction was evidently between danger and
death. It is, however, the central institutional tension of the revival. The
later creature hazards and limited rescue arrangements show how narrow that
distinction was even before sabotage. (`gof-ch19-005`, `gof-ch31-003`,
`gof-ch31-006`)

### 3. Impartial mechanism versus vulnerable mechanism

The Goblet is called an impartial judge or selector in `gof-ch12-007` and
`gof-ch16-002`, but it is later deceived by a powerful Confundus Charm and a
false school category. (`gof-ch17-005`, `gof-ch35-002`) These claims are
compatible if impartiality describes how the object evaluates the categories
presented to it, not immunity from external manipulation. The chapter may use
this as a limit of procedural neutrality, but must not claim that the Goblet
was knowingly biased.

### 4. Neutral judging versus school loyalty

The revived panel combined the three school heads with two ostensibly
objective Ministry judges. Yet Karkaroff's scoring and Bagman's private offer
of assistance reveal institutional and personal partiality. (`gof-ch20-007`,
`gof-ch24-006`) This does not disprove the rules; it shows that common procedure
did not remove school rivalry from judgement.

### 5. Source criticism of *Hogwarts: A History*

In `gof-ch15-003`, Hermione cites *Hogwarts: A History* for the 1792 incident
and then calls the book selective because it omits Hogwarts house-elves. This
is a genuine warning about comprehensiveness and institutional perspective,
but not a direct contradiction of the cockatrice account. The Tournament
claim remains usable as attributed original-book-core evidence while the
source's broader omissions remain acknowledged elsewhere in the project.

## Research conclusion

The evidence supports a chapter built around an institution rather than a
sequence of spectacular tasks. Hogwarts entered a recurring arrangement with
two peer schools: it accepted a rotating host, one representative champion,
shared judging, a common result, and a purpose larger than victory. The same
structure converted school honour into pressure upon individual pupils and
made danger part of what the institution rewarded. The 1792 disaster matters
because it brought that danger to the three heads governing the event; the
later discontinuation matters because the accumulated danger finally defeated
tradition. The 1994 revival is most useful as a controlled comparison: it
preserved representation, tasks, rivalry, hosting, and inter-school purpose,
while adding explicit safety reforms and governmental organisation that must
not be backdated.

The next authorised stage should be a curated `evidence-selection.yaml`,
followed by a revision-matched evidence synthesis. No outline or chapter prose
should be produced until that synthesis passes the validation gate and the
resulting outline receives approval.
