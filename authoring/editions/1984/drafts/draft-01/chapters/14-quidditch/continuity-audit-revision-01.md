## Chapter 13 → 14 continuity

Chapter 13 ends:

> Among those repetitions, none gathered House identity into a more visible
> season of its own than Quidditch.

Chapter 14 begins by making that claim concrete: House membership acquires
colours, players, opponents and results on the pitch. It does not repeat
Chapter 13's theory of participatory custom. It immediately changes scale from
the school calendar generally to the one seasonal institution reserved for
Chapter 14.

The opening remains at Hogwarts and with House identity. Queerditch Marsh does
not appear until the temporal pivot in paragraph 3, after the institutional
question has been established.

- Paragraphs 1–2 establish the mature institution and its question.
- Paragraph 3 turns backwards and marks the chronological limit.
- Paragraphs 4–5 compress only the general developments necessary to prevent
  a founders-era modern game.

  history.

  claiming either is an origin.

  from later observations.

- Paragraphs 27–33 test the institution through risk, supervision, injury and
  interruption.

- Paragraph 36 completes the Hogwarts institutional synthesis.
- Paragraph 37 applies the approved narrow mature-period formulation before
  turning outward.

## Chronology and continuity safeguards

- The draft does not state or imply that Hogwarts Quidditch developed
  continuously through every general change to the sport.
- The precise adoption date, origin of four House teams, first Quidditch Cup
  and pitch construction remain open.
- Paragraph 37 follows the approved narrower principle: by the closely
  observed mature period, changing players, equipment and circumstances had
  not displaced the sport's institutional role.
- A12 remains excluded.
- Harry-era procedure remains later-observed rather than acquiring an implied
  pre-1984 date through proximity to the Lockhart or Regulus evidence.

## Chapter 14 → 15 continuity

The verified Chapter 15 title is *Hogwarts and the Wider Wizarding World*.
Chapter 14's penultimate paragraph closes the internal argument completely:
Houses, pitch, captains, season, Cup and spectatorship make the institution
work at Hogwarts. Only the final paragraph turns outward.

That turn is supported but restrained. The British and Irish League and the
international competitions establish that Quidditch belonged to a wider
sporting culture. The draft does not claim a formal League-Hogwarts
relationship or preview Chapter 15's broader contents. It simply leaves the
school pitch as one local expression of a world extending beyond the grounds.
