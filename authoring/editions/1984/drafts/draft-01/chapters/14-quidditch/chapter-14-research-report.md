## Source-Access Findings

### Directly known before 1984

The general history in QTA and the broad founding-age statement are direct historical claims within the evidence corpus. The Lockhart retrospective directly asserts a pre-1984 pitch incident. None supplies the origin of Hogwarts Quidditch.

### Later evidence carrying an older Hogwarts condition

`dh-ch10-004` is the clearest example: a 1997 observation of a photograph preserves a pre-1984 Slytherin team and player role. It establishes existence by the photographed period, not institutional beginnings. `ext-a32-003` is later official retrospective testimony about a pre-1984 pitch.

### Later observations of mature practice

Nearly all operational detail—four House teams, trials, practice, captaincy, facilities, match-day attendance, commentary, standings, Cup arithmetic, medical consequences, and celebrations—is observed in 1991–97. It is strong evidence for the mature institution and weak evidence for the date or exact form of earlier practice.

### Bounded editorial inferences

The following may be argued if marked as synthesis rather than source fact:

- Hogwarts adopted and adapted a game whose mature features arose after the school’s foundation.
- House organisation made the sport especially durable and legible within Hogwarts.
- The pitch and calendar gave existing House rivalry physical and seasonal recurrence.
- Cancellation reveals ordinary expectation by contrast.
- Team photographs, remembered results, captaincy, and Cup celebrations converted individual play into House memory.

The following may **not** be inferred:

- founders or earliest pupils playing modern Quidditch;
- an exact adoption, team-formation, Cup-foundation, or pitch-construction date;
- universal 1990s trials, commentary, equipment, or eligibility procedures in earlier centuries;
- automatic application of every professional rule to Hogwarts;
- a complete tournament format from partial fixture observations;
- a Hogwarts team career for a professional player merely because the person attended Hogwarts.

### Validated central argument

The evidence supports the proposed central argument after removing any implied origin story. Hogwarts is older than mature Quidditch. The date and manner of adoption are unknown. By pre-1984 evidence the school possessed a dedicated pitch and at least one House team; later observations show a mature four-House competition whose arena, calendar, captains, standings, spectators, commentary, and Cup repeatedly made House identity public. The history recoverable here is therefore a history of institutional fit and adaptation, not a continuous chronicle from the founders.

### Strongest historical gaps

1. exact date and circumstances of adoption at Hogwarts;
2. origin of the House teams;
3. origin of the Hogwarts Quidditch Cup;
4. construction date and architectural development of the pitch;
5. early selection and captaincy procedures;
6. a complete stated fixture and tie-break system;
7. dedicated evidence IDs for the relevant A12 McGonagall material;
8. securely evidenced Hogwarts-to-professional player careers.

## Proposed Chapter 14 Outline

The complete movement-level plan, including purpose, main claim, evidence IDs, chronological status, limitation, and transitions, is recorded in `outline-revision-01.md`.

1. **House identity made visible** — receive Chapter 13 through mature match-day spectatorship; later observation only.
2. **A school older than the game it came to house** — establish the chronological restraint.
3. **The game assembled by stages** — compress only the external developments necessary for Hogwarts.
4. **The earliest recoverable traces: evidence without an origin** — present the pre-1984 pitch and Slytherin-team evidence; state “origin unknown.”
5. **Four Houses, four teams** — explain the mature seven-player House structure without a rules manual.
6. **The pitch as institutional ground** — show how space enabled practice, administration, spectatorship, and exceptional reuse.
7. **Captains, trials, and the making of a team** — reconstruct later captain-led selection and training with House/year qualifications.
8. **Brooms, provision, and exceptional access** — distinguish school resources, personal equipment, patronage, ownership rules, and eligibility.
9. **Match day: fourteen players, one school audience** — make collective spectatorship the chapter’s conceptual centre.
10. **The season and the Quidditch Cup** — describe cumulative stakes and scoring margins without inventing a format.
11. **Risk within regulation** — balance accepted danger with refereeing, custody, treatment, and intervention.
12. **Seasons interrupted** — use cancellation, repurposing, bans, and replacements to reveal the ordinary rhythm by contrast.
13. **Players, records, and inherited expectation** — treat photographs, captaincy, remembered matches, trophy presentation, and celebration as institutional memory.
14. **A recurring public expression of Hogwarts** — close on teams, loyalties, pitch, season, Cup, and spectatorship; turn outward only in the final movement toward the wider wizarding culture of Chapter 15.

The outline forms an argument rather than an inventory: visible mature importance prompts chronological restraint; restraint leads to the limited school archive; the archive opens into a carefully labelled mature reconstruction; match day and season supply the institutional centre; risk and interruption test its boundaries; memory and the wider sporting context close it.

## Open Questions

Only the following unresolved questions materially affect drafting:

1. **Will the research layer extract dedicated evidence entries from official source A12 before drafting?** If yes, the earliest-history chronology and movement 4 must be re-audited. If no, the relevant Isobel Ross and Minerva McGonagall claims remain excluded.
2. **Should the final approved chapter state that the observed fixtures are “consistent with” each House meeting the others, or avoid even that bounded inference?** The current outline avoids the inference because it adds little to the institutional argument.
3. **Should the term “four House teams” be introduced as a compact synthesis of repeatedly observed named teams, or should the prose enumerate the four only once?** Either is supportable for the mature system; neither should be projected backward.
4. **How much twentieth-century broom chronology is useful after the general-history compression?** The evidence supports it, but the outline treats it as subordinate to equipment inequality at Hogwarts. It can be reduced further without harming the argument.

Questions that do **not** require editorial invention—adoption date, Cup origin, pitch construction, original trials, and full fixture rules—should remain explicitly unresolved in the chapter.
