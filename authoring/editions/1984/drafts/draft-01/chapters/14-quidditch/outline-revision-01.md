## 1. House identity made visible

**Purpose:** Receive Chapter 13’s final sentence and establish the chapter’s institutional question at Hogwarts before stepping into general sporting history.  
**Main claim:** Quidditch gave existing House membership a public season: teams represented Houses, while matches drew the wider school into watching, supporting, judging, and remembering their fortunes.  
**Evidence:** `ps-ch11-004`, `cos-ch10-003`, `poa-ch15-006`, `ootp-ch19-005`, `hbp-ch14-005`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Later observations of the mature institution; integrated editorial synthesis, not 1984-era historical proof of origins.  
**Important limitation:** Do not claim that Quidditch created House rivalry or that this match culture existed unchanged from the school’s beginning.  
**Movement:** Begins exactly where Chapter 13 ends. The public spectacle raises the historical question answered by the next movement: how old could this institution actually be?

## 2. A school older than the game it came to house

**Purpose:** Establish the decisive chronological restraint.  
**Main claim:** Hogwarts’s broad founding era precedes the securely recoverable development of mature Quidditch, so neither the founders nor the earliest pupils can casually be assigned the modern game.  
**Evidence:** `cos-ch09-003`, `qtta-ch01-002`, `qtta-ch02-001`, `qtta-ch03-001`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Pre-1984 historical core; reconstructed Bagshot-compatible chronology, with the school date remaining approximate.  
**Important limitation:** Early broom flight and an eleventh-century proto-game do not by themselves establish any sport at Hogwarts.  
**Movement:** Reframes Quidditch as a later institutional acquisition. The next movement identifies only the developments needed to understand what “later” means.

## 3. The game assembled by stages

**Purpose:** Supply a compressed external chronology without turning the chapter into an abridged *Quidditch Through the Ages*.  
**Main claim:** The mature game emerged through successive changes: early team organisation and ball roles; the 1269 Snidget episode and later mechanical Snitch; permanent-pitch guidance; scoring areas; nationally agreed rules; fixed hoops; and continuing role and broom development.  
**Evidence:** `qtta-ch03-002`, `qtta-ch04-001`, `qtta-ch04-002`, `qtta-ch04-003`, `qtta-ch05-001`, `qtta-ch06-001`, `qtta-ch06-005`, `qtta-ch06-006`, `qtta-ch06-007`, `qtta-ch09-001`, `qtta-ch09-003`, `qtta-ch09-006`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Direct general history, largely pre-1984; suitable for the reconstructed historical voice when explicitly kept general.  
**Important limitation:** Do not supply a Hogwarts adoption date from this sequence, and omit details that do not change what Hogwarts could have adopted.  
**Movement:** Converts an abstract warning into a usable chronology. Having established what cannot be original to Hogwarts, the chapter can ask what school-specific history is actually recoverable.

## 4. The earliest recoverable traces: evidence without an origin

**Purpose:** State the earliest secure indexed Hogwarts evidence and the limits of the archive.  
**Main claim:** The current evidence securely places a dedicated pitch at Hogwarts during Lockhart’s school years and preserves a photograph of a pre-1984 Slytherin team with Regulus Black as Seeker; it does not disclose when Quidditch, House teams, the Cup, or the pitch began.  
**Evidence:** `ext-a32-003`, `dh-ch10-004`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Later-carried evidence of pre-1984 conditions; editorially framed.  
**Important limitation:** **Origin unknown.** Do not use the unextracted A12 McGonagall statements unless dedicated evidence entries are added and re-audited.  
**Movement:** Replaces a missing origin story with an honest terminus ante quem. The next movement moves from that narrow historical footing to the mature structure later observed.

## 5. Four Houses, four teams

**Purpose:** Reconstruct the basic unit of the mature institution without teaching the entire rulebook.  
**Main claim:** Later Hogwarts practice organised the familiar seven-player game through House teams: three Chasers, two Beaters, a Keeper, and a Seeker, under a captain. The structural importance lies in permanent House representation, not in the rules for their own sake.  
**Evidence:** `cos-ch07-001`, `poa-ch08-002`, `gof-ch06-004`, `poa-ch15-006`, `hbp-ch24-007`, with pre-1984 confirmation of at least one House team from `dh-ch10-004`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Mixed: one pre-1984 condition carried later, and predominantly 1990s observation of the mature system.  
**Important limitation:** The evidence does not date the creation of four teams or prove identical rosters across all earlier centuries.  
**Movement:** Establishes the organisational actors. The next movement shows how the school’s physical resources made their rivalry recurring rather than occasional.

## 6. The pitch as institutional ground

**Purpose:** Treat physical space only insofar as it enables school Quidditch.  
**Main claim:** The pitch joined playing field, raised spectator stands, changing facilities, equipment custody, and later administrative space. It could serve practice, formal matches, and an audience large enough for the school; its Triwizard conversion demonstrates an established arena temporarily diverted from its ordinary use.  
**Evidence:** `ext-a32-003`, `ps-ch11-004`, `cos-ch10-004`, `ootp-ch14-004`, `gof-ch28-004`, `gof-ch31-002`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Pre-1984 existence plus later observation of facilities and exceptional repurposing.  
**Important limitation:** No construction date or architectural sequence survives; general pitch standards in `qtta-ch06-001` must not be assigned to Hogwarts without explicit qualification.  
**Movement:** Gives the teams a common arena. The next movement gives them leadership, membership, and preparation.

## 7. Captains, trials, and the making of a team

**Purpose:** Explain how player achievement became an organised and socially visible school role.  
**Main claim:** In later seasons captains convened teams, judged trials, set practices, shaped tactics, and chose replacements; Heads of House could communicate opportunities or intervene, but observed selection remained substantially captain-led. Captaincy itself carried recognised status.  
**Evidence:** `cos-ch07-002`, `poa-ch08-002`, `ootp-ch13-007`, `ootp-ch14-004`, `hbp-ch06-001`, `hbp-ch08-004`, `hbp-ch09-002`, `hbp-ch11-003`, `hbp-ch14-003`, `hbp-ch14-004`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Later observation of mature practice.  
**Important limitation:** Do not universalise one House’s trial method, infer a written selection code, or identify an unevidenced appointing authority for captains.  
**Movement:** Moves from institutional shell to participants and status. The equipment question then reveals unequal material conditions within the same competition.

## 8. Brooms, provision, and exceptional access

**Purpose:** Clarify school resources, personal equipment, and the narrow first-year evidence.  
**Main claim:** Hogwarts maintained brooms for instruction and later trial use, while individual ownership could confer an advantage and patronage could equip an entire team. The rule barring first-years from bringing personal broomsticks did not itself state a ban on playing; Harry’s selection was exceptional rather than a template.  
**Evidence:** `ps-ch05-002`, `ps-ch09-002`, `ps-ch09-003`, `cos-ch07-002`, `hbp-ch11-003`; general technological context from `qtta-ch09-001`, `qtta-ch09-003`, `qtta-ch09-006`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Later Hogwarts observation, with pre-1984 general broom chronology.  
**Important limitation:** Do not claim that school lesson brooms normally equipped teams, that all players owned brooms, or that first-years were universally ineligible.  
**Movement:** Completes the account of team formation. The next movement expands from selected players to the school assembled around them.

## 9. Match day: fourteen players, one school audience

**Purpose:** Form the chapter’s conceptual centre and answer what transformed play into an institutional event.  
**Main claim:** Whole-school attendance, blocks of House colour, banners and enchanted supporter objects, changing-room preparation, formal handshakes and starts, a referee, amplified commentary under staff supervision, and celebrations that entered the castle made Quidditch collectively witnessed and collectively interpreted.  
**Evidence:** `ps-ch11-004`, `ps-ch11-005`, `cos-ch10-003`, `poa-ch15-006`, `poa-ch15-007`, `ootp-ch19-005`, `ootp-ch19-006`, `ootp-ch30-003`, `hbp-ch14-005`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Later observation of the mature institution; integrated later editorial addition.  
**Important limitation:** Commentary arrangements and attendance are observed repeatedly but not proved identical for every match or earlier century. The most hostile song and the 1994 Cup-decider atmosphere are not ordinary baselines.  
**Movement:** Demonstrates the public expression promised in the opening. The next movement shows how repetition and standings extended that spectacle across a year.

## 10. The season and the Quidditch Cup

**Purpose:** Reconstruct the competition only as far as the sources allow.  
**Main claim:** Matches accumulated into a seasonal House competition: autumn practices and fixtures affected later standings; teams calculated required margins; and a later final match could resolve the Cup. The importance of points scored means the institution cannot be reduced to simple win-loss results.  
**Evidence:** `ps-ch11-001`, `poa-ch12-004`, `poa-ch15-004`, `poa-ch15-007`, `ootp-ch19-003`, `ootp-ch30-003`, `hbp-ch24-007`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Later observation of mature seasonal practice.  
**Important limitation:** **Origin unknown.** Do not invent a bracket, claim a fully stated round robin, specify unevidenced tie-breakers, or conflate the Quidditch Cup with the House Cup.  
**Movement:** Gives match day recurrence and consequence. The next movement asks what level of danger Hogwarts permitted inside this organised form.

## 11. Risk within regulation

**Purpose:** Show that school Quidditch was supervised and medically supported without imposing modern Muggle assumptions.  
**Main claim:** Considerable physical danger remained compatible with organised Hogwarts sport: hard training, Bludgers, fouls, falls, collisions, and bad weather coexisted with refereeing, locked equipment, staff intervention, medical treatment, and authority to respond when circumstances exceeded ordinary play.  
**Evidence:** `qtta-ch06-003`, `qtta-ch06-007`, `qtta-ch06-008`, `qtta-ch06-010`, `cos-ch10-004`, `cos-ch10-005`, `poa-ch09-006`, `poa-ch09-007`, `poa-ch15-005`, `ootp-ch19-006`, `hbp-ch14-004`, `hbp-ch19-005`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** General rules plus later Hogwarts observations, several explicitly exceptional.  
**Important limitation:** Do not convert professional rules into school rules without observed correspondence. Sabotage, Dementors, unprecedented protective measures, and post-whistle violence reveal outer limits, not normal procedure.  
**Movement:** Establishes supervised risk as part of operation. The next movement uses explicit suspension to reveal what counted as normal institutional continuity.

## 12. Seasons interrupted

**Purpose:** Use exceptional years sparingly to expose the expected rhythm of ordinary years.  
**Main claim:** A match could be cancelled during a school emergency; the entire Inter-House Cup could be suspended for the Triwizard Tournament; the pitch could be repurposed; and later sanctions could remove players and force replacements. Each intervention was noticeable because House Quidditch was ordinarily expected to continue.  
**Evidence:** `cos-ch14-003`, `gof-ch12-005`, `gof-ch28-004`, `gof-ch31-002`, `ootp-ch19-003`, `ootp-ch19-007`, `ootp-ch21-005`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Post-1984 later-only events; editorial evidence for the mature normal by contrast.  
**Important limitation:** These crises must not dominate the chapter or be narrated as facts available to a 1984 author. No permanent institutional change is established from them.  
**Movement:** Shows resilience without claiming unbroken history. The penultimate movement turns from interrupted seasons to what the school retained across them.

## 13. Players, records, and inherited expectation

**Purpose:** Explain institutional memory without inventing a documentary archive.  
**Main claim:** Team photographs, remembered captains and matches, the status of captaincy, Cup calculations based on previous performance, trophy presentation, and celebrations extending into House space allowed individual service to become House memory. Team membership mattered beyond the minutes of play.  
**Evidence:** `dh-ch10-004`, `gof-ch06-004`, `poa-ch15-004`, `poa-ch15-007`, `ootp-ch30-003`, `hbp-ch06-001`, `hbp-ch24-007`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** One pre-1984 condition preserved later, plus later observations and recollections.  
**Important limitation:** No specific old Quidditch plaque, record book, or securely dated founding trophy was located. Do not construct a hall of fame or professional-alumni catalogue.  
**Movement:** Converts season results into longer memory. The conclusion can now return from remembered players to the institution that made them representative.

## 14. A recurring public expression of Hogwarts

**Purpose:** Close the institutional argument and make a restrained transition to Chapter 15.  
**Main claim:** Hogwarts did not create Quidditch, and its adoption date is unknown. Yet the mature institution fitted the school with unusual force: Houses supplied teams and inherited loyalties; the pitch supplied common ground; the school year supplied recurrence; the Cup supplied cumulative consequence; and spectatorship made triumph and failure visible to the school as a whole. By the period in which its workings can be closely observed, changes in players, equipment and circumstance had not displaced that institutional role.  
**Evidence:** synthesis of C14-01 through C14-13, especially `dh-ch10-004`, `ps-ch11-004`, `poa-ch15-007`, `gof-ch12-005`, `ootp-ch30-003`, `hbp-ch24-007`  
**Chronological status / passage mode:** Bounded editorial synthesis, explicitly separating pre-1984 facts from later observation.  
**Important limitation:** The conclusion is confined to the securely observed mature period and must not imply continuous Hogwarts institutional development across all changes in Quidditch or a proved line from the founders.  
**Movement:** Close on Hogwarts first. Only the final sentence should turn outward: this intensely local House institution was also one use of a sport organised and shared far beyond the school, supported generally by `qtta-ch07-001` and `qtta-ch08-001`. That opens Chapter 15, *Hogwarts and the Wider Wizarding World*, without previewing it at length.
