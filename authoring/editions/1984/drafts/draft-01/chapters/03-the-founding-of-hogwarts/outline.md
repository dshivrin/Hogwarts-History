# Chapter 3 Outline — The Founding of Hogwarts

Status: approved for Draft 01 by the 2026-09-20 chapter run brief.

Presentation model: continuous reconstructed historical narrative. Later-published clarifications may support pre-1984 events, but their provenance remains in adjacent hidden metadata. No post-1984 event enters the chapter.

## Opening — An Idea Becomes a Place

Bridge directly from Chapter 2's closing movement. Establish the broad age of the foundation and the absence of an exact year. Move immediately from shared intention to the practical work of creating a school.

## I. A Place Apart

Establish Scotland only at the supported national level, then the deliberate remoteness of the site. Connect distance to Muggle fear and persecution without inventing a detailed site-selection strategy or projecting later landscape features backwards.

## II. Raising Hogwarts

Treat “built together” as joint responsibility for a permanent castle, not evidence of personal masonry or known construction methods. Use the absence of engineering detail to focus on consequence: teaching gained a durable place.

## III. A School Hidden from the World

Separate founder-era purpose from later-attested magical effects. The remote location belongs to the foundation; the Muggle-facing ruin enchantment is known but undated. Exclude Unplottability and anti-Apparition claims from the founder-era account.

## IV. The First School

Show the founders seeking, bringing, and educating magical children for several years. Refuse invented first-cohort details and modern school machinery. Use Hankerton Humble only as one narrow indication that the institution required work beyond teaching.

## V. Built to Outlast Its Builders

Use the Book and Quill to show durable selection without inventing their maker or wider admissions logistics. Reconcile their selection role with the founders' personal search at the narrowest supported level. Introduce the Sorting Hat only as an attributed succession tradition.

## Closing — One School, Four Legacies

Conclude that Hogwarts made education repeatable across generations. Turn to Chapter 4 by observing that the institution preserved the founders' differences as well as their cooperation, without narrating the later rupture.
