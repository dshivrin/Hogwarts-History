## Chapter argument

Hogwarts became historically durable when the founders turned a shared
educational purpose into a secluded place, repeated teaching, and limited
institutional mechanisms that did not depend entirely on their own presence.
The documentary minimum is stronger than any picturesque reconstruction: a
remote castle, children sought and taught, several years of common operation,
and later evidence for selection and administration. The chapter treats
permanence cumulatively and stops before Houses and rupture.

## Opening

Begin with the difference between agreeing to teach and creating a school that
can continue. Give the broad age and exact-date limitation once. State the
supported sequence—place, castle, pupils, teaching—without a ceremonial
foundation scene or retrospective summary of Chapters 1 and 2.

## Historical progression

The sequence is broad date → secluded place → shared construction → enduring
concealment → repeated recruitment and teaching → limited administration and
selection mechanisms → institutional continuity. The exact internal sequence
of personal searching and Book-and-Quill operation remains unknown. The
chapter ends before cooperation breaks down.

## Treatment of uncertainty and legend

State the date limitation once. Treat Scotland as later clarification at
national scale only. Treat the ruin effect as undated, not founder-authored.
Label the relation between personal search and magical selection as bounded
inference. Present the Hat's origin as tradition. Omit the Pensieve entirely,
exclude Hogwarts-specific Unplottability, and defer modern anti-Apparition
protections.

- **Later editorial additions:** Scotland, the castle-completion context and Book/Quill installation, and Hankerton Humble

## Conclusion

Conclude that Hogwarts could persist beyond its builders without claiming that
the mature school already existed in full. Turn from one common institution to
the problem Chapter 4 owns: how the founders' differing judgements became an
enduring House structure. Do not foreshadow the rupture beyond that necessary
transition.
