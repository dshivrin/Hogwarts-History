## Executive finding

The evidence supports a functional account of Hogwarts governance but not a
formal constitution. Before 1984, headmasters can be shown imposing school-wide
rules, placing staff on probation, refusing teaching appointments, and
preserving a place at Hogwarts for an expelled pupil. These acts establish
substantial practical authority. They do not prove that the headmaster acted
alone in every matter or possessed the same powers under every administration.

Later observation reveals authority distributed through other offices. A Head
of House could determine punishment within her House after the headmaster had
set the larger institutional response. A twelve-member Board of Governors
could suspend and recall a headmaster, remove one of its own members, and—by
1997 testimony—make the final decision about reopening the school. The
procedures behind those powers remain largely unrecorded, and the best example
of suspension was compromised by coercion.

Ministry jurisdiction appears at the school boundary in several forms: a 1943
closure discussion, later criminal and pupil-discipline cases, and political
pressure over staffing and teaching. These sources reveal neither complete
school independence nor simple Ministry supremacy. The Umbridge-era decrees
are the clearest documented change because they created unprecedented internal
inspection, appointment, dismissal, and punishment powers. Their novelty
prevents them from being backdated as ordinary Hogwarts governance.

The chapter may therefore argue that Hogwarts was governed by overlapping
authorities whose boundaries became clearest under pressure. The arrangement
was practical, negotiated, and vulnerable to coercion or political change. It
must not be presented as an ancient charter or a perfectly ordered hierarchy.

- **Knowledge access:** later Dumbledore commentary about pre-1984 events.

- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`.

- **Historical conclusion:** Dippet and Dumbledore each refused Tom Riddle a

- **Primary movement:** documented later alteration.

- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`.

- **Primary movement:** conclusion.
