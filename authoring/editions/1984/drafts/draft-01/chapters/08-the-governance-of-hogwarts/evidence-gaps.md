# Chapter 8 Evidence Gaps — Revision 01

## Missing constitutional structure

- No source supplies a Hogwarts charter or complete hierarchy of authority.
- The evidence does not date the creation of the headmastership, Board of
  Governors, Heads of House, or their respective powers.
- No source connects any of these offices to a Founder.
- The method for selecting, appointing, replacing, or removing governors is not
  recorded.
- The evidence does not establish meeting rules, voting thresholds, terms of
  office, or whether the twelve-member board observed in 1993 had always kept
  that size.

## Headmaster and staff authority

- Dippet's ban and Kettleburn's repeated probation establish practical powers,
  but not their written basis or ordinary procedure.
- Two refused teaching applications demonstrate headmaster discretion in those
  cases, not an exclusive appointment power under every administration.
- Dumbledore's decision to retain Hagrid as gamekeeper does not show that he
  reversed Hagrid's expulsion or wand sanctions.
- The 1992 flying-car case shows one division of responsibility between
  headmaster and Head of House. It cannot supply a complete disciplinary
  hierarchy or tariff.

## Governor evidence

- The 1993 suspension order was reportedly produced through threats. It proves
  the existence of a governor mechanism more securely than the legitimacy of
  that particular use.
- The recall of Dumbledore and removal of Lucius Malfoy are recorded without
  procedure.
- The governors' final authority over reopening is stated during the 1997
  crisis, but the final decision itself is not shown.

## Ministry jurisdiction

- In 1943 the Ministry was discussing closure; the source does not identify a
  statute, final decision, or exact legal power.
- Dumbledore's statement that he could not overrule the Minister concerns an
  external criminal matter and cannot be expanded into universal Ministerial
  supremacy over Hogwarts.
- Fudge's description of Dumbledore's former “free rein” is a hostile political
  assessment, not a neutral constitutional definition.
- The revision of Harry's expulsion notice after Dumbledore's intervention
  proves practical negotiation, not the procedure or legal reasoning behind it.

## Post-1984 change

- The Umbridge decrees belong wholly to the later editorial layer.
- Their express novelty makes them evidence of alteration, not evidence that
  the Ministry ordinarily possessed the same internal powers before 1984.
- The Trelawney and Firenze confrontations expose a temporary division of
  powers under those decrees; they do not establish the ordinary appointment,
  dismissal, or residence rules.

## Unsupported assumptions to exclude

- a founder-created headmastership, board, or constitutional settlement;
- a stable ancient balance among headmaster, governors, and Ministry;
- governor independence merely because a document bore twelve signatures;
- unlimited headmaster authority over employment, punishment, residence, or
  external law;
- a general Ministry power inferred from one emergency or one political
  administration;
- ordinary disciplinary practice inferred from Umbridge's exceptional rules;
- formal appeal rights, judicial review, tenure, or modern administrative
  language not used by the sources.
