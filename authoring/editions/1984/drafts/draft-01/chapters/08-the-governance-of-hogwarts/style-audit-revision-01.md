## Historical writing

- The pantomime aftermath introduces authority through a concrete Hogwarts
  incident rather than an abstract constitutional survey.
- Staffing cases develop from admission to refusal, retention and resignation
  without becoming biographies.
- The flying-car incident is used only to establish distributed office
  responsibility.
- Governor evidence proceeds from formal mechanism to corruption, reversal and
  institutional-continuation authority.
- Ministry evidence moves chronologically from external pressure to negotiated
  boundaries and then to documented internal intervention.
- The Umbridge sequence explains change in governing machinery instead of
  retelling pupil adventures.

  coercion and recall; reopening; 1943 closure pressure; external legal limit;
  negotiated expulsion; political account of autonomy; unprecedented

  conditional appointment; intervention as change; layered synthesis;
  educational consequence.
- Adjacent paragraphs with substantially identical purposes: none.

  not merely another rejected application.
- Hagrid appears twice for distinct purposes: retention after sanction and a
  later attempted resignation under public pressure.
- The final two paragraphs do not repeat examples: the first defines the
  arrangement; the second states its institutional consequence.

### Chapter-level conceptual repetition

- Each of the seven outline movements answers a different question: internal
  headmaster power, staff access, delegation, governor oversight, Ministry
  jurisdiction, documented alteration, and synthesis.
- No section re-proves the same office power after its evidentiary movement.
- The governing thesis is withheld until the evidence has established its
  parts, then stated once in full.

### Cross-chapter repetition

  relevance because that material has already been substantially used.
- Founders, Houses, secrecy, castle responsiveness, and the Chamber narrative
  are not re-explained.
- Hagrid's biography is reduced to the institutional decisions required by the
  current argument.
- The Umbridge year is treated as change in authority rather than a general war
