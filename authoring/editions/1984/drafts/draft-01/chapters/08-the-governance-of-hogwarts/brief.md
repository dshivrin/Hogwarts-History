## Chapter purpose

Explain the offices, authorities, and external pressures through which
Hogwarts was governed, distinguishing routine internal administration from
governor oversight, Ministry jurisdiction, and exceptional political
intervention.

## Central historical question

Who could act for Hogwarts, through which office, and with what limits?

## Approved argument

Hogwarts was governed through overlapping authorities rather than by a single
sovereign office. The headmaster exercised broad practical control within the
school; staff offices carried delegated authority; governors could supervise
or remove the head and decide questions affecting the school's continuation;
and the Ministry intervened where law, safety, or politics crossed the school
boundary. Later crises exposed and sometimes altered this balance, but do not
prove that extraordinary controls were ordinary or ancient.

## Entry and exit

- **Entry:** continue Chapter 7's conclusion that Hogwarts authority was real
  but uneven by moving from the extent of authority to the offices that
  exercised it. Begin with a concrete Dippet-era rule, not a constitutional
  abstraction.
- **Exit:** conclude that Hogwarts's governing arrangement is most visible when
  offices conflict, then turn naturally towards the institutional consequence:
  authority shaped who might teach and what the school could require.

## Required subjects

- headmaster authority over school-wide rules, staff probation, appointment,
  refusal, retention, and resignation;
- delegated authority through Heads of House;
- governor suspension, recall, removal, consultation, and closure authority;
- Ministry involvement in closure, external legal matters, pupil discipline,
  staffing, inspection, dismissal, and sanctions;
- the Umbridge administration as documented post-1984 change rather than
  evidence of ordinary practice;
- limits created by uncertain procedure, retrospective testimony, coercion,
  and crisis conditions.

## Explicit exclusions

- founder-era origins for the headmastership or any governing office;
- a biographical survey of headmasters;
- complete curriculum history or classroom pedagogy;
- ordinary pupil discipline, prefect life, or House-point systems beyond the
  single example needed to establish delegated staff authority;
- library censorship, headmaster portraits, centaur jurisdiction already used
  in Chapter 7, wartime occupation under Snape and the Carrows, and altered-
  timeline *Cursed Child* evidence;
- any invented charter, statutory hierarchy, governor-selection procedure,
  legal formula, or stable chronology.

## Presentation model

Use the unified narrative model. Dippet-era events and other pre-1984 facts may
enter reconstructed Bagshot narration with their retrospective provenance kept
in source-access comments. Post-1984 governor and Ministry evidence must be
introduced through explicit temporal pivots and natural attribution. No
separate addendum is required.
