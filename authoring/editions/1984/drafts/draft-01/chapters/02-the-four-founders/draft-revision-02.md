# Chapter Two

## The Four Founders

### Four Names in a Long Memory

Only at the threshold of Hogwarts do its founders emerge from the scattered
world of the preceding chapter. Godric Gryffindor, Helga Hufflepuff, Rowena
Ravenclaw and Salazar Slytherin were counted among the greatest witches and
wizards of their age. Their distinction matters, but so does what they chose
to do with it: they joined their powers in the education of magical children.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-003 -->

Two witnesses provide most of the surviving outline. Professor Binns preserves
the firm public history of their common undertaking, their years of harmony
and the disagreement that emerged among them. The Sorting Hat offers a
different testimony. Traditionally associated with all four founders and
carrying their remembered preferences into the life of the school, it gives
poetic accounts of their ideals and friendships. Its songs vary, but the Hat's
long service at Hogwarts gives the tradition a place in the history as well as
in the ceremony.

The four names are much clearer than the four early lives. Godric's Hollow is
recorded as Gryffindor's birthplace. For the others, tradition supplies only
the broad landscapes of valley, glen and fen; it also associates Gryffindor
with a wild moor. These may be old traces of origin, but only Godric's Hollow
can be placed with confidence.

<!-- evidence: dh-ch16-003, gof-ch12-002 -->

Their surviving portraits are consequently made from choices rather than
childhoods. Gryffindor is remembered through courage and opposition;
Hufflepuff through labour and an unusually generous conception of teaching;
Ravenclaw through intellectual excellence; Slytherin through ambition,
cunning and an ancestry restriction that challenged the common undertaking.
None is a complete life. Together, however, they form a history of four
distinct people attempting one remarkable work.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-003, cos-ch09-004, gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

### The Work They Chose Together

The founders did more than receive pupils who happened to find them. They
sought out children who showed signs of magic, brought them to one place and
educated them. Four exceptional magical people thus made teaching a deliberate
and continuing enterprise.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-003, cos-ch09-004 -->

Binns records that they worked in harmony for several years. The Hat's songs
remember more: a common hope, friendship among all four, and particular bonds
between Gryffindor and Slytherin and between Hufflepuff and Ravenclaw.
Friendship is a more intimate claim than cooperation, but the pairings give
shape to a partnership that endured. They place differently minded founders
within genuine relationships before their standards of education began to
pull them apart.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-003, cos-ch09-004, gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

The founders' first meeting and division of work have not survived. What does
survive is the choice beneath the later structures: four accomplished people
made the education of magical children a shared responsibility and chose one
another as partners. The site, castle and first arrangements would give that
choice institutional form.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-003 -->

### Four Founders, Four Educational Ideals

The founders' remembered preferences concern qualities in pupils rather than
complete theories of education. Gryffindor valued courage; Hufflepuff, hard
work; Ravenclaw, intelligence; Slytherin, ambition and cunning. These ideals
could overlap in any child. They reveal where each founder looked for promise,
not four mutually exclusive kinds of human nature.

<!-- evidence: gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

Helga Hufflepuff's distinction lies in what she did when a pupil failed to fit
another founder's preferred measure. The Hat remembers her as willing to teach
the pupils and treat them alike. Hard work remained a quality she honoured,
but it was not merely a gate through which only the already industrious might
pass. Her ideal allowed education itself to disclose worth in children whom a
narrower selection might overlook. This is evidence of her remembered offer
as a teacher, not proof of universal admission throughout Hogwarts history.

<!-- evidence: gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

Rowena Ravenclaw's ideal was more selective, but no less educational. She is
remembered as seeking the cleverest pupils and the sharpest minds, a standard
that placed intellectual excellence at the centre of a pupil's promise. The
contrast with Hufflepuff is illuminating precisely because tradition remembers
the two women as friends. School legend also associates Ravenclaw with a lost
diadem reputed to enhance wisdom; it does not tell us what she taught, wrote or
discovered.

<!-- evidence: gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003, dh-ch29-006 -->

Godric Gryffindor enters the record with more physical definition. He had a
named birthplace, was remembered for courage, and was later described as an
accomplished conventional swordsman in an age when a wizard might defend
himself with steel as well as a wand. The old goblin-made sword bearing his
name gives material form to that remembered martial competence, though the
later history of the object cannot be carried backwards into his life.

<!-- evidence: dh-ch16-003, gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003, ext-a08-001, ext-a08-003 -->

<!-- source-access: ext-a08-001 and ext-a08-003 are later official retrospective accounts. They support the founder-era manufacture and swordsmanship claims used here, but are not presented as publications available in 1984. -->

His courage acquires its greatest historical importance in his opposition to
Slytherin. Tradition remembers the two men first as friends. Binns records
Gryffindor as Slytherin's principal opponent when ancestry became a condition
of magical education. The movement from friendship towards disagreement makes
Godric more than the emblem of a preferred virtue. It shows him applying
judgement to the purpose of the institution he had helped create.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-004, ootp-ch11-003 -->

Salazar Slytherin must likewise be seen as a founder before his doctrine is
judged. He was one of the four great magical people of the age, shared the work
of seeking and educating children, and remained in harmony with his colleagues
for years. Tradition associates him with ambition and cunning, but neither
quality explains his motives or supplies achievements the record does not
name.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-003, cos-ch09-004, gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

His ancestry restriction was different in kind. Binns records that Slytherin
distrusted pupils of Muggle parentage and wished magical education restricted
to children from all-magical families. This was a judgement about who should
be taught, not merely about the qualities he admired in those already admitted.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-004 -->

### The Limits of Agreement

The founder traditions preserve two related but distinct kinds of selection.
The first concerned the pupils each founder preferred: courage for Gryffindor,
intellect for Ravenclaw, hard work for Hufflepuff, and ambition and cunning for
Slytherin. These preferences could distinguish one founder's chosen pupils
from another's within a common school.

<!-- evidence: gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

The second question was wider: should children of Muggle parentage receive
education at Hogwarts at all? Courage, intelligence, labour, ambition and
cunning answer the question, *What promise does this pupil show?* An ancestry
rule asks first, *May this child be taught?* A child of Muggle parentage might
possess every quality the founders admired and still be excluded before any
founder had the opportunity to cultivate it.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-004, gof-ch12-002, ootp-ch11-003 -->

The restriction remained Slytherin's position; the account does not establish
that Hogwarts adopted it as policy. Gryffindor's opposition is clear, while
Hufflepuff's remembered willingness to teach widely offers a further contrast.
Ravenclaw's complete view of admission does not survive and need not be
invented to make the divergence serious.

<!-- evidence: cos-ch09-004, ootp-ch11-003 -->

Four people with different measures of promise nevertheless joined to seek and
teach magical children. Their cooperation made Hogwarts possible; their
unresolved difference over which children should be taught placed a strain
within that common purpose. The next historical question is how they gave the
purpose a site, a structure and the daily life of a functioning school.
