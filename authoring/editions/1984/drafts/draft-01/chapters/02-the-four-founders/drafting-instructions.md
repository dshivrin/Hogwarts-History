## Chapter argument

The evidence supports a history of a partnership more securely than four
conventional biographies. The chapter should move from the four founders'
unevenly recoverable backgrounds, through their common educational undertaking
and remembered ideals, to the point at which one difference tested the limits
of their agreement.

The positive centre is their shared work. Courage, intelligence, labour, and
ambition could coexist as different measures of promise. Slytherin's ancestry
restriction was different in kind because it concerned which magical children
should be allowed access to learning. Present that distinction without
inventing complete educational philosophies or a unified alternative
admissions policy.

End prospectively. Return to what the four accomplished together and lead into
Chapter 3's question: how did their shared educational purpose become a
functioning school? Do not narrate Slytherin's departure and then jump backward
to the foundation.

## Section requirements

### Four Names in a Long Memory

- Continue naturally from Chapter 1's closing question rather than summarising
  the preceding chapter.
- Introduce the four names, their collective stature, the limited origin
  evidence, and the uneven survival of personal history.
- Establish the source hierarchy once: Professor Binns supplies the firm
  historical framework, while the Sorting Hat preserves a different category
  of founder tradition and claimed memory.
- Distinguish the date of an ancient event, the date on which testimony was
  recorded, and the 1984 narrator's access to that testimony.
- Do not open four disconnected miniature biographies or map moor, glen,
  valley, and fen onto unsupported modern birthplaces.

### The Work They Chose Together

- Make the founders' common educational undertaking the chapter's affirmative
  centre.
- Establish that four exceptional magical people sought and educated magical
  children together and worked in harmony for several years.
- Attribute intimate friendship pairings to later Hat tradition; do not present
  them as independently documented relationships.
- Do not reconstruct meetings, conversations, private motives, who first
  proposed the school, or the precise sequence by which the plan emerged.
- Keep site choice, construction, concealment, recruitment machinery, opening,
  early operation, governance, and division of labour for Chapter 3.

### Four Founders, Four Educational Ideals

- Develop the founders comparatively rather than as four self-contained
  biographies. Hufflepuff and Ravenclaw may be placed in illuminating contrast,
  as may Gryffindor and Slytherin.
- Present courage, hard work, intelligence, ambition, and cunning as attributed
  preferences or traditions, not complete personalities or later House
  stereotypes projected backwards.
- Do not invent subjects taught, teaching methods, writings, offices, family
  relationships, or private philosophies.
- Use founder-associated objects only when they make a historical portrait more
  concrete. Keep them subordinate to the people and their ideas.

### The Limits of Agreement

- Distinguish preferences concerning aptitude or effort from Slytherin's
  ancestry-based restriction on access to magical learning.
- Attribute the restriction to Slytherin. Do not call it an adopted Hogwarts
  policy.
- Establish Gryffindor's opposition without inferring complete admissions
  policies for Gryffindor, Hufflepuff, or Ravenclaw.
- Present the emergence of disagreement and the serious Gryffindor–Slytherin
  argument, but do not reconstruct the argument, date the formation of any
  belief, narrate Slytherin's departure, or describe the institutional
  consequences.
- Close on the founders' shared achievement and hand the history forward to
  Chapter 3.

## Founder-specific controls

### Godric Gryffindor

- Godric's Hollow may be identified as his birthplace through the public
  historical attribution recorded in the package.
- Bravery is a later founder tradition, not a complete biography.
- His opposition to Slytherin's ancestry restriction is historically stronger
  than most personal detail, but it does not establish his entire admissions
  policy.
- Conventional duelling skill may be used only briefly and with the access
  qualification recorded in the claim table.
- The sword may appear as an old founder-associated relic with later-attested
  goblin manufacture and reputed worthiness. Do not settle goblin and wizard
  ownership claims or narrate later appearances through the Hat.

### Helga Hufflepuff

- Treat hard work and her remembered willingness to teach all pupils alike as
  later Hat tradition.
- Do not turn that tradition into proof of universal Hogwarts admission or a
  policy shared by all four founders.
- The golden cup may be mentioned only as a limited House memorial association.
  Do not present private heirloom testimony, physical provenance, or chain of
  custody as public knowledge established for the 1984 narrator.

### Rowena Ravenclaw

- Treat exceptional intelligence or sharpness of mind as an attributed pupil
  preference, not proof of her subjects, methods, writings, or total character.
- Do not infer agreement with Hufflepuff's position from their remembered
  friendship.
- The lost diadem may be presented as longstanding school lore: associated
  with wisdom, said to have vanished with Ravenclaw, and unseen in living
  memory. Do not import its hidden history, location, or later fate.

### Salazar Slytherin

- Distinguish later traditional characterisations such as ambition and cunning
  from Binns's stronger attribution of an ancestry restriction.
- Present distrust of pupils of Muggle parentage as Slytherin's documented
  position, not as proof of a school-wide rule or later House behaviour.
- The Chamber may be named only as a disputed founder legend relevant to how
  Slytherin was remembered. Reserve its substance, later confirmation, and the
  departure narrative for Chapter 5.

## Unified source treatment and the Sorting Hat

The Sorting Hat was a longstanding Hogwarts institution whose annual songs
could change. Its songs recorded in 1994 and 1995 may inform the evidence
assessment and narrative as later testimony claiming old memory. Their
particular wording remains a poetic carrier, not a founder-era transcript.

Accordingly:

- do not reproduce or closely paraphrase those later lyrics;
- do not claim that the recorded versions circulated in the founder era;
- do not invent an earlier song, performance, interview or conversation;
- identify Hat-supported material as tradition, later testimony, or claimed
  memory where that distinction matters;
- do not repeat the same access disclaimer after every Hat-supported claim.

A later source may preserve an older event without becoming contemporaneous
evidence for it. Publication after 1984 is not an exclusion criterion, but the
source's character, date, authority and limitations must remain visible in
metadata and, where historically important, through natural attribution.

## Evidence discipline

- Use the existing canonical evidence IDs and exact source records listed in
  the preparation package.
- Read the relevant YAML entry before relying on it. Inspect an original
  passage only when wording, context, access, or contradiction requires it.
- Do not use the open-question overlay as evidence. It is an editorial control
  and discovery aid whose verified facts point back to canonical records.
- Distinguish explicit evidence, reasonable synthesis, founder tradition,
  disputed legend, private information, and unsupported inference.
- Do not silently reconcile Binns and the Hat, the competing sword ownership
  accounts, or any other qualified source relationship.
- Preserve unresolved questions by omitting unsupported detail or stating a
  concise limit where necessary. Do not solve them through invention.
- If a new material contradiction is found, keep the research layer unchanged,
  qualify or omit the affected prose, and report it at the end.

Every factual paragraph or independently supported factual unit must be
followed immediately by working evidence metadata in the established form:

```html

```

Use only evidence IDs that support the associated factual unit. These comments
are development metadata and should not intrude into the reader-facing prose.
Do not add modern academic citations to the prose itself.

## Style requirements

- Match the current Draft-01 style lock and Chapter 1's latest working final:
  scholarly in judgement, conversational in presentation, and concrete in its
  magical history.
- Write as an in-universe British magical historian, not as a modern scholar,
  fan encyclopaedia, or narrator of the novels.
- Let people, choices, objects, and recorded acts precede analytical
  conclusions.
- State a meaningful uncertainty once, beside the claim it limits, and then
  continue the history.
- Use restrained dry intelligence where natural. Avoid constant wit,
  melodrama, pseudo-medieval diction, and abstract academic scaffolding.
- Each paragraph should normally advance one historical idea; each section
  must move the central argument forward.
- Do not repeat Chapter 1's account of pre-Hogwarts education except for the
  brief continuity needed to begin the new chapter.
- Hufflepuff's house-elf association may appear only as a brief, specifically
  attributed claim from the 2007 author interview preserved in `ext-b10-001`.
  Keep it distinct from her pupil-facing educational ideal, do not make it an
  independently documented founder-era policy, and reserve the wider history
  of magical labour.

## Chapter boundary

Reserve for Chapter 3:

- site selection, concealment, castle construction, and completion;
- how pupils were found or brought to the school in practice;
- the Quill of Acceptance and Book of Admittance;
- opening, first teaching arrangements, governance, early operation, and
  institutional division of labour.

Reserve for later chapters:

- Chapter 4: formation and development of the Houses, Sorting practice, and
  founder preferences as institutional selection;
- Chapter 5: the full dispute, Slytherin's departure, the Chamber narrative,
  and consequences of the rupture;
- Chapter 12: fuller founder memorials and portraits;
- Chapter 13: the Hat's annual songs and ceremonial development;
- Chapter 15 or later: any properly supported public treatment of the
  goblin–wizard ownership dispute.

Do not turn a short necessary reference into advance narration of reserved
material.
