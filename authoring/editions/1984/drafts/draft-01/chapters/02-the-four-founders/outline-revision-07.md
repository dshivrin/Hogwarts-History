## Chapter argument

The founders are recoverable less through complete biographies than through a
shared undertaking and a small set of remembered educational judgements. Their
cooperation is historically firmer than the Hat's intimate friendship
traditions. Courage, effort, intellect, and ambition describe preferred
qualities; Slytherin's ancestry condition differed in kind because it proposed
to exclude otherwise magical children from education. The chapter ends at that
limit of agreement, before narrating the rupture.

## Opening

Carry forward Chapter 1's final question by naming the four people who chose to
create the school. Establish Binns as the source of the historical framework
and the Hat as a later poetic carrier of founder tradition. Make this source
distinction once, then rely on natural attribution rather than repeating it in
every paragraph.

## Historical progression

The chapter moves from named actors and source limits, to common work, to
distinct remembered judgements, to the point at which one judgement challenged
the reach of the whole undertaking. It does not invent when each preference
formed. It ends after establishing disagreement and before Slytherin's
departure.

## Treatment of uncertainty and legend

Treat moor, valley, glen, and fen as poetic origin traditions; only Godric's
Hollow is a named birthplace in the reviewed public history. Hat testimony may
preserve founder memory but is not a transcript. Keep the diadem to one
sentence of longstanding school tradition and the sword to its bounded
association. Exclude the unresolved ownership dispute, private cup provenance,
Helena narrative, lineage material, Chamber, and house-elf claim.
