## Purpose

Complete the book's reconstructed historical foundation by tracing the
overlapping careers and successive headmasterships of Armando Dippet and Albus
Dumbledore. The chapter asks what changed at Hogwarts across their periods of
leadership, what remained inherited, and which documented consequences can be
associated with the judgement of either man without treating the school as the
creation of a single Headmaster.

## Central historical question

What changed at Hogwarts across the Dippet and Dumbledore headmasterships, and
what can be attributed to the men who held the office rather than to the school
they inherited?

## Working proposition to test

The evidence does not preserve a clean handover or support a simple progress
narrative. It preserves a long overlap: Dumbledore taught under Dippet,
encountered Tom Riddle as a prospective pupil, challenged the accepted account
of the 1943 crisis and persuaded Dippet to retain Hagrid. Dippet's administration
made consequential decisions about staff, pupil residence and access to
teaching; Dumbledore later inherited several of the people and problems already
visible in that record.

The working proposition for Revision 01 is therefore: **the change from Dippet
to Dumbledore is best understood not as the replacement of a failed school by a
modern one, but as a transfer within a continuing institution. Dumbledore used
the inherited office in a recognisably personal way—especially through
case-specific admission, sanctuary and refusal—while his public stature made
his presence consequential to Hogwarts during war. The record supports a
pattern of individual decisions, not a formal programme of reform.**

## Chronological and source-access boundary

The reconstructed historical boundary is 1984. Later sources may carry
pre-1984 events, but they remain later-carried testimony or later editorial
history and must not be silently voiced as Bagshot's demonstrated knowledge.

The recoverable sequence is:

1. an undated Dippet-era pantomime while Dumbledore was a young
   Transfiguration teacher;
2. Dumbledore acting as a Hogwarts teacher in the 1938 approach to Tom Riddle;
3. the 1943 Chamber crisis under Dippet, with Dumbledore still a teacher;
4. Riddle's first post-school teaching request to Dippet, shortly after his
   school career and therefore only bounded rather than independently dated;
5. Dumbledore's publicly remembered defeat of Grindelwald in 1945;
6. an undocumented succession interval, after which a later Riddle request is
   addressed to Dumbledore as Headmaster;
7. Dumbledore securely in office by the Lupin admission, placed by established
   adjacent working chronology in the 1971 intake;
8. Trelawney's pre-1981 appointment and sanctuary, whose exact date is not
   preserved in the selected evidence;
9. Hogwarts's remembered wartime reputation through Voldemort's fall in 1981;
10. no securely documented new school event between 1981 and the 1984 boundary.

No date or mechanism may be supplied for Dippet's accession, Dippet's departure
or Dumbledore's appointment. Retirement, death, dismissal, election, governor
action, Ministry action and personal nomination remain unsupported.

## Required analytical distinctions

- the office of Headmaster versus the person holding it;
- formal decision versus persuasion or personal influence;
- an inherited practice versus a new decision;
- an exceptional accommodation versus a general rule;
- an individual's public reputation versus legal headmasterial power;
- wartime deterrence versus a documented defensive programme;
- event chronology versus source publication and knowledge access;
- later correction of a false account versus retrospective condemnation of an
  actor who lacked the correcting evidence.

## Supported chapter subjects

- Dippet's documented administration through the pantomime aftermath,
  Kettleburn's continued employment, the 1943 crisis and Riddle's first teaching
  request.
- Dumbledore's long pre-headship position inside the school, including his
  teaching role, his dealings with Riddle and his advocacy for Hagrid.
- The absence of a recoverable succession mechanism, using the two Riddle
  teaching requests to bracket rather than fill the gap.
- Dumbledore's refusal of Voldemort's later request as an early securely
  documented headmasterial decision with a lasting school consequence.
- Lupin's admission and accommodation as a chronological and biographical act
  of Dumbledore's headship, not a repeat of Chapters 17 and 18's safety or
  authority analysis.
- Trelawney's appointment and sanctuary as a second, differently structured
  case of individual protection within Hogwarts.
- Hagrid, Lupin and Trelawney as a concrete pattern to test, while withholding
  any claim of formal inclusion policy, steady liberalisation or universal
  eligibility.
- Dumbledore's personal public standing, the 1945 achievement that preceded
  his headship, and later participant testimony linking his reputation to
  Hogwarts's wartime safety.
- The mixed inheritances left by individual decisions: retained people,
  continuing rules, altered physical arrangements and the Defence post's
  instability.

## Relationship to Chapters 9, 10, 15, 17, 18 and 19

- **Chapter 9** owns curriculum, examination and subject history. Chapter 20
  uses the two Riddle teaching requests only as a chronological bracket and as
  decisions about institutional access.
- **Chapter 10** owns ordinary pupil life and the pantomime as communal
  Christmas experience. Chapter 20 uses the same episode only to place
  Dumbledore within Dippet's staff and to show one rule and one staff relation
  crossing administrations.
- **Chapter 15** owns the school–Hogsmeade boundary. Chapter 20 does not analyse
  the Lupin tunnel as boundary management; it asks what Dumbledore chose to
  authorise and how that choice fits his tenure.
- **Chapter 17** owns danger, containment, breach and response. Chapter 20
  mentions the Lupin breach only as a limit on what Dumbledore's continued
  accommodation can prove, and the Chamber only as an event within Dippet's
  administration.
- **Chapter 18** owns the constitutional analysis of headship and already shows
  that individual judgement matters. Chapter 20 converts that proposition into
  a chronological history of these two administrations, with special emphasis
  on overlap, succession uncertainty and what one period passed into the next.
- **Chapter 19** owns modern Hogwarts as an institution under public, national
  and wartime pressure. Chapter 20 takes up its final question by examining the
  people through whom those pressures were met, without repeating the modernity
  survey.

## Editorial notes

- Keep the chronology clear without manufacturing dates between its secure
  points.
- Prefer decisions and consequences to adjectives such as progressive,
  liberal, weak, modernising or inclusive.
- Let the first and second Riddle teaching requests carry the transition
  structurally; they show the office changing hands without pretending that
  the handover itself survives.
- Give Dippet enough ordinary administration that 1943 does not become his
  entire headship.
- Use Dumbledore's earlier teaching career only to explain continuity and his
  later institutional choices.
- The eventual prose should remain a history of Hogwarts. Every biographical
  detail must change the reader's understanding of the school.
