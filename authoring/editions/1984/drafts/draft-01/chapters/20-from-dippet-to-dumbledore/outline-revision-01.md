## Proposed chapter argument

The transition from Armando Dippet to Albus Dumbledore was a transfer within a
continuing institution. Dumbledore had served for years inside Dippet's
Hogwarts, first appearing as a young Transfiguration teacher and later acting
for the school in the admission of Tom Riddle. During the 1943 crisis, the two
men occupied different but connected positions: Dumbledore suspected the
accepted account and advocated for Hagrid; Dippet held the office and authorised
Hagrid's continued place at Hogwarts.

The handover itself is not recoverable. Riddle's two applications to teach
place Dippet in office at the first and Dumbledore at the second. They bracket
succession without supplying its year or mechanism. This absence should shape
the narrative rather than be repaired by inference.

As Headmaster, Dumbledore made several consequential decisions about who could
enter or remain within Hogwarts. His refusal of Voldemort's later request was
followed by lasting instability in the Defence post. Lupin's admission and
Trelawney's sanctuary, considered beside the earlier Hagrid outcome, show a
repeated willingness to preserve an individual's place through exceptional
arrangements. The pattern is personal and case-specific, not a documented
programme of inclusion.

Dumbledore also brought a public stature formed before his securely documented
headship. His 1945 victory over Grindelwald became part of his public identity;
later participants linked Voldemort's fear of him to Hogwarts's wartime
reputation. The chapter's conclusion is therefore mixed inheritance rather
than progress: people, restrictions, accommodations, dangers and consequences
crossed the change of office, and individual judgement altered Hogwarts without
remaking it from first principles.

### Paragraph plan

### Paragraph plan

    after crisis, exceptional arrangements under Dumbledore, and instability

25. Answer the comparison directly. Dippet and Dumbledore both made

    changed Hogwarts without severing it from Dippet's institution.
