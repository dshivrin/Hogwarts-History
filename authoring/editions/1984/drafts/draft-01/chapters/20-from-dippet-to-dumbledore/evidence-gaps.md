# Chapter 20 Evidence Gaps

## The two tenures

- No selected source dates Armando Dippet's accession, identifies his
  predecessor or states how he obtained the headship.
- The Dippet-era pantomime establishes Dumbledore as a young Transfiguration
  teacher within Dippet's staff, but its year is not preserved.
- No complete staff list, annual record or general administrative history
  survives for either tenure.
- The evidence therefore supports overlap and continuity, not two complete or
  symmetrical biographies.

## The 1943 crisis

- Riddle's diary first supplies a self-serving and false account of the
  attacks; his later confession corrects the accusation against Hagrid.
- No neutral contemporary inquiry, governor minute, Ministry order or complete
  disciplinary file survives.
- The evidence does not identify who formally ordered Hagrid's expulsion or
  wand-breaking, what procedure was followed, or which body held final closure
  authority.
- Dumbledore suspected Riddle and persuaded Dippet to keep and train Hagrid.
  The resulting appointment was Dippet's formal decision; it must not be
  attributed to Dumbledore alone.
- Dippet's conduct cannot be judged through knowledge supplied only by Riddle's
  later confession. Nothing selected proves incompetence, cowardice or removal
  from office because of the crisis.

## The succession gap

- No source supplies the date, process or cause by which Dumbledore succeeded
  Dippet.
- Riddle's first teaching request places Dippet in office after Riddle left
  school. His later request places Dumbledore in office. These scenes bracket
  the change but do not date it.
- The first request is only relatively dated. The later memory is itself
  positioned ten years after an otherwise undated memory and cannot establish
  a secure calendar year.
- Dippet's retirement, death, dismissal or resignation, a governor vote,
  Ministry intervention and Dumbledore's nomination are all unsupported.
- Dumbledore's 1945 defeat of Grindelwald is a secure point in his public
  biography, not evidence that he was already Headmaster or that the victory
  caused his appointment.

## Dumbledore's individual decisions

- Hagrid, Lupin and Trelawney supply three differently structured cases of
  retention, admission and sanctuary. They do not establish a written
  inclusion policy, a new legal entitlement or universal eligibility.
- The Lupin evidence does not identify every consultation, permission, cost,
  builder or protective measure involved. It preserves Dumbledore's decision
  and the broad accommodation.
- Adjacent working chronology places Lupin's entry in 1971, but the selected
  accounts themselves establish the date relatively through age and school
  cohort rather than naming the calendar year.
- The near-fatal breach of the Lupin arrangement prevents treating the case as
  proof of a comprehensive safety reform. The response preserved secrecy and
  continued the accommodation; it did not eliminate the risk.
- Trelawney's interview occurred before Voldemort's fall in 1981, but the
  selected evidence does not name its exact date, her contractual status or
  the formal mechanism of appointment.
- Dumbledore's reasons for offering Trelawney sanctuary are preserved by later
  testimony and official retrospective history. They may be attributed, but
  not converted into a general institutional doctrine.

## Teaching requests and the Defence post

- Dippet's refusal of the young Riddle and Dumbledore's refusal of the later
  Voldemort are decisions about institutional access, not a complete history of
  Defence Against the Dark Arts.
- The evidence associates the later refusal with annual turnover in the post,
  but does not establish the mechanism of that consequence.
- Chapter 9 owns the curriculum and staffing history. Chapter 20 cannot expand
  these two scenes into a subject survey or theory of a curse.

## Public stature and war

- Later testimony establishes that Voldemort feared Dumbledore and that
  Hogwarts was remembered as one of the few safe places during the First
  Wizarding War.
- No selected evidence identifies fortifications, evacuation plans, emergency
  rules, attacks on the castle, changes to teaching or a continuity-of-schooling
  programme.
- The testimony supports reputation and possible personal deterrence, not a
  documented defensive system or proof that ordinary life was unaffected.
- The Chocolate Frog card and Doge obituary are later carriers. They establish
  public remembrance of Dumbledore's 1945 victory and stature, not what every
  contemporary knew or why Hogwarts appointed him.
- Snape's wartime service and Dumbledore's intervention at a later proceeding
  are deferred because the selected entry does not securely place the relevant
  school appointment or proceeding before 1984.

## The 1984 boundary and knowledge access

- Much of the recoverable history is carried by later memories, confessions,
  testimony and official retrospective writing.
- Later-carried pre-1984 events may enter the unified history only with their
  source status preserved. Private posthumous explanations cannot be voiced as
  demonstrated Bagshot knowledge.
- No securely documented new Hogwarts event between Voldemort's fall in 1981
  and the 1984 boundary materially advances this chapter.
- The chapter may end with inherited consequences still visible by 1984, but
  it may not import later conduct to complete Dumbledore's character.

## Ownership and continuity controls

- Chapter 9 retains curriculum and the full Defence staffing history.
- Chapter 10 retains the pantomime as pupil and Christmas experience.
- Chapter 15 retains the Lupin passage as a school–Hogsmeade boundary.
- Chapter 17 retains the Chamber and Lupin cases as danger, containment and
  breach.
- Chapter 18 retains the constitutional powers of the headship and the general
  proposition that individual judgement matters.
- Chapter 19 retains twentieth-century institutional reach, public pressure
  and the wartime significance of Hogwarts.
- Chapter 20 advances the chronological overlap, undocumented succession,
  personal pattern of case-specific decisions and mixed inheritance across the
  two administrations.

## Claims prohibited in Revision 01

- Dippet failed, was weak, or lost the headship because of the Chamber crisis.
- Dumbledore replaced a discredited regime or created modern Hogwarts.
- The succession occurred in a named year or by a named mechanism.
- The 1945 victory made Dumbledore Headmaster.
- Dumbledore established a general inclusion, sanctuary or welfare policy.
- Exceptional accommodations prove steady liberalisation or progress.
- Hogwarts possessed a documented wartime defence programme.
- Voldemort's fear alone made the castle safe.
- The Defence post's instability had a demonstrated magical mechanism.
- Private or post-1984 revelations were available to the reconstructed 1984
  narrator.

## Research verdict

The corpus supports a bounded institutional history of succession without a
surviving handover. Its strongest evidence is a long overlap: Dumbledore served
under Dippet, dealt with Riddle before and during the 1943 crisis, and influenced
an outcome that Dippet formally authorised. Two later teaching requests place
the office first in Dippet's hands and then in Dumbledore's without filling the
interval. Under Dumbledore, documented admission, sanctuary and refusal
decisions form a personal, case-specific pattern, while his earlier public
stature affected Hogwarts's wartime reputation. The evidence supports mixed
inheritance and individual judgement, not a ranking of the men or a programme
of reform.
