## Chapter purpose

Chapter 20 traces the overlapping careers and successive headmasterships of
Armando Dippet and Albus Dumbledore. Its historical task is not to rank the two
men or construct complete biographies. It asks what changed at Hogwarts across
their administrations, what passed from one into the other, and which
consequences can securely be associated with personal judgement rather than
with the office or institution alone.

## Source-access decision

The chronological spine is principally later carried. Memories and later
testimony preserve Dumbledore's 1938 visit to Riddle, the 1943 crisis, both
teaching requests, the Lupin accommodation, the Trelawney interview and the
First Wizarding War. Official retrospective histories preserve the Dippet-era
pantomime, Lupin's admission and Trelawney's sanctuary. These sources can
establish pre-1984 events in a unified history, but their carrier and limits
must remain visible in evidence metadata and natural attribution.

The 1991 Chocolate Frog card is later observation of public reputation. It may
confirm that the 1945 victory belonged to Dumbledore's established public
identity, but it cannot date his accession. Doge's later obituary is useful
corroboration but remains an admiring retrospective. Private posthumous
explanation of Dumbledore's career choice is deferred rather than converted
into omniscient motive.

## Executive finding

The evidence supports continuity before contrast. Dumbledore was not an
outsider arriving to replace Dippet's Hogwarts: he was already a young teacher
within Dippet's staff, later acted for the school in the 1938 approach to Tom
Riddle, and remained a teacher during the 1943 Chamber crisis. The pantomime
episode and Kettleburn's long employment also show rules and people crossing
the boundary between the two administrations.

The 1943 crisis is the strongest point of direct interaction. Dippet confronted
the possible closure of the school with incomplete information and refused
Riddle's request to remain over the summer. Riddle secured Hagrid's expulsion
through a false accusation. Dumbledore suspected Riddle and persuaded Dippet to
retain and train Hagrid as gamekeeper. That outcome must be divided accurately:
Dumbledore supplied advocacy; Dippet made the formal decision.

The transition itself is missing. Riddle's first request to teach places Dippet
in office after Riddle's school career; the later request places Dumbledore in
office. The first refusal rested on Riddle's youth. The second confronted a
changed applicant whom Dumbledore recognised and refused. The resulting annual
turnover in Defence staffing gives the later decision a durable institutional
consequence, although its mechanism is not established. The two encounters
bracket succession without dating or explaining it.

Dumbledore's later headship is most securely characterised through particular
decisions. Lupin was admitted with exceptional arrangements; after those
arrangements were breached, the accommodation continued under secrecy rather
than becoming evidence of risk eliminated. Trelawney received a post and
sanctuary after an interview Dumbledore had initially approached sceptically.
Together with Dumbledore's earlier advocacy for Hagrid, these cases show a
repeated willingness to preserve a person's place within Hogwarts. They do not
establish a formal inclusion programme or a universal rule.

Dumbledore's public standing also mattered to the school. His 1945 defeat of
Grindelwald preceded the securely documented part of his headship and became a
defining public achievement. Later participants independently associated
Voldemort's fear of Dumbledore with Hogwarts's reputation as one of the few safe
places during the First Wizarding War. This establishes personal reputation as
an institutional fact, but no wartime defensive programme.

The strongest supported chapter argument is therefore: **the change from
Dippet to Dumbledore was a transfer within a continuing institution, not the
replacement of a failed school by a modern one. Dippet's administration made
consequential decisions under pressure; Dumbledore's long service within that
administration shaped the people and problems he inherited. As Headmaster,
Dumbledore used the office through case-specific admission, sanctuary and
refusal, while his public stature affected Hogwarts's wartime standing. The
record supports personal judgement and mixed inheritance, not a programme of
reform or a simple narrative of progress.**

- **Historical conclusion:** In 1938 Dumbledore approached Tom Riddle on behalf

  admired Riddle, while Dumbledore maintained a private watch. The relationship
  that would matter in 1943 and under Dumbledore's headship therefore began
  within Dippet's administration.
- **Event chronology:** 1938 through Riddle's school years.
- **Knowledge access:** later-carried memory and retrospective testimony.
- **Supporting evidence:** `hbp-ch13-003`, `hbp-ch13-004`, `hbp-ch17-004`.
- **Classification:** supported institutional chronology.
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`.
- **Limitations:** no Riddle childhood biography, admissions survey or claim
  that Dumbledore knew Riddle's later course.
- **Primary section:** Dumbledore Inside Dippet's Hogwarts.

### C20-03 — The 1943 outcome joined Dippet's formal authority to Dumbledore's influence

  Dumbledore's alone: advocacy and formal decision were distinct.
- **Event chronology:** 1943, with Riddle's confession carried later.
- **Knowledge access:** interested diary memory, later confession and later

- **Supporting evidence:** `hbp-ch17-005`, `cos-ch13-005`, `cos-ch13-007`,

- **Historical conclusion:** The 1945 defeat of Grindelwald became a defining

### C20-09 — The transition left mixed inheritances rather than a simple progress narrative

- **Historical conclusion:** Dippet's period left a prohibition, retained staff,
  Hagrid's place at Hogwarts and the unresolved consequences of the Riddle
  crisis. Dumbledore's period added exceptional accommodations, protective
  appointments, a consequential refusal and a wartime reputation. Some
  decisions preserved people, some preserved rules, and some produced new
  instability. Change crossed the succession without becoming a coherent
  reform programme.
- **Event chronology:** Dippet tenure through the 1984 boundary.
- **Knowledge access:** bounded editorial synthesis of C20-01 through C20-08.
- **Supporting evidence:** `beedle-ch02-005`, `beedle-ch02-006`,

  `ps-ch04-005`.
- **Classification:** supported institutional interpretation.
- **Disposition:** `advance_to_outline`.
- **Limitations:** no ranking of the two men, no failed-to-modernised model and
  no claim of comprehensive transformation.
- **Primary section:** What One Headship Leaves Another.
