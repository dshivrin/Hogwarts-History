## Chapter purpose

Reconstruct Hogwarts as an educational institution: how pupils enter, how a
seven-year education is organised, what its common and optional curriculum
contains, how teachers instruct, how knowledge and practical skill are
assessed, and how examination results govern advanced study.

## Central historical question

What educational structure carried a magical child from admission to advanced
qualification, and how much of that structure can be established before the
1984 reconstruction boundary?

## Research argument

The available evidence supports a staged education rather than a mere sequence
of spells. Later records show entry at about eleven, a seven-year course, a
common first-year foundation, elective choice after second year, public
O.W.L. examinations in fifth year, and selective N.E.W.T.-level study in the
final two years. Teaching combined books, lectures, essays, demonstration,
supervised practice, correction, and practical examination, but no single
lesson can be treated as the school's universal pedagogy. Surviving historical
points establish continuity in particular subjects, textbooks, and
qualifications; they do not permit a complete curriculum history.

## Entry and exit

- **Entry:** move from Chapter 8's authorities to the educational act they
  enabled, beginning with the acceptance letter and its enclosed first-year
  list.
- **Exit:** conclude that Hogwarts joined common formation to later
  specialisation and increasingly consequential assessment, then move towards
  the pupils who lived through that structure without narrating their daily
  life here.

## Required subjects

- magical selection, invitation, acceptance, normal entry age, and support for
  Muggle-born families;
- the seven-year structure and the limits of the evidence for its age;
- the first-year book and equipment list;
- the common curriculum, compulsory flying, electives, and the point at which
  choices begin;
- final-year specialised offerings and N.E.W.T.-level study;
- books, lectures, essays, demonstration, supervised practical work,
  correction, homework, specialist facilities, and safety measures;
- ordinary examinations, O.W.L.s, N.E.W.T.s, external examiners, grades,
  course thresholds, and career-linked subject advice;
- specific points of curriculum continuity and documented change.

## Explicit exclusions

- dormitories, common rooms, meals, friendships, leisure, weekends, ordinary
  timetables, routine movement, and the felt pressure of school life;
- a renewed account of Houses, headmaster powers, staff appointment, governors,
  Ministry jurisdiction, or institutional discipline;
- a second explanation of the Book and Quill mechanism already given in
  Chapter 3; Chapter 9 uses only the distinction between selection and later
  notification;
- a subject-by-subject encyclopedia or biographies of professors;
- a complete founder-era, medieval, or century-by-century curriculum;
- universal teaching principles inferred from one professor or one class;
- Harry-era textbooks, procedures, or thresholds silently projected into 1984;
- authorial planning notes treated as in-world curriculum history.
