# Chapter 9 Evidence Gaps — Revision 01

## Admissions

- No admission application, appeal process, entrance examination, or complete
  administrative workflow is documented.
- The official later account says the Quill reacts when a child first shows
  magic, while a preserved 2000 interview transcript says it detects magical
  birth. The common core is an enchanted Hogwarts register; the trigger cannot
  be harmonised.
- The normal invitation age is supported by later commentary and individual
  cases, not by a pre-1984 school regulation preserved in the corpus.
- A child's entry in the register is not itself evidence of acceptance,
  attendance, or the logistics by which every family responds.
- A childhood memory says Muggle-born families receive an explanatory school
  visit. The witness was himself a child, so the scope and formal procedure are
  unknown.

## Seven-year structure and curriculum

- The clearest seven-year statement is Harry-era testimony. It does not date
  the origin of the arrangement.
- The seven compulsory subjects, compulsory flying, elective minimum, and
  conditional Alchemy offering come from a retrospective official summary
  aligned with the Harry-era school. They cannot be silently backdated.
- The evidence does not establish whether every core subject continued
  unchanged through every year or whether identical timetables applied to all
  Houses.
- The precise point at which particular electives could later be dropped is
  incompletely documented.
- No source supports a founder-era seven-year programme, modern subject
  departments, O.W.L.s, N.E.W.T.s, or a medieval timetable.

## Books and materials

- The 1991 first-year list is direct school-document evidence for that intake,
  not proof that the same editions or titles were prescribed in 1984 or earlier.
- A book in a pupil's possession or in the library is not necessarily a
  required text; Revision 01 advances only explicit lists, assigned reading,
  or an approved-textbook statement.
- *Fantastic Beasts* can be related securely to its 1927 publication and
  long-approved Hogwarts status because both scan pages were inspected. The
  evidence still does not show whether every year group was required to buy it.

## Teaching method

- No school-wide pedagogical code or formal teaching standard survives.
- Charms, Herbology, Defence, History of Magic, and later Charms scenes show a
  range of methods, but each remains evidence for that professor, subject,
  class, and year.
- House points appear inside lessons, but their wider disciplinary and social
  meaning belongs to Chapters 4, 8, and 10.
- Greenhouses, live creatures, classroom animals, and dangerous plants show
  the educational use of specialist facilities and materials; they do not
  establish uniform risk policy.
- Umbridge's 1995 theory-only Defence course is a documented later departure,
  not evidence of ordinary pre-1984 practice or of Ministry control before the
  intervention described in Chapter 8.

## Assessment and progression

- First- and third-year examination scenes establish a repeated mixture of
  written and practical assessment in the 1990s, but not its date of origin or
  an invariant annual form.
- The Marauders-era O.W.L. survives as one Pensieve memory. Its exact date is
  inferred, and it cannot establish every procedure shown in 1996.
- The later O.W.L. timetable, Great Hall arrangement, anti-cheating measures,
  external examiners, and result delivery must remain visibly post-1984.
- No selected source supplies the complete N.E.W.T. examination procedure.
  The evidence establishes N.E.W.T.s as the highest Hogwarts qualification and
  shows access to advanced courses, not a full final-examination handbook.
- Teacher-specific entry thresholds and career requirements can change. One
  Potions threshold changes with the professor, which prevents a universal
  school-wide minimum from being inferred.
- Career advice is directly observed for fifth-years and specific occupations;
  the corpus does not support a complete vocational-guidance system.

## Historical development

- Surviving points establish particular continuities: *Fantastic Beasts* as a
  Hogwarts text from its 1927 publication, a long Defence teaching tenure,
  and O.W.L./N.E.W.T. use among earlier generations.
- These points do not fill the intervals between 1927, the mid-century, the
  Marauders' school years, 1984, and the 1990s.
- Dumbledore's later testimony says he was disinclined to continue Divination
  before appointing Trelawney. It proves contemplated discontinuation, not an
  earlier abolition or a complete departmental history.
- The evidence does not date the first appearance of most subjects, the
  creation of departments, or the adoption of the examination system.

## Cross-chapter risks

- Chapters 2 and 3 own founder purpose and the founding; Chapter 4 owns Houses;
  Chapter 8 owns authority and staffing power.
- Chapter 10 owns the felt experience of homework pressure, friendship,
  schedules, meals, dormitories, leisure, possessions, ordinary discipline,
  and daily routine.
- Chapter 11 owns library history and censorship; Chapter 13 owns ceremonies;
  later specialist chapters own detailed subject lore, teacher biographies,
  and wartime governance.

## Research-layer issues discovered

- No canonical evidence file was modified.
- The `fb-ch00-003` extraction summary understates the inspected page's
  chronological precision: the page says the textbook was approved from
  publication, and `fb-ch01-001` dates publication to 1927. Revision 01 records
  this verified relationship in authoring metadata rather than altering the
  protected research layer.
