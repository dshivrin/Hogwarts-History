## Executive finding

The evidence supports a coherent educational framework but not an ancient or
unchanging curriculum. Later accounts identify prospective pupils through an
enchanted register, normally invite them at about eleven, and place them in a
seven-year course. The detailed Harry-era structure begins with a common body
of magical subjects and compulsory flying, adds elective study after second
year, culminates in O.W.L.s after fifth year, and restricts sixth- and
seventh-year N.E.W.T. work according to results and subject thresholds.

Instruction was neither wholly bookish nor wholly practical. Selected lessons
show reading, lecture, note-taking, essays, demonstration, repeated wand
practice, supervised work with dangerous living material, correction, and
follow-up exercises. These examples justify a varied account of teaching, not
a universal Hogwarts pedagogy. Ordinary year-end assessment also mixed papers
and practical tasks. Public O.W.L.s were administered by outside examiners in
the later record, and their grades affected advanced study and career-linked
subject choices.

Historical depth survives only in points. *Fantastic Beasts* was published in
1927 and the inspected foreword says it had been an approved Hogwarts text
since publication. A long-serving Defence professor, earlier O.W.L. and
N.E.W.T. candidates, and a Marauders-era O.W.L. establish further continuity.
Dumbledore's pre-1984 willingness to discontinue Divination and Umbridge's
post-1984 theory-only Defence course document change, but they do not supply a
complete curricular history. The chapter may therefore explain a staged,
mixed-method education while preserving both chronology and the large silences.
