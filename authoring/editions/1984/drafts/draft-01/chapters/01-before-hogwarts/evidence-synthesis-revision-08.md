## Chapter Purpose

Explain what the surviving record can and cannot establish about magical
knowledge, childhood, and relations with Muggles before Hogwarts, then show why
the founders' decision to gather pupils in a concealed and enduring place was
an institutional change. The chapter begins with a dispersed magical society
and ends at the threshold of the founders' undertaking; founder biography,
construction, Houses, and the later rupture remain outside its scope.

## Consolidated Claims

### C1-08-01 — The pre-school record is fragmentary

- **Claim:** No reviewed evidence establishes a general British system of magical education before Hogwarts. What survives concerns scattered objects, crafts, encounters, and later comparisons rather than an earlier curriculum, school network, or ordinary course of childhood instruction.
- **Classification:** established
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Absence of a surviving system is not proof that children received no instruction or that every generation rediscovered magic independently.
- **Primary section:** Before the School

### C1-08-02 — Skilled magical arts preceded or surrounded the foundation

- **Claim:** The evidence supports an early-medieval culture of skilled magical practice: broom flight is fixed to 962, an artefact older than Hogwarts bears modified Saxon runes, and wandmaking traditions preserve ancient claims and firmer medieval craft evidence.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** The uncertain foundation date prevents a claim that the 962 record precedes the school. The Pensieve does not prove a prior school or magical function for its runes. Wand traditions differ in authority and do not establish an uninterrupted business or founder-era teaching system.
- **Primary section:** Old Arts
