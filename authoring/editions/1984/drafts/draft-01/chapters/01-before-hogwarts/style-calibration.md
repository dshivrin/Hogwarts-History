# Chapter 1 Style Calibration

Status: REVISION-01 FINDINGS AND PROPOSALS — NOT GLOBAL STYLE AUTHORITY

This file applies the Draft-01 style lock to Chapter 1 and does not override
it. The final sections now record lessons from the first complete revision for
human review.

## Historical distance

The narrator should sound closest to a historian examining an uneven archive,
not a storyteller reconstructing a lost scene. Sparse evidence calls for firm
control of categories: state the few established conditions plainly, mark
later analogies as later, and allow silence to remain. Avoid faux eyewitness
detail, atmospheric antiquarianism, and phrases that make “a thousand years
ago” sound more precise than it is.

## Uncertainty density

Qualification will be unusually frequent because the central educational
question is largely unanswered. Concentrate uncertainty at decision points
rather than attaching “perhaps” to every sentence. A section may establish its
evidentiary limit once, make two or three bounded observations within it, and
return to the consequence. Vary confidence language and reserve the strongest
formulae for `cos-ch09-003`.

## World familiarity

Treat magic, wands, magical children, Muggles, the International Statute of
Secrecy, Hogwarts, and the four House names as familiar to an in-universe
reader. Explain the historical operation or chronology of a concept only when
the argument needs it. Do not translate ordinary magical life for Muggle
readers or explain who the founders are before Chapter 2.

## Narrative energy

The chapter's motion should come from a tightening historical problem:

```text
an unrecoverable educational landscape
→ limited evidence of how learning can occur outside schools
→ a society visible to and entangled with Muggles
→ fear makes protected distance consequential
→ an enduring educational centre becomes possible
```

Use contrast, consequence, and the narrowing of the question to maintain pace.
Do not compensate for the lack of named events with scenery, invented customs,
or a survey of unrelated ancient magic.

## Anecdote use

- The best humanising material is small and bounded: later wizarding children
  learning magical limits from bedtime stories (`beedle-ch04-001`) and the
  long familiarity of such stories (`beedle-fm-002`). Present these as later
  evidence of a possible kind of learning, not scenes from the founder era.
- The Weasleys' home education (`ext-b04-001`) is a clear later example, but its
  Authority-D carrier and modern date should keep it brief and comparative.
- Gryffindor's conventional swordsmanship in `ext-a08-003` can make pre-Statute
  contact concrete, but using the founder as a character would trespass on
  Chapter 2. Prefer the social practice over the personality.
- Do not dramatise persecution through invented victims or incidents.

## Risk of encyclopedia style

- The section on learning before the school is most likely to become a list of
  imagined teaching modes. Limit it to evidenced later comparisons and the
  consequence of their insufficiency.
- The Muggle-awareness section could become a creature catalogue. Mention only
  enough examples to establish visibility and misidentification.
- The threshold section could become a list of founders, Houses, artefacts, and
  admissions mechanisms. Keep only the institutional minimum and defer detail.

## Risk of unsupported invention

- Avoid connective phrases that silently turn later home education into a
  thousand-year continuity.
- Do not invent tutors, apprentices, family curricula, local schools, child
  accidents, persecutions, laws, population pressures, or failed predecessors.
- Do not convert the remote siting rationale into proof that persecution caused
  every aspect of the foundation.
- Do not claim that Hogwarts centralised, standardised, nationalised, or
  democratised magical education. “Concentrated teachers and pupils in a durable
  place” is the narrower supported synthesis.
- Do not attribute the narrator's uncertainty to lost wars, destroyed archives,
  secrecy, or neglect unless evidence later establishes the loss mechanism.

## Draft-wide lessons to watch

- Can the prose express documentary limits without sounding timid or repetitive?
- Does each paragraph move from claim through evidence to significance rather
  than accumulate caveats?
- Does the narrator distinguish source type naturally, especially teacher
  history, ceremonial tradition, official retrospective prose, and
  Authority-D interviews?
- Are later confirmations kept visibly later instead of entering the narrative
  as timeless facts?
- Is dry humour restrained enough not to trivialise persecution or documentary
  absence?
- Does the chapter generate momentum through historical consequence rather than
  novelistic scene-setting?
- Do the final paragraphs stop cleanly before founder biography and foundation
  detail?
- After prose exists, assess these points before proposing any change to
  `style-lock.md`; predictions in this calibration file are not style authority.

## Revision 01 findings

### Methodological caution

The first draft made uncertainty the repeated engine of whole paragraphs. The
revision works better when the principal documentary limit is stated in the
opening and thereafter appears only where it changes what the narrator may
claim: the age of Hogwarts, the use of broad pre-Statute evidence, the absence
of dated potion and plant history, and the limits of recruitment evidence.
The chapter remains cautious, but the reader encounters that caution chiefly in
the scale and wording of its conclusions.

### Historical synthesis and narrator liberty

The strongest new passage joins the 962 broom record to the history of craft,
travel, concealment, and knowledge transmission. The source directly supplies
the date, limited controls, and value of discretion; the narrator may then
reason that repeated construction and use imply accumulated practical skill.
The date must remain part of the broad early-medieval horizon rather than be
declared earlier than Hogwarts. This degree of synthesis feels credible
because it introduces no invented maker, workshop, incident, or system. The
same method allows the school to be described as collecting dispersed
knowledge without calling it the first or only educational institution.

Plausible reconstruction is most useful at the level of ordinary process:
tools being copied, riders learning controls, experienced hands correcting
novices, and collective instruction producing comparison. It becomes unsafe
when it names an otherwise unattested social structure or turns one possible
route of transmission into a general custom.

### Concrete examples and contextual balance

The early broom and Brother Benedict's misidentified Jarvey give the chapter
more life than lists of hypothetical educational arrangements. Each is small
enough to remain evidence-led but concrete enough to make the period visible.
The Muggle material now performs three historical tasks: it locates magical
life in a shared landscape, demonstrates asymmetric knowledge, and explains
why protected distance mattered. It no longer relies on unsupported trade or
neighbourhood scenes.

The negative result on potions, plants, and healing is also useful calibration.
Familiarity within the modern curriculum creates a powerful impression of
antiquity, but topical relevance cannot replace a date. One compact boundary in
the old-arts section is preferable to either silently back-projecting those
subjects or repeatedly apologising for their absence.

### Rhythm, wit, scale, and transitions

- Sentence rhythm improves when direct historical statements and interpretive
  consequences alternate, rather than every paragraph opening with a caution
  formula.
- Dry wit is most effective once or twice in a section and attached to the
  evidence, such as the discomfort of the early broom. It should not become the
  narrator's default cadence.
- Paragraphs of roughly 70–120 words suit this voice. Shorter paragraphs can
  mark a conclusion; longer ones should carry an actual historical sequence.
- Six sections provide enough movement for a chapter of roughly 3,500–5,000
  words. The old-arts and Muggle sections can be slightly longer because their
  examples supply the chapter's concrete world; the opening should remain
  compact.
- Transitions work best by carrying a consequence forward: craft requires
  transmission, transmission occurs in a shared landscape, visibility creates
  a problem of safety, and safety makes the fixed school consequential.

## Proposed Draft-01 principles for human approval

These proposals should not enter `style-lock.md` until editorial approval:

1. **Restraint should be visible in the accuracy of the history, not constantly
   announced in the prose.** Establish a recurring evidentiary limitation once,
   then repeat it only when the boundary materially changes.
2. **A historian may connect well-supported facts into a modest historical
   conclusion without qualifying every sentence, provided the conclusion does
   not introduce unsupported specific events, dates, people, or institutions.**
3. Prefer one concrete, well-supported object, document, or practice to a list
   of plausible but unattested examples.
4. Treat failed retrieval as an editorial boundary, not necessarily as a topic
   the reader must repeatedly hear about.
5. When broad evidence spans many centuries, pair it with a closer-dated record
   before using it to characterise a particular period.
