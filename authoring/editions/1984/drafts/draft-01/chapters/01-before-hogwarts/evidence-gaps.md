# Evidence Gaps

## Chronological gaps

- The precise founding date is unknown. The best direct account says only “over
  a thousand years ago” and expressly calls the date uncertain
  (`cos-ch09-003`; open question `founding-and-the-four-houses-003`).
- Because `cos-ch09-003` carries a relative age spoken after 1984, the revision
  does not reproduce it mechanically. It uses the stable conclusion that the
  school predates the present millennium while preserving the unknown year.
- `qtta-ch01-002` supplies a genuine fixed point—European broom use in 962—in
  the broad early-medieval context, but the uncertain foundation date does not
  establish whether that record preceded or followed Hogwarts's opening.
- No inspected record supplies a sequence from earlier educational practices to
  the decision to create Hogwarts.
- The duration and geography of the persecution described at the time of the
  foundation are not established. `fb-ch02-004` supplies broadly medieval
  context but does not securely identify the same decade, region, or phase.
- “Before the International Statute of Secrecy” in `ext-a08-003` spans a period
  far wider than the founder era and cannot provide a close date.
- The date at which Hogwarts became enduring, dominant, or regionally
  comprehensive is not established; the evidence records a foundation, not a
  measured institutional transition.

## Educational gaps

- No inspected evidence directly describes a pre-Hogwarts British curriculum,
  teacher, teaching household, apprenticeship, itinerant instructor, private
  tutor, gathering place, or earlier school.
- The records do not establish whether most magical children received deliberate
  instruction before Hogwarts, learned only household practices, or received no
  systematic teaching.
- Later home education, Muggle primary schooling, correspondence courses, and
  bedtime instruction (`ext-b04-001`, `ext-b02-002`, `ext-a28-001`,
  `beedle-fm-002`, `beedle-ch04-001`) do not establish what happened before the
  school's foundation.
- The age, ability, social background, and geographic distribution of the first
  pupils are unknown.
- The corpus does not establish whether Hogwarts was Britain's first, only,
  largest, or first permanent magical school.
- No evidence shows how pre-foundation children obtained, used, or learned with
  wands. The Ollivander establishment's 382 BC claim (`ps-ch05-007`) is evidence
  for a tradition of ancient wandmaking, not for uninterrupted business history
  or wand education before Hogwarts.
- The dated medieval broom (`qtta-ch01-002`) proves that a repeatable transport
  craft existed by 962, but no record identifies its makers, teaching method, or
  ordinary route of transmission.
- It is unknown how children born outside established magical families were
  recognised or reached before the school created a recruitment system.

## Revision 01 old-arts search

- The expanded retrieval pass queried potions, antidotes, alchemy, magical
  plants, Herbology, healing, wands, runes, spells, broom flight, creature lore,
  and Muggle historical context, then inspected canonical YAML for the serious
  candidates.
- No inspected record securely dates potion-making, medicinal plant use, or a
  healing practice before Hogwarts. `ext-a21-002` is an imprecisely dated later
  Hogwarts practice; `fb-ch06-006` gives an undated potion-material link;
  `cos-ch14-001` and `cos-ch16-002` concern Harry-era Mandrake use; and
  `fb-ch07-006` dates neither the discovery nor use of phoenix tears.
- `ext-a17-002` explicitly describes the Hogwarts Pensieve as older than the
  school, but its official carrier postdates 1984 and does not establish that
  the dating was available to this edition's narrator.
- `fb-ch05-003` identifies the first recorded Basilisk breeder but gives no date
  for Herpo the Foul. The generated phrase “ancient Basilisk breeding tradition”
  is discovery metadata, not independent evidence of chronology.
- `qtta-ch02-001` establishes a technological sequence from controllable brooms
  to organised broom games without supplying a date. `qtta-ch03-001` provides an
  eleventh-century diary, probably too late for Chapter 1 and better reserved for
  Chapter 14.

## Contradictory accounts

- `ext-a08-003` describes pre-Statute wizards mingling freely with Muggles,
  whereas `cos-ch09-003` describes an age of fear and persecution in which the
  castle was placed far from Muggle eyes. These claims can coexist across
  places, classes, and moments, but the evidence does not explain the variation.
- Later evidence offers different educational frames: `ext-b04-001` allows
  Muggle primary school or home education, while `ext-a28-001` describes
  home-schooling and correspondence courses in countries without schools. Their
  scopes differ and neither should be treated as a complete universal model.
- There is no direct contradiction over the foundation itself, but Binns's
  historical summary and the Sorting Hat's songs have different evidentiary
  characters. They should corroborate only their shared minimum claims; poetic
  additions remain tradition.

## Open questions

- `founding-and-the-four-houses-001`: What sources underlie Professor Binns's
  distinction between founding history and Chamber legend?
- `founding-and-the-four-houses-003`: Can the foundation be dated more precisely
  than “over a thousand years ago”?
- `founding-and-the-four-houses-004`: What evidence can clarify the founders'
  early cooperation without relying wholly on the Sorting Hat's account?
- `pre-hogwarts-historical-context-006`: How should accidental childhood magic
  be connected to magical education without overstating school policy?
- `pre-hogwarts-historical-context-007`: What roles did families, Hogwarts, or
  public authorities have in identifying and managing childhood magic?
- `pre-hogwarts-historical-context-017`: How were prospective pupils recorded
  before first contact, and how widely did birth-time registration apply?
- `pre-hogwarts-historical-context-024`: Which wand knowledge belongs to Hogwarts
  teaching and which to external supplier tradition?
- No canonical open question currently asks directly what educational systems,
  if any, existed in Britain before Hogwarts. That absence should not be mistaken
  for a settled answer.

## Source-authority issues

- `ext-b04-001` and `ext-b02-002`, the clearest statements about education before
  Hogwarts entry, survive as Authority-D interview transcriptions from 2004 and
  2000. They are later confirmations, not evidence of founder-era practice.
- `ext-b14-001` and `ext-b14-002` are also Authority-D transcriptions and offer
  undated author commentary rather than historical or institutional records.
- `ext-a28-001`–`004` are Authority-A official prose but were published in 2016
  and make largely undated generalisations. Source authority does not cure their
  chronological uncertainty or establish 1984 narrator access.
- `cos-ch09-003` is a knowledgeable teacher's account, but Binns does not cite
  the records behind it and states that the foundation date is uncertain.
- `gof-ch12-002` and `ootp-ch11-003` are sung by an artefact associated with the
  foundation. Their ceremonial and poetic form warrants treatment as tradition,
  even when they corroborate Binns's minimum account.

## Tempting but unsupported claims

- Hogwarts was the first magical school in Britain.
- Hogwarts replaced an established network of family schools, tutors,
  apprenticeships, itinerant masters, village classes, or correspondence courses.
- All magical parents taught their children magic, or all Muggle-born children
  lacked instruction.
- Childhood magic made an institution necessary because it was becoming more
  common or dangerous.
- Growing persecution was the sole or principal motive for founding the school.
  The strongest evidence directly explains the castle's distance from Muggles,
  not the entire institutional decision.
- Magical society was uniformly integrated with Muggles or uniformly hidden.
- The four founders represented a planned national settlement or shared one
  educational philosophy before opening the school.
- Standardised subjects, seven-year schooling, modern admissions, Houses, or
  wand requirements existed from the first moment of the project.
- A surviving pre-Hogwarts artefact such as the Pensieve proves a preceding
  school, settlement, curriculum, or institutional site.
- Hogwarts's creation was inevitable.

## Research-layer issues

- The canonical open-question topic “Pre-Hogwarts Historical Context” primarily
  concerns characters before their own school attendance, especially Harry, not
  the period before Hogwarts's foundation. This nomenclature collision makes it
  a weak discovery route for Chapter 1's central question.
- `fb-ch02-004` depends on surrounding medieval context for the phrase “this
  time”; the entry accurately records the limitation, but the date remains too
  broad for a close founder-era claim.
- `ext-a28-001`–`004` are correctly exposed as `unknown_or_uncertain`, but their
  topical relevance can make them easy to overuse despite the missing dates and
  post-1984 carrier.
- No malformed evidence ID or broken canonical YAML was found during this pass.
  These observations are recorded here only; no research-layer file was changed.
- Several extraction-era placement titles use “ancient” more confidently than
  the underlying source dates permit, notably `fb-ch05-003`. Revision work must
  continue to inspect the canonical record's anchor, context, and limitations
  rather than accepting its generated title as chronology.
