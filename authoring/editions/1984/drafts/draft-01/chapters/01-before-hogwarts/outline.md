# Chapter 1 Outline — Before Hogwarts

Status: Proposed outline only. Completion of this file does not advance the
chapter beyond `evidence_selected`.

## Central Argument

The significance of Hogwarts can be shown more securely than the educational
world it displaced. The surviving evidence does not reveal a coherent British
system before the school: it names no earlier institution, curriculum, or
ordinary method of instruction. Later records prove that magical learning can
occur in households and through shared childhood culture, but they do not
license a reconstruction of the founder era. That silence should govern the
chapter rather than invite invention.

What the record does establish is that magical and non-magical people were not
separated by the later regime of secrecy, that fear and persecution formed part
of the founding context, and that four exceptional magical people built a
remote castle to which they sought out and brought young magic-users for
education. Hogwarts therefore matters as an enduring concentration of place,
teachers, and pupils under dangerous conditions. The chapter need not claim it
was the first or only school: its historical movement is from an obscure and
unevenly documented educational landscape to the threshold of a durable,
concealed institution.

## Evidence posture

- Direct historical support: `cos-ch09-003` supplies the chapter's minimum
  founding context; `ext-a08-003` and `fb-ch02-001`–`004` supply broader social
  context with chronological limits.
- Reasonable synthesis: a castle-based school that repeatedly sought out and
  assembled pupils constitutes a durable concentration of education. “First”,
  “only”, “national”, and “standardised” are not supported.
- Tradition: the Sorting Hat accounts (`gof-ch12-002`, `ootp-ch11-003`,
  `ext-a02-001`) corroborate a shared minimum but are reserved chiefly for
  Chapters 3–5 and must remain ceremonial testimony.
- Later comparison: `beedle-fm-002`, `beedle-ch04-001`, `ext-b04-001`, and
  `ext-b02-002` show that household and informal learning existed in later
  periods. They do not fill the pre-foundation gap.
- Speculation: proposed tutors, apprenticeships, itinerant teaching, local
  schools, and family curricula are excluded from narrative claims.

## Opening Strategy

- Historical entrance: begin with the documentary problem that the first firm
  view of magical education arrives with the institution itself. Before the
  castle, the record contains children and a magical society but no recoverable
  educational system.
- Evidence: the contrast between `cos-ch09-003` and the explicitly later or
  undated comparisons `ext-b04-001`, `ext-b02-002`, `beedle-fm-002`, and
  `beedle-ch04-001`.
- Reason for opening: it establishes the narrator's method, prevents an
  invented panorama of “ancient schooling”, and turns scarcity into a focused
  historical question.
- Lead into the argument: move from what cannot be reconstructed to the few
  conditions that can be established, then ask what changed when a fixed place
  began to gather teachers and pupils.
- Prohibited opening: do not begin by announcing the founding date or naming
  the founders.

## Major Sections

### 1. The History That Does Not Survive

- Historical purpose: establish the evidentiary boundary and make the absence
  of a pre-school institutional record part of the historical method.
- Main claim: surviving evidence is markedly stronger for Hogwarts's emergence
  than for the educational arrangements before it; absence of evidence does not
  prove absence of teaching or earlier institutions.
- Evidence IDs: `cos-ch09-003`; contextual contrast with `ext-b04-001`,
  `ext-b02-002`, `ext-a28-001`, `beedle-fm-002`, `beedle-ch04-001`.
- Chronological role: opens at an undated period before the foundation and
  defines which later evidence may be used only as comparison.
- Connection to previous section: this is the opening section.
- Connection to next section: once the silence is explicit, the chapter can ask
  what limited evidence says about children and learning without pretending to
  recover a system.
- Uncertainties to preserve: no inference that records were never kept, that no
  teaching occurred, or that Hogwarts was the first school.

### 2. Learning Before the School: Evidence and Analogy

- Historical purpose: answer the central educational question as far as the
  corpus permits and stop where direct support ends.
- Main claim: later magical history documents home education, optional Muggle
  primary schooling, and family transmission of cultural and magical limits;
  these examples make informal learning possible, not proven, before Hogwarts.
- Evidence IDs: `beedle-fm-002`, `beedle-ch04-001`; later confirmation
  `ext-b04-001`, `ext-b02-002`; uncertain comparison `ext-a28-001`.
- Chronological role: deliberately steps forward into later evidence to test an
  analogy, then returns to the unresolved pre-foundation period.
- Connection to previous section: supplies bounded examples for the categories
  the opening refused to invent.
- Connection to next section: because instruction cannot be reconstructed
  directly, the argument shifts to the better-supported social world in which
  magical children and prospective teachers lived.
- Uncertainties to preserve: no founder-era home-schooling claim, curriculum,
  universality, teacher identity, or assumed disadvantage for children outside
  magical families.
- List-risk control: do not catalogue hypothetical educational modes. Compare
  only the few evidenced later forms and state why they cannot be back-projected.

### 3. A World Not Yet Apart

- Historical purpose: replace the modern assumption of complete separation
  with a more cautious account of contact, visibility, and imperfect knowledge.
- Main claim: before statutory secrecy, wizards could mingle with Muggles, while
  medieval non-magical culture retained distorted knowledge of magical beings;
  contact and misunderstanding could coexist.
- Evidence IDs: `ext-a08-003`, `fb-ch02-001`, `fb-ch02-002`.
- Chronological role: supplies broad pre-Statute and medieval context without
  claiming a uniform condition in a single founder-era decade.
- Connection to previous section: places the unknown educational practices
  inside a social world where magical families were not necessarily sealed off
  from their neighbours.
- Connection to next section: visibility and misunderstanding create the
  conditions in which fear and concealment become historically consequential.
- Uncertainties to preserve: the degree of mixing in Britain, regional
  variation, the social status of those who mingled, and the dates of the
  surviving Muggle representations.
- Encyclopedia-risk control: magical creatures are evidence of Muggle
  awareness, not subjects for a creature catalogue.

### 4. Fear and the Uses of Distance

- Historical purpose: identify the strongest supported pressure surrounding
  the school project while separating site rationale from an all-purpose
  founding motive.
- Main claim: Professor Binns directly connects the castle's distance from
  Muggle eyes to an age in which magic was feared and witches and wizards were
  persecuted; broader medieval evidence shows how misunderstanding could
  intensify such fear.
- Evidence IDs: `cos-ch09-003`, supported cautiously by `fb-ch02-004`; the
  apparent tension with `ext-a08-003` must remain visible.
- Chronological role: narrows the chapter from broad pre-Statute contact to the
  conditions Binns associates with the foundation.
- Connection to previous section: turns visibility and imperfect knowledge
  into a concrete institutional problem: where education could safely occur.
- Connection to next section: a need for distance explains why the material
  form of a remote school mattered, preparing the entrance of the founders as
  historical actors.
- Uncertainties to preserve: persecution's precise date, scale, and location;
  whether it motivated the school itself or specifically its concealed site;
  whether magical–Muggle contact continued alongside danger.
- Invention-risk control: do not create raids, burned schools, endangered
  pupils, political decrees, or a coordinated crisis absent from the evidence.

### 5. At the Threshold of an Institution

- Historical purpose: state what changed with Hogwarts without narrating the
  founders' biographies or the act of foundation.
- Main claim: the record shows four powerful witches and wizards building a
  remote castle, seeking out magical youngsters, and bringing them to one place
  for education. The combination of durable place, active recruitment, and
  assembled instruction is the chapter's defensible institutional turning
  point.
- Evidence IDs: `cos-ch09-003`; `gof-ch12-002` may be acknowledged in working
  notes as corroborating the shared founding minimum, with all House and Hat
  detail deferred.
- Chronological role: reaches the foundation boundary and stops before the
  school's construction, organisation, Houses, or conflict are narrated.
- Connection to previous section: the remote site becomes not merely refuge but
  the location of a continuing educational enterprise.
- Connection to next section: Chapter 2 can now ask who the four founders were,
  what can be known of them, and why they chose to act together.
- Uncertainties to preserve: the school's novelty relative to unknown
  predecessors, the founders' individual motives, the sequence of recruitment
  and construction, and any claim of inevitability.

## Historical Progression

```text
documentary silence about pre-school education
→ bounded later analogies, explicitly not back-projected
→ a pre-Statute world of contact and imperfect Muggle knowledge
→ fear and persecution make protected distance consequential
→ one remote place gathers founders and pupils
→ Chapter 2 asks who the founders were
```

## Closing Strategy

- End with the institutional threshold: children can now be sought out and
  brought to a fixed, concealed place, but the people who created that place
  have not yet been examined.
- Treat the foundation as contingent. Fear, distance, talent, and educational
  need made Hogwarts significant; they did not make its creation inevitable.
- Do not close with a miniature founder roll-call, Sorting account, House
  preview, construction narrative, or Slytherin conflict.
- Final handoff question for Chapter 2: what can the historical record, as
  distinct from school tradition, tell us about the four people who undertook
  the project?
