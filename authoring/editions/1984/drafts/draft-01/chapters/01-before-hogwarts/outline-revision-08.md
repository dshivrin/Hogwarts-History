## Chapter argument

Hogwarts arose in a society that already possessed sophisticated magical
practice but left no recoverable general system of pre-school education. Its
historical importance lay not in inventing magic or necessarily displacing an
earlier school, but in gathering teachers, pupils, knowledge, and concealment
within an enduring institution. The argument moves from the character of the
surviving evidence, through skilled practice and shared life with Muggles, to
the practical value of a protected place for repeated learning.

## Opening

Open with the contrast between the institutional record Hogwarts later created
and the scattered traces that precede it. Establish the chapter's positive
method: fragments can show practices and conditions even when they cannot
reconstruct an educational system. Introduce C1-08-01 without cataloguing every
source or beginning with an apology for missing evidence.

## Historical progression

The sequence is evidentiary and causal: fragmentary record → demonstrable arts
→ plausible channels of transmission established only by later comparison →
shared magical–Muggle landscape → danger of repeated visibility → durable,
concealed institution. The 962 broom record remains a broad chronological
anchor, not a claim about whether the school had already opened. The chapter
stops as the founders enter the history.

## Treatment of uncertainty and legend

State once that no general pre-Hogwarts educational system survives. Keep the
foundation date uncertain. Treat the Ollivander antiquity material as shop
tradition and descendant belief beside firmer medieval evidence. Omit the
Pensieve discovery legend and all unsupported educational reconstructions.
Later examples demonstrate possible channels or continuities only when their
later date is explicit.

## Conclusion

Conclude that Hogwarts made one historically consequential answer to dispersed
knowledge, uneven access, and dangerous visibility. Introduce the founders as
the next historical question without naming their ideals, relics, or later
conflict. The final movement should invite Chapter 2 rather than summarise it.
