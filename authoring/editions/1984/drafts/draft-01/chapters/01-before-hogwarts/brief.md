# Chapter Brief

## Identity

- Chapter number: 01
- Chapter title: Before Hogwarts
- Draft: Draft 01
- Workflow state after preparation: `evidence_selected`

## Purpose

Establish the limited but consequential evidence for magical childhood,
wizard–Muggle relations, and educational provision before Hogwarts, then show
why the creation of a concealed, permanent place for seeking out and educating
magical children represented a significant institutional development. The
chapter must also teach the reader how cautiously the book will handle a period
for which the institutional record begins more clearly than the history that
preceded it.

## Central historical question

What can surviving evidence establish about magical education and society
before Hogwarts, and why did a permanent, concealed school matter in those
conditions?

## Secondary questions

- What, if anything, can be known about how magical children learned before a
  permanent school?
- Do the records support familial, private, itinerant, apprenticeship, or other
  forms of instruction, or only later analogies?
- What did relations between magical and non-magical people look like before
  statutory secrecy?
- How securely can Muggle fear and persecution be connected to the school's
  location and to the larger decision to found it?
- What changed when pupils could be sought out and brought to one enduring
  place for education?
- Which familiar founding claims are records, which are traditions, and which
  remain reconstruction?

## Chronological scope

The chapter concerns the period before Hogwarts's foundation and ends as the
school project begins. The foundation is known only as more than a thousand
years before Professor Binns's 1992 account; its precise date is uncertain.
Earlier and broadly medieval evidence may establish social conditions, while
later examples of home learning or childhood culture may be used only as
explicitly labelled comparisons. Fifteenth-century persecution, the 1692
Statute settlement, and modern admissions practice do not become causes or
features of the founder era merely because they are better documented.

## Required subjects

- The documentary scarcity surrounding education before the school.
- The difference between evidence of later household or childhood learning and
  proof of pre-foundation educational practice.
- Pre-Statute contact between wizards and Muggles.
- Muggle awareness, misunderstanding, fear, and persecution where the evidence
  is chronologically relevant or explicitly comparative.
- Professor Binns's account of a remote castle, four founders, and children
  sought out and brought there to be educated.
- The institutional significance of a durable, concealed educational centre,
  expressed as reasonable synthesis rather than a claim that Hogwarts was the
  first or only school.

## Explicit exclusions

- Founder biographies, personalities, relics, and individual motives, reserved
  primarily for Chapter 2.
- The detailed collaboration, construction, site selection, admissions
  instruments, and opening of the school, reserved primarily for Chapter 3.
- House principles, Sorting, and the institutional development of the four
  Houses, reserved primarily for Chapter 4.
- The founders' dispute, Salazar Slytherin's departure, bloodline programme,
  and Chamber tradition, reserved primarily for Chapter 5.
- A general world history, catalogue of ancient magic, wand history, castle
  tour, or account of post-1984 events.
- Hogwarts house-elf servitude, whose omission is required by the edition bible.

## Relationship to neighbouring chapters

- Previous chapter: None. This chapter establishes the book's historical method
  and the world from which the institution emerged.
- Next chapter: Chapter 2, *The Four Founders*.
- Handoff to Chapter 2: the evidence has established a need for a concealed,
  enduring place of education and has reached the moment when four historical
  actors undertook that project. Chapter 2 must identify those actors, assess
  the sources for their lives and attributed aims, and preserve the difference
  between history and founder tradition.
- Cross-chapter dependencies: Chapters 3–5 own the act of foundation, the House
  system, and the founders' rupture. Chapters 9–10 own later educational and
  admissions practice; Chapter 15 owns international comparisons.

## Target length

Recommended eventual length: approximately 3,000–4,500 words. The direct
pre-foundation evidence is too sparse to support a 6,000–9,000-word chapter
without repetition, general wizarding history, or unsupported connective lore.
Final length should follow the approved argument rather than fill a quota.

## Editorial notes

- The chapter's restraint is part of its argument, not an apology for missing
  material.
- “Permanent institution” is an editorial synthesis from the building of a
  castle and the establishment of a continuing school; it must not be presented
  as source wording.
- “First”, “only”, “national”, “standardised”, and “inevitable” are prohibited
  descriptions unless new evidence establishes them.
- No prose drafting begins until the evidence selection, argument, outline, and
  calibration receive human editorial approval.
