## Repetition and compression

### Sentence and paragraph repetition

- Automated comparison found no repeated eight-, ten- or twelve-word sequence
  within the manuscript.
- House material develops in distinct stages: sleeping, common-room function,
  internal supervision, public identity and later synthesis.
- Castle material changes function between first encounters and acquired
  competence; it does not repeat a room-by-room description.
- Communication appears first as public breakfast post, later as continued
  contact and exceptional inspection.
- Christmas moves from later reduced-school residence to one older festive
  anchor; the pantomime disaster is compressed to preserve its new communal
  function.
- The conclusion names the interpretive balance without recataloguing all
  thirty-one preceding examples.

### Chapter-level conceptual repetition

- The ten outline movements answer separate questions: residence, daily
  rhythm, communal meals, after-hours work, leisure and relationships, outside
  contact, holidays, lived discipline, environmental pressures and synthesis.
- Qualifications are stated beside the claims they govern rather than repeated
  as standalone disclaimers.
- The two navigation paragraphs are retained because the first establishes the
  newcomer's practical burden and the second the competence—and remaining
  fallibility—of experienced pupils.
- The Great Hall paragraphs distinguish ordinary assembly, informational use
  and ceremonial intensification.

### Cross-chapter repetition

- Chapter 4 retains House origins, Sorting, ideals and the full argument about
  House identity; Chapter 10 shows House membership functioning as residence.
- Chapter 6 retains architecture and the full analysis of mutable routes and
  embedded dormitory rules; Chapter 10 reduces those features to time,
  inconvenience, privacy and pupil competence.
- Chapter 7 retains the grounds as landscape and boundary; lake leisure and
  Hogsmeade departure appear only as pupil experience.
- Chapter 8 retains formal authority and uses Dippet's pantomime ban to
  establish headmaster power; Chapter 10 compresses the same event and uses it
  only to establish staff-and-pupil Christmas culture.
- Chapter 9 retains curriculum, teaching and examinations; Chapter 10 begins
  exactly where its final paragraph points, with work and relationships lived
  outside formal instruction.
- Chapter 11 retains library history, collections and censorship; Chapter 10
  uses the library solely as a setting for study and ends at the threshold of
  written institutional memory.
- Chapter 13 retains the full history of ceremonies and traditions; feasts and
  the pantomime appear here only for their effect on resident pupils.

### Compression and flow

- Reader-facing prose contains 2,858 words before hidden metadata.
- No arbitrary length target was used.
- The original pantomime account was compressed after comparison with Chapter
  8 so that the new paragraph develops communal festivity rather than retelling
  the disaster or ban.
- Navigation mechanics were compressed after comparison with Chapter 6; only
  the burden on movement and acquired practical knowledge remain.
- Games and clubs without sufficiently strong evidence were omitted.
- Concrete distinctions among ordinary discipline, dangerous detention and
  exceptional abuse were preserved because further compression would erase
  the chapter's historical boundary.
