# Chapter 10 Evidence Gaps — Revision 01

## Daily rhythm

- No source supplies a universal Hogwarts bell schedule or complete ordinary
  day from waking to bedtime.
- Later scenes establish breakfast, lessons, lunch or dinner contexts,
  homework, free periods and after-hours restrictions, but not fixed times for
  every year or House.
- One first-year week includes midnight Astronomy, double lessons and a free
  Friday afternoon; these particulars cannot be promoted into a timeless
  timetable.
- The record does not establish an ordinary waking mechanism, washing routine,
  formal lights-out time or distinct bedtime rules for each age group.
- Sixth-year free periods are documented but shown being consumed by work;
  the evidence does not define an official entitlement or prove the same
  pattern before 1984.

## House residence

- Gryffindor supplies most observed dormitory and common-room life. The
  evidence does not justify treating its entrance, furniture or routines as
  universal across all four Houses.
- Five four-posters are observed in one Gryffindor year-group dormitory. The
  source does not establish a fixed dormitory capacity.
- The founder-attributed rule preventing boys from entering the girls'
  dormitory is later reported from *Hogwarts: A History* and directly observed
  only in Gryffindor Tower. No equivalent girls-to-boys restriction is shown.
- Common-room studying, notices, parties, arguments and prefect intervention
  are well attested later; their precise pre-1984 continuity is not.
- Laundry, routine cleaning, fire-tending and other service labour are excluded
  under the Draft-01 style lock's decision not to reopen Hogwarts house-elf
  servitude.

## Meals and the Great Hall

- Later evidence securely shows four House tables, a staff table, ordinary
  breakfast, owl post, notices, news, feasts and House Cup display.
- It does not establish a complete meal timetable, attendance rules, seating
  flexibility at ordinary meals or pre-1984 continuity of every practice.
- Food appearing and disappearing is observed in the novels, but the selected
  structured evidence does not support a clean account of its mechanism
  without entering deferred house-elf material.
- Holiday rearrangement to one shared table is one later scene and may have
  been an ad hoc decision rather than fixed Christmas procedure.

## Homework, study and recreation

- The record is rich in assignments and revision but poor in formal homework
  policy. Workload varies by year, subject, teacher and crisis.
- Hermione's schedules and habits are not ordinary by default; they are used
  only where other evidence supports broader revision or library patterns.
- Permitted study and homework groups are supported by a student's rule check
  and a teacher's reported answer, not by the text of a rulebook.
- Chess, Gobstones and Exploding Snap are present in the corpus but the
  structured evidence is too thin or too late to establish their frequency as
  general school customs in this revision.
- Quidditch evidence is limited to spectatorship, House identity, practices
  and celebrations. Its institutional history remains outside this chapter.
- No comprehensive clubs-and-societies register survives. One old Gobstones
  clipping and later student groups do not prove a stable extracurricular
  system.

## Friendship and social difference

- The novels supply abundant individual relationships but few structured
  entries designed to establish friendship formation as an institution.
- Marauders-era memory and testimony prove one cohort's friendships, rivalry,
  bullying, romance and prefect failure; they are not a social census.
- Riddle's Slughorn circle establishes one influential network, not the normal
  social organisation of every generation.
- Later blood-status abuse and House rivalry demonstrate visible cultural
  division but do not quantify its prevalence before 1984.
- Birthdays, gossip, teasing, reconciliation and cross-year help are often
  plot-level observations. Revision 01 should use only examples that develop a
  larger supported pattern.

## Home, Hogsmeade and holidays

- Owl post establishes letters, parcels and outside news, but no complete
  school postal regulation or ordinary delivery time beyond repeated breakfast
  observation.
- Howlers show that family discipline could become public in the Great Hall;
  their frequency and school response are unknown.
- Third-year eligibility and guardian permission are clear later. The date of
  the rule's adoption, ordinary trip frequency, supervision inside the village
  and transport arrangements are not established.
- Pupils without permission are shown remaining at Hogwarts or evading the
  rule; no formal alternative weekend programme is documented.
- Christmas sign-up and residence recur in later years. Easter residence is
  supported by one pupil's pattern, not a full school holiday policy.
- The pantomime is direct evidence of Dippet-era festive culture but is an
  exceptional failed experiment, not proof that theatrical performance was a
  regular Hogwarts tradition.

## Rules, prefects and punishment

- Night wandering is clearly forbidden later, but no formal curfew time or
  age-differentiated curfew survives.
- Point deductions, detention, confiscation and loss of privileges are
  observed, but no fixed tariff links particular offences to particular
  penalties.
- Student accounts support prefect patrols and punishment powers; exact limits
  remain contested, especially under Umbridge's exceptional Inquisitorial
  Squad.
- Filch's enthusiasm for old chains and corporal punishment is not proof of
  their date, normality or frequency.
- The Forbidden Forest detention is directly observed but unusually dangerous
  and should not be described as a routine detention type.
- Umbridge's blood quill is an exceptional concealed abuse under Ministry-era
  intervention, not normal Hogwarts discipline.

## Castle, health and material life

- The castle-navigation evidence is overwhelmingly Harry-era and proves later
  experience, not unchanged pre-1984 routes or enchantments.
- Hospital-wing evidence concerns injuries, poisoning and magical accidents;
  ordinary minor illness and parental notification are poorly documented.
- The corpus does not establish ordinary laundry, bathing frequency, washroom
  arrangements, replacement of broken equipment or a systematic lost-property
  process.
- Pets in dormitories are observed, but the full rule is supplied only by
  later official commentary and is not dated.
- Hogwarts financial assistance is securely shown in Tom Riddle's admissions
  memory. Its administration, scale and later continuity are unknown.
- Second-hand dress robes and books show visible economic difference, but the
  record does not support a general theory of pupil class structure.

## Cross-chapter risks

- Chapter 4 owns House origins, ideals and formal identity.
- Chapter 6 owns architecture and room-by-room castle description.
- Chapter 7 owns the grounds as landscape and boundary.
- Chapter 8 owns formal authority, governance and disciplinary power.
- Chapter 9 owns curriculum, teaching and examinations.
- Chapter 11 owns library history, collections and censorship.
- Chapter 13 owns ceremonies and traditions as institutional histories.
- Later Quidditch material should establish only recreation and social meaning,
  not repeat the sport's history or governance.

## Research-layer issues discovered

- No canonical evidence file was modified.
- The structured entries do not capture several useful bounded passages in
  full, including first-years being led from the feast to Gryffindor Tower,
  their trunks already present beside five four-posters, and one first-year
  Friday afternoon described as free. These passages were inspected directly
  but are not advanced as independent claims without a matching evidence ID.
- The Beedle extraction was confirmed visually against PDF pages 26–28. It
  accurately records festive celebrations, staff and student participation,
  Great Hall use, hospital-wing aftermath and Dippet's later ban.
