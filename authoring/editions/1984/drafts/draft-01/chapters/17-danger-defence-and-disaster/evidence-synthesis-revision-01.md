## Chapter Purpose

Chapter 17 explains how Hogwarts manages dangers that belong to the school as
an institution rather than to one exceptional competition. It begins with
standing protections against discovery and movement, proceeds through
containment, rules, supervision and medical recovery, and tests those layers
against accidents, hidden threats, hostile entry and war. Its subject is the
relationship among prevention, failure, response and adaptation—not an
inventory of bad events. The chapter ends by showing why protection at Hogwarts
ultimately depends upon judgement and revision, handing that problem to the
history of heads and reformers in Chapter 18.

Chapter 7 owns the geography and ecology of the grounds. Chapter 8 owns the
structure of authority, including the pantomime as an example of headmaster
control. Chapter 9 owns pedagogy and safe teaching practice. Chapter 10 owns
ordinary rules, discipline and the pupil experience of the hospital wing.
Chapter 16 owns Tournament danger and supplies the immediate argument that
magic's danger cannot be removed altogether. Chapter 17 may recall material
from these chapters only to establish a distinct mechanism or consequence of
institutional risk management.

## Source-Access Decision

Two selected passages receive exceptional weight in the reconstructed 1984
layer. `poa-ch09-001` and `gof-ch11-005` are explicitly attributed to
*Hogwarts: A History*. They support protections beyond walls,
anti-Apparition measures and concealment from Muggle discovery. They do not
date those protections, inventory every spell or establish that Hogwarts is
Unplottable.

The remaining pre-1984 incidents are principally later-carried history. The
Beery–Kettleburn pantomime survives in Dumbledore's later commentary; the 1943
closure crisis is preserved through later testimony and a diary memory; the
Willow containment is explained decades afterward by participants. These may
enter a unified history only with attribution and an intelligible temporal
frame. They do not become knowledge available to the reconstructed Bagshot
narrator merely because their events occurred before 1984.

Post-1984 observations perform two different functions. Some reveal the
operation or adjustability of an older-looking institution—hospital care,
Forest supervision and temporary suspension of anti-Apparition protection.
Others are later events in their own right: the second Chamber opening,
Dementor security, wartime screening, the Vanishing Cabinet breach and the
Battle of Hogwarts. These later events may test the chapter's institutional
model. They may not be backdated or allowed to dominate the 1984
reconstruction.

## Consolidated Claims

### C17-01 — Hogwarts protects by distinguishing threats, not by sealing itself absolutely

- **Claim:** The strongest standing evidence describes different protections
  for different threats. Muggles approaching Hogwarts are diverted by an
  appearance of ruin and danger, while witches and wizards are constrained by
  anti-Apparition protection and, in crisis, by watched routes. Later evidence
  shows that at least one restriction can be relaxed locally and temporarily.
  The result is a selective defensive perimeter, not proof of total
  impenetrability.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** the two central descriptions are
  explicitly attributed to *Hogwarts: A History* and qualify for the
  reconstructed 1984 layer. The statement that castle and grounds have
  “always” been protected is later-carried official evidence; the supervised
  Great Hall exception is a post-1984 observation.
- **Classification:** established core with bounded later confirmation.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** “Always” supplies no founding date. No selected source says
  Hogwarts itself is Unplottable, names the makers of the protections or proves
  that every secret passage is ordinarily watched. The later exception proves
  adjustability, not the historic frequency or authority for exceptions.
- **Primary section:** More Than Walls
- **Cross-chapter status:** new synthesis from castle protection evidence;
  callback to Chapter 7 only for the perimeter already described there.

### C17-02 — Dangerous things are often bounded, supervised or prohibited rather than removed

- **Claim:** Hogwarts repeatedly retains a hazard while placing distance,
  enclosure, rules or supervision around it. A dangerous condition may be
  routed away from the castle; a dangerous lesson may proceed under procedural
  instruction; a normally forbidden area may be entered in groups under adult
  direction. Temporary corridor closure and a locked one-year hazard show the
  same range in a later, exceptional form. These cases support a distinction
  between containment, supervised permission and an obedience-dependent rule.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** Lupin's containment is pre-1984 history
  disclosed later. The Forest lesson, creature lesson and third-floor corridor
  are post-1984 observations; none establishes an old universal procedure.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported institutional pattern, with examples
  that must retain their dates and individual circumstances.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** The evidence does not prove a written hierarchy of controls
  or a single safety policy. The third-floor corridor and its guarded object
  were temporary. The Willow passage was also secrecy and accommodation, not
  merely public safety. The near-fatal breach records confidentiality afterward
  but no physical reform.
- **Primary section:** Danger Kept Within Bounds
- **Cross-chapter status:** development of Chapters 7 and 9; those chapters
  retain geographic and pedagogical ownership.

### C17-03 — Recovery is a layer of protection, but healability does not make harm acceptable

- **Claim:** Hogwarts's institutional response includes recovery after
  prevention fails. The hospital wing treats substantial magical injuries,
  controls visitors and can sustain overnight care; staff can cultivate a
  magical remedy during a prolonged crisis. Severe injury may exceed the
  school's capacity and require St Mungo's. A classroom injury can also trigger
  testimony, scrutiny and communication beyond the treating matron.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** all selected examples are post-1984
  observations. They may show later institutional capacity and limits, not a
  complete 1984 hospital policy or an unchanged ancient practice.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported later institutional evidence.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Medical capability does not demonstrate that an injury was
  considered acceptable. The examples do not define a transfer threshold,
  staffing establishment, formal incident-report procedure or historical date
  for the hospital wing's capacities.
- **Primary section:** Recovery and Accountability
- **Cross-chapter status:** development of Chapter 10's pupil-life treatment of
  the hospital wing and Chapter 9's teaching context.

### C17-04 — The pantomime disaster records a complete pre-1984 response sequence

- **Claim:** The failed Christmas pantomime provides the clearest surviving
  sequence of staff-introduced danger, fire and injury followed by evacuation,
  hospital treatment, individual probation and a headmaster's prohibition of
  the practice. It proves that Hogwarts could respond to one accident by
  restricting both a teacher and an activity. Kettleburn's reported sixty-two
  periods of probation suggests graduated control across a career, but does not
  disclose a general disciplinary code.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** the incident occurred under Armando
  Dippet while Dumbledore was a young Transfiguration teacher, but no exact year
  is supplied. It is later-carried pre-1984 history from Dumbledore's
  commentary, not automatically reconstructed Bagshot knowledge.
- **Classification:** established incident and response; broader
  institutional inference is reasonable and bounded.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** The comic tone neither disqualifies the evidence nor permits
  embellishment. The sources do not define probation, date the other sixty-one
  periods, show whether Beery was disciplined, or prove that every serious
  accident produced a ban. Dippet's pantomime prohibition is one documented
  reform, not a comprehensive safety revolution.
- **Primary section:** Recovery and Accountability
- **Cross-chapter status:** development of Chapter 8, which uses the same event
  to establish authority; Chapter 17 uses its incident-to-adaptation sequence.

### C17-05 — An unlocated internal threat defeats protections aimed at known places and conduct

- **Claim:** The Chamber history reveals an institutional limit more severe
  than a breached door. Repeated searches by learned witches, wizards and heads
  reportedly failed to find the danger; in 1943 death and continuing attacks
  made closure a live possibility. During the later opening, leadership could
  restrict movement, provide escorts, patrol, prepare treatment and plan
  evacuation, yet those measures managed exposure while leaving the hidden
  source untouched. Only later discovery identified the mechanism and creature.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** the search tradition and 1943 crisis
  concern pre-1984 Hogwarts but reach the reader through later sources. The
  1992 restrictions and final discovery are later editorial history and cannot
  be assigned to Bagshot's knowledge in 1984.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported institutional failure and later
  escalation; the breadth and timing of earlier searches remain attributed.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Do not retell the founder legend, attacks or solution. The
  evidence does not preserve an independent contemporary record of the first
  opening, prove that every head conducted a search, or establish a permanent
  post-crisis reform. The mechanism known in 1993 cannot be used to make 1943
  or 1984 authorities appear to know what they did not.
- **Primary section:** The Danger No Search Could Find
- **Cross-chapter status:** new risk-management analysis; Chapter 5 retains
  ownership of the Slytherin tradition.

### C17-06 — Emergency security depends upon people and can itself create danger

- **Claim:** Hogwarts's First-War reputation for safety is associated in later
  testimony with Dumbledore's deterrent presence, but its material basis is
  unknown. A later crisis shows how personal authority and improvised
  arrangements operated: pupils were concentrated, prefects and ghosts
  assisted, staff searched, and the headmaster refused an external force entry
  to the castle. That external force nevertheless failed to distinguish an
  Animagus intruder and eventually endangered an innocent pupil, whereupon it
  was removed. Meanwhile, a written password could authenticate possession of
  a credential without establishing identity or intent.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** the First-War claim concerns pre-1984
  Hogwarts but is later oral testimony. The remaining evidence belongs to the
  1993–94 Sirius Black crisis and is later editorial history.
- **Classification:** reasonable_inference from strongly supported later cases;
  the First-War reputation is attributed rather than operationally explained.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** The Great Hall response is not proven to be a formal standing
  protocol. The source does not define Dumbledore's legal authority over the
  Dementors or prove what older heads could have done. The Animagus case does
  not establish the full scope of castle detection magic. Reputation is not a
  spell inventory.
- **Primary section:** When Protection Becomes a Threat
- **Cross-chapter status:** new synthesis; portrait access overlaps Chapter 12
  and must be reduced to the defeated credential assumption.

### C17-07 — Wartime hardening adds layers, but an unknown route can bypass them

- **Claim:** Under later wartime threat Hogwarts combines fortified gates and
  wall jinxes with controlled entry, screening, inspection and behavioural
  rules. A cursed object almost reaches the school through a pupil; the
  response isolates the object, brings specialist inspection, gathers witness
  evidence and follows the victim medically. A still more serious breach uses
  a dangerous object already inside a concealed room to create a route that
  defenders watching known passages and corridors did not know existed. The
  failure is not evidence that every protection ceased; it shows that the
  defensive plan assumed hostile movement would use detectable, known routes.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** all evidence belongs to 1996–97 and is
  later editorial history. It demonstrates exceptional capability and failure,
  not normal pre-1984 practice.
- **Classification:** established later case with a strongly supported analysis
  of the defeated assumption.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** The evidence does not distinguish every old enchantment from
  every newly strengthened one, identify the age of the Vanishing Cabinets or
  prove that the school knew the internal cabinet remained functional. Do not
  turn the section into a plot recap or attribute the whole defence to the
  castle independent of Dumbledore and staff action.
- **Primary section:** Hardening the School, Missing the Route
- **Cross-chapter status:** consequence of C17-01 and C17-02; later analogue to
  Chapter 16's finding that apparently neutral protections can be defeated by
  manipulating their assumptions.

### C17-08 — Siege reveals defensive capacity and its limits without making the castle self-defending

- **Claim:** During the Battle of Hogwarts, ordinary movement constraints and
  hostile control require an improvised evacuation route. Leadership removes
  under-age pupils, permits adult choice and assigns defensive sectors. Staff
  activate boundary protections and animate or deploy features of the castle,
  showing that Hogwarts can become part of its own defence. Sustained attack
  nevertheless breaches those defences. During a pause, the Great Hall becomes
  a place of triage and mourning, extending institutional response from defence
  to immediate recovery.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** this is later editorial history from
  1998. It cannot prove that the same measures were available, organised or
  expected before 1984.
- **Classification:** established exceptional later case.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Do not narrate the battle. Distinguish the castle's materials
  and enchantable features from spells, decisions and labour supplied by
  particular defenders. The evidence selected here does not establish the
  chronology or administration of post-war restoration.
- **Primary section:** The Castle Under Siege
- **Cross-chapter status:** new terminal stress test; portrait communications
  and passage networks remain Chapter 12 material.

### C17-09 — Hogwarts adapts episodically; no cumulative safety code is established

- **Claim:** Across the selected cases Hogwarts responds through a repertoire:
  warnings, locks and prohibitions; supervision and escort; treatment and
  external transfer; inquiry and specialist handling; staff probation and
  activity bans; concentration, evacuation, closure threats and strengthened
  enchantments. The evidence establishes repeated adaptation to particular
  dangers, but not a continuously codified safety regime. Institutional
  protection remains dependent on what leaders and staff notice, know and are
  prepared to change.
- **Chronology and knowledge access:** synthetic conclusion across pre-1984
  later-carried incidents and explicitly dated later editorial cases.
- **Classification:** strongly_supported synthesis, negatively bounded by the
  absence of a general code in the selected evidence.
- **Disposition:** advance_to_outline
- **Limitations:** Absence of surviving evidence is not proof that no general
  rules existed. The claim must be phrased as “not established” rather than
  “did not exist.” Several incidents record no durable reform, and the chapter
  must say so rather than supply one.
- **Primary section:** conclusion distributed through all sections, stated
  explicitly at the end of The Castle Under Siege.
- **Cross-chapter status:** consequence; provides the handoff to Chapter 18's
  heads and reformers.
