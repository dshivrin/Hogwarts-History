# Chapter 17 Evidence Gaps

## Missing chronology

- No selected source dates the establishment, renewal or ordinary maintenance
  of Hogwarts's Muggle-concealment or anti-Apparition protections.
- The Beery–Kettleburn pantomime can be placed under Armando Dippet and while
  Dumbledore was a young Transfiguration teacher, but not in a precise year.
- Kettleburn's other sixty-one reported periods of probation are undated and
  cannot be arranged into a career-long sequence.
- The history of the Vanishing Cabinets before their later use is insufficient
  to show when Hogwarts knew that its cabinet was dangerous or functional.
- No selected evidence establishes a post-Battle restoration chronology.

## Knowledge-access gaps

- The standing-protection passages attributed to *Hogwarts: A History* are
  available to the reconstructed 1984 layer; their exact lost wording and
  surrounding explanation are not.
- Dumbledore's commentary on the pantomime is later archival testimony. Its
  pre-1984 subject does not make it Bagshot's demonstrated knowledge.
- The Willow containment and its near-fatal bypass are disclosed later by
  participants. Their institutional recording before 1984 is unknown.
- The 1943 Chamber crisis is carried through later testimony and a diary
  memory. No independent contemporary Hogwarts record was located.
- The Chamber's actual access mechanism and creature become known only after
  the second opening. They must not be supplied to a 1943 or 1984 viewpoint.
- Hogwarts's First-War safety reputation is reported later by Hagrid. The
  spells, policies and contemporary administrative arrangements behind it are
  unknown.

## Contradictory or limiting evidence

- Strong protective enchantments coexist with secret passages, credential
  failure, transformed entry and an unknown Cabinet route. This is not a simple
  contradiction: the evidence suggests protections designed for particular
  movements and assumptions, but it leaves their full scope undefined.
- The claim that castle and grounds have “always” been protected by
  anti-Apparition charms is a later retrospective. The observed temporary
  Great Hall exception shows adjustability without resolving who historically
  held authority to create exceptions.
- Hogwarts's reputation as a safe place during the First Wizarding War cannot
  be converted into proof of perfect protection, especially in light of later
  penetrations under different conditions.
- The existence of powerful magical healing is limited by transfer to St
  Mungo's and must not be treated as evidence that the institution regarded
  injuries as acceptable.

## Unresolved open questions

- Is there direct Hogwarts-specific evidence that the school is Unplottable?
  None was located in the selected corpus.
- Were the Great Hall concentration, prefect guards and hourly checks during
  the Sirius crisis an established emergency protocol or an improvised
  response?
- What did teacher probation formally entail: duration, supervision, loss of
  duties, or merely a warning? The evidence supplies no definition.
- Was the Ministry empowered to close Hogwarts in 1943, or was closure being
  discussed as a practical consequence? The selected evidence does not settle
  the legal authority.
- What formal duties, if any, did parents and governors have after a classroom
  injury? The later hippogriff case shows notification and pressure, not a
  complete procedure.
- Which 1996 protections were newly created, which were strengthened and which
  were ordinary standing features?
- What protections, if any, were permanently changed after the Willow near
  miss, either Chamber opening, the Cabinet breach or the Battle of Hogwarts?
  No durable reform is recorded in the selected evidence.

## Unsupported assumptions excluded from the synthesis

- Hogwarts is Unplottable.
- Present-day or Harry-era measures existed at the founding or in an unchanged
  nineteenth-century form.
- Every restricted area is protected by magic rather than by obedience.
- Every serious accident produces investigation or reform.
- The school deliberately values danger itself.
- A healable injury is institutionally acceptable.
- The same office, person or body controls safety, security and staff conduct
  in every period.
- The Chamber was institutionally understood before 1992 in the form later
  discovered.
- The Dementors, wartime screening or Battle defences were normal school
  arrangements.
- The castle defended itself independently of staff knowledge, choice and
  spellwork.
- Adaptation accumulated into a codified, comprehensive safety system.

## Source-authority concerns

- `cos-ch09-006` preserves Professor Binns's account of repeated searches and
  institutional disbelief. Its later disproof makes it valuable evidence of
  institutional knowledge limits, not reliable proof that no Chamber existed.
- `cos-ch13-007` is a preserved diary memory shaped by the person whose conduct
  was at issue. It can establish the scene and leadership pressure with high
  confidence, but it does not substitute for a full official record.
- `ps-ch04-005` is later oral testimony about First-War reputation. It supports
  perception and deterrence, not the detailed operation of security.
- The pantomime account is humorous but materially specific. Tone does not
  invalidate it; it does caution against converting one memorable incident
  into a universal procedural rule.
- Later official retrospective entries can carry historical facts but must not
  silently expand Bagshot's demonstrated knowledge.
