# Draft 01 Style Lock

Status: PROVISIONAL DRAFT-01 BASELINE — TO BE CALIBRATED FURTHER WITH
CHAPTERS 2–3

This is the operational prose contract for Draft 01. It derives from the 1984
edition bible, editorial policy, and style guide; it narrows those authorities
into working rules without replacing them.

Revision 02 of Chapter 1 establishes the first full Draft-01 voice baseline.
The baseline remains provisional until Chapters 2 and 3 test it against
founder biography and the foundation narrative.

## Core baseline

- **Audience:** Harry Potter readers interested in the history and lore of
  Hogwarts. The manuscript itself remains an in-universe history for readers
  already familiar with ordinary magical life.
- **Voice:** An in-universe historian of Hogwarts and magical Britain.
- **Register:** Intelligent, polished and readable British historical prose.
- **Rule:** Scholarly in judgement, conversational in presentation.
- **Evidence:** Rigorous underneath and minimally intrusive in the prose.
- **Uncertainty:** State meaningful uncertainty once, beside the claim it
  limits, and then continue the history.
- **Synthesis:** Reasonable synthesis is allowed when evidence supports it and
  no unsupported event, person, date or institution is introduced.
- **Vocabulary:** Prefer concrete magical and historical language to academic
  abstractions.
- **Personality:** Dry intelligence is welcome; constant wit is not.
- **Exposition:** Do not explain ordinary magical concepts unnecessarily.
- **Narrative:** Show people, objects, practices and events before drawing the
  larger conclusion.
- **Structure:** Explain how things developed and why they mattered rather than
  listing lore.

## Narrative identity

- The narrator is an in-universe historian writing historical nonfiction about
  Hogwarts and magical Britain.
- Assume a magically educated readership. Explain Muggle matters or basic
  magical concepts only when the historical argument naturally requires it.
- Do not adopt Harry Potter's perspective or the voice of the novels.
- Do not report private events, thoughts, or conversations merely because a
  novel reveals them. The historian may use only knowledge plausibly available
  to the 1984 manuscript.

## Register and tone

- Use polished British historical prose: clear, intelligent, conversational,
  and slightly old-fashioned where natural.
- Prefer precise, quiet sentences to ornament. Never use pseudo-medieval
  diction, modern fandom or wiki language, melodrama, or fantasy-novel suspense.
- Keep the tone serious and intelligent. Dry humour is welcome when restrained
  and historically observant, never as an end in itself.
- Prefer roads, gardens, tools, rooms, makers and recorded acts to phrases such
  as “historical permeability”, “institutional meaning”, “social mechanism” or
  “material culture”. Use an abstract term only when it is clearer than the
  concrete history.

## Historical method

- Build the account through cause and consequence, institutional development,
  change over time, competing accounts, and the limits of the record.
- Distinguish demonstrable history from school tradition, later interpretation,
  and speculation.
- Prefer a small number of consequential claims to fact lists, trivia chains,
  encyclopedic itemisation, or repeated “Hogwarts has…” constructions.
- Use anecdotes only when evidence supports them and they illuminate an
  institutional development.
- Do not mistake a later school subject for the beginning of the practices it
  organises. Knowledge may precede its classroom name, and skilled practice may
  continue outside the school after the institution exists.

## Narrator confidence

- Match confidence to evidence. Useful distinctions include *records
  establish*, *evidence suggests*, *contemporary accounts report*, *school
  tradition holds*, *later accounts claim*, *the precise date is uncertain*,
  and *no surviving evidence establishes*.
- Vary the wording; these are epistemic categories, not recurring formulas.
- Establish a recurring limit once. Repeat it only when the boundary changes or
  when a later example might otherwise be mistaken for earlier evidence.
- A generated summary, later observation, or poetic tradition may guide inquiry
  but may not acquire greater authority in prose than its underlying source.
- Later evidence may establish persistence or supply a comparison; it does not
  by itself establish origin, continuity, or what the 1984 narrator knew.

## Paragraph behaviour

Each paragraph should normally advance one historical idea. Where the material
allows, move from claim to evidence or context and then to significance or
consequence. Qualification should appear beside the claim it limits, not in a
detached disclaimer. No exact paragraph length is mandatory.

## Section behaviour

Each major section must perform a historical task and move the chapter's
argument forward. Do not create a section merely because several evidence
records share a tag. Open with a problem, condition, or revealing fact; close
by carrying a consequence into the next stage of the history.

## Repetition and continuity

- Do not re-explain material established in an earlier chapter unless a new
  context materially changes its meaning or a brief reminder is necessary.
- Honour accepted chronology and continuity. Chapter-local plans may reserve
  facts for later chapters and must not pre-empt them for convenience.
- Preserve the reconstruction's documented omissions, including its silence on
  Hogwarts house-elf servitude, unless the editorial authority explicitly
  changes that policy.

## Canon restraint

The prose must never become more certain than the underlying evidence. Do not
fill gaps with invented lore, infer an early origin from later persistence, or
turn plausibility into history. Where evidence ends, the historian should
either qualify the synthesis or leave the question open.

## Draft-wide authority

> Once prose drafting begins, every Draft-01 chapter must treat this file as a mandatory style authority. Chapter-local instructions may refine subject matter but may not silently override the draft-wide voice.

> Substantial changes to Draft-01 style must be made here first, not independently inside one chapter.

Every later chapter-preparation, drafting, revision, and style-audit task must
load and apply this file. Calibration may refine the contract centrally, but
until a revision is accepted here, the current rules govern the whole book.
