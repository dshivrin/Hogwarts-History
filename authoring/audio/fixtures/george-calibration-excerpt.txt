The history of Hogwarts begins before Hogwarts had a history of its own. A school preserves registers, rules, appointments, disputes and recollections; the world preceding it leaves no such convenient deposit. What survives from that world is scattered among later histories, old objects, specialist books and the imperfect notice of Muggles. The fragments are unequal, but they are not silent. They describe a magical society already in possession of skilled arts, already sharing Britain with its non-magical neighbours, and already facing the difficulty of remaining visible without becoming vulnerable.

Hogwarts is older than the present millennium. Its precise year of foundation was uncertain even in the account most commonly taught at the school, and no day or season can responsibly be supplied. The secure outline is spare. Four witches and wizards built a castle far from Muggle observation, sought out young people who showed signs of magic, brought them to that place and educated them there. The founders, the gathering of pupils, the remoteness of the site and the purpose of instruction belong to history; much of the picturesque detail later attached to them belongs elsewhere.
